## What the Research Actually Says

A 2025 study on small group engagement found that online Bible study groups maintained higher consistent attendance than in-person groups — particularly among young professionals, parents with young children, and people with irregular schedules.

The reasons make sense when you think about it:

**Flexibility removes barriers.** No commute. No parking. No finding a babysitter. You log in from wherever you are.

**Consistency beats intensity.** A group that meets every week online will outlast a group that meets twice a month in person, then fizzles out.

**Written participation lowers the threshold.** In an online room, asking a question feels lower-stakes than raising your hand in a room full of strangers.

## How Our Online Bible Study Works

Here is what actually happens on a Wednesday evening:

7:25pm — People start joining the Google Meet. Cameras on or off, your choice. The WhatsApp group gets one message: "Link is in. See you at 7:30."

7:30pm — We start. Someone reads the passage out loud. Usually Mr. Sim, sometimes a regular who volunteered.

7:35pm — We read the passage together. Everyone has the same text. Usually Exodus, or Luke, or Psalms — working through books one chapter at a time.

7:45pm — Discussion opens. You can type a question in the chat. You can unmute. You can just listen.

8:30pm — Someone will ask the question that three other people were too shy to ask. That question usually generates the best discussion of the night.

9:00pm — We close. Someone says goodnight. The room empties in 30 seconds. Nobody lingers awkwardly.
