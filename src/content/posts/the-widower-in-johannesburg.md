# The Widower in Johannesburg: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Being a Man Alone After Your Wife Has Died

## The Scene

The man in Alberton has been a widower for fourteen months. His wife died in January. The family has moved on. The church congregation has moved on. He is still standing in the kitchen at seven in the evening wondering what to cook and whether anyone will notice if he just goes to bed.

**Isaiah 49:13** — *"Shout for joy, you heavens; rejoice, you earth; burst into song, you mountains! For the Lord comforts his people and will have compassion on his afflicted ones."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Widows and Widowers

1 Timothy 5:3-8 gives specific instructions about caring for widows. The passage is primarily addressed to the family — the children and grandchildren are responsible for their widowed family members. But the church community also has a responsibility. 'Religion that God our Father accepts as pure and faultless is this: to look after orphans and widows in their distress' (James 1:27). The widower is not excluded from this command.

## The Specific Loneliness of the Widower

The widower in South Africa often faces a practical loneliness. He does not cook. He does not clean. He does not know how to manage the house alone. But the deeper loneliness is not about the cooking. It is about the person who is no longer there — the morning conversation, the evening silence together, the shared calendar.

## What the Church Often Gets Wrong About the Widower

The church often focuses on the widow. This is right and good. But the widower is equally vulnerable. He is also grieving. He is also alone. He is often less likely to ask for help because men in South Africa are culturally less likely to acknowledge need. The church must learn to see the widower.

## Practical Ways to Care for the Widower

First: practical help — meals, help with the house, a regular phone call. Second: consistent presence — not a one-time visit, but an ongoing relationship. Third: invite him to things. He will not always come. Keep inviting. Fourth: ask about his wife. Let him tell the story. Fifth: give him something to do — the widower who has a role in the church community is less isolated than the widower who is just a visitor.

## A Prayer for the Widower

Father, we bring the man who is alone. We bring the man who has lost the person he built his life with. We bring the man who is pretending to be fine. We ask for the community to see him. We ask for the practical help he needs. We ask for the conversations he is missing. We ask for the health to manage alone. We ask for the hope that grief is not the last word. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
