## The Topic That Most Churches Avoid

Money is one of the topics that most South African churches avoid. The avoidance is not because the Bible is silent about money. The Bible is not silent about money. The Bible talks about money more than almost any other topic. The avoidance is because the conversation about money is the conversation that goes wrong quickly. The conversation about money is the conversation that gets hijacked by the prosperity gospel. The conversation about money is the conversation that gets hijacked by the manipulation. The conversation about money is the conversation that gets hijacked by the legalism.

This post is an attempt to have the conversation the Bible way. The conversation that is not about the prosperity gospel. The conversation that is not about the legalism. The conversation that is not about the manipulation. The conversation that is about the stewardship.

The post is for the Christians in Johannesburg — in Sandton, in Randburg, in Roodepoort, in Soweto, in the suburbs and the townships — who want to think about money in the way the Bible thinks about money. The post is for the Christians who are earning. The post is for the Christians who are unemployed. The post is for the Christians who are retired. The post is for the Christians who are in debt. The post is for the Christians who have savings.

## What the Bible Actually Says About Money

The Bible has a lot to say about money. More than two thousand verses. More than the Bible says about prayer. More than the Bible says about faith.

**Malachi 3:10** — *Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, that there may be food in my house. Test me in this, says the Lord Almighty, and see if I will not throw open the floodgates of heaven and pour out so much blessing that there will not be room enough to store it.*

**2 Corinthians 9:6-7** — *Remember this: Whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and whoever sows generously will also reap generously. Each of you should give what you have decided in your heart to give, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver.*

**1 Timothy 6:10** — *For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. Some people, eager for money, have wandered from the faith and pierced themselves with many griefs.*

**Luke 12:15** — *Then he said to them, 'Watch out! Be on your guard against all kinds of greed; life does not consist in an abundance of possessions.'*

**Matthew 6:24** — *No one can serve two masters. Either you will hate the one and love the other, or you will be devoted to the one and despise the other. You cannot serve both God and money.*

**Acts 2:44-45** — *All the believers were together and had everything in common. They sold property and possessions to give to anyone who had need.*

**Proverbs 22:7** — *The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is slave to the lender.*

**1 Timothy 5:8** — *Anyone who does not provide for their relatives, and especially for their own household, has denied the faith and is worse than an unbeliever.*

**Hebrews 13:5** — *Keep your lives free from the love of money and be content with what you have, because God has said, 'Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you.'*

What you notice when you read these verses together is that the Bible's teaching on money is not a single teaching. It is a set of teachings that hold together. The teachings are:

1. **Money is a tool, not a master.** The Bible is clear that money is dangerous when it becomes the master. The Bible is also clear that money is a tool that can be used for good.

2. **Generosity is a Christian discipline.** The Bible is clear that the Christian is called to give. The giving is not the same as the tithing. The giving is the overflow of the grateful heart.

3. **Stewardship is the primary calling.** The Bible is clear that the Christian is not the owner of the money. The Christian is the steward of the money. The money belongs to God. The Christian is the manager.

4. **Contentment is the antidote to greed.** The Bible is clear that the love of money is the root of all kinds of evil. The antidote is not the poverty. The antidote is the contentment.

5. **Provision for the family is a Christian duty.** The Bible is clear that the Christian who does not provide for the family has denied the faith.

These five teachings together are the framework. The framework is the way the Bible thinks about money.

## The Five Money Myths That Have to Die

There are five money myths that circulate in many South African churches. The myths are not the Bible. The myths are the distortions of the Bible. The myths have to be named clearly. The myths have to be left behind.

### Myth 1 — "If You Give More, You Will Get More"

This is the prosperity gospel. The prosperity gospel is the teaching that financial giving is a transaction with God. The teaching is that if you give a thousand rand, God will give you back ten thousand rand. The teaching is that the more you give, the more you get.

The prosperity gospel is not the Bible. The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 9:6 that *whoever sows generously will also reap generously*. The verse is not a transaction. The verse is a principle. The principle is that the generous person is the kind of person who is generous in every area of life. The principle is not that the generous person will be financially rewarded.

The Christian who has learned to give generously has learned to give because the giving is the right thing to do. The Christian who has learned to give generously has not learned to give because the giving is the transaction with God.

### Myth 2 — "The Tithe Is 10% and That Is the Whole Duty"

The tithe is in the Bible. The tithe is the giving of the first 10% of the income to the work of God. The Old Testament tithe is not the only giving the Bible teaches. The Old Testament tithe is the foundation. The New Testament giving is the overflow.

The New Testament teaching is that the Christian is to give *as the Lord has prospered them* (1 Corinthians 16:2). The New Testament teaching is that the Christian is to give *what they have decided in their heart to give* (2 Corinthians 9:7). The New Testament teaching is that the Christian is to give *to the poor, to the church, to the missionary, to the family, to the neighbour*.

The strict 10% tithe is a starting point. The strict 10% tithe is not the whole duty. The Christian who has given only 10% and has not given to the neighbour has not given the whole duty.

### Myth 3 — "Debt Is Always a Sin"

The Bible says in Proverbs 22:7 that *the borrower is slave to the lender*. The Bible is not anti-debt in principle. The Bible is anti-debt-as-a-way-of-life. The Bible is anti-debt-that-controls-you. The Bible is anti-debt-that-disables-you.

The mortgage on the house in Sandton is not a sin. The bond is the way most South Africans buy a house. The car loan on the car is not a sin. The car loan is the way most South Africans buy a car. The student loan on the degree is not a sin. The student loan is the way most South Africans pay for the degree.

The Bible is clear that the Christian is to *owe nothing to anyone — except to love one another* (Romans 13:8). The verse is not about the financial debt. The verse is about the obligation. The verse is about the relationship. The Christian is to love the neighbour. The Christian is to owe the neighbour the love.

The Christian who has learned to manage the debt is the Christian who has learned to live within the means. The Christian who has learned to manage the debt is the Christian who has not taken on the debt that the income cannot support.

### Myth 4 — "Poverty Is a Sign of Spiritual Failure"

This is the other side of the prosperity gospel. The other side of the prosperity gospel is the teaching that the poor person is the spiritually failing person. The other side of the prosperity gospel is the teaching that the rich person is the spiritually succeeding person.

The Bible is not silent about this. The Bible says in Luke 6:20 — *Blessed are you who are poor, for yours is the kingdom of God.* The Bible says in James 2:5 — *Has not God chosen those who are poor in the eyes of the world to be rich in faith and to inherit the kingdom he promised those who love him?*

The Bible is clear that the poor person is not the spiritually failing person. The Bible is clear that the rich person is not the spiritually succeeding person. The Bible is clear that the spiritual life is not measured by the bank account.

The Christian who has learned to honour the poor has learned to follow the Bible. The Christian who has learned to honour the poor has learned to give to the poor. The Christian who has learned to honour the poor has learned to sit at the table with the poor.

### Myth 5 — "Money Is a Private Matter"

The Bible is clear that money is not a private matter. The Bible is clear that the Christian is to give account of the money. The Bible is clear that the church is to be involved in the money. The Bible is clear that the older men are to teach the younger men about the money.

The Christian who has learned to make the money a private matter has learned to hide the sin. The Christian who has learned to make the money a community matter has learned to confess the sin. The Christian who has learned to make the money a community matter has learned to grow in the stewardship.

## A Practical Money Plan for the South African Christian

This is the practical money plan that several people in our Wednesday Bible study community have adopted. It is not a perfect plan. It is a starting plan.

### The 10% Tithe

The 10% tithe is the first thing. The 10% tithe is the giving of the first 10% of the income to the work of the church. The 10% tithe is the foundation. The 10% tithe is the practice.

The 10% tithe is not the only giving. The 10% tithe is the foundation for the other giving. The 10% tithe is the practice that makes the other giving possible.

### The 10% Savings

The 10% savings is the second thing. The 10% savings is the keeping of the second 10% of the income for the future. The 10% savings is the emergency fund. The 10% savings is the house deposit. The 10% savings is the retirement.

The 10% savings is the stewardship. The 10% savings is the practice of the future. The 10% savings is the practice that the Bible calls *the ant storing up in the summer* (Proverbs 6:6-8).

### The 10% Giving Beyond the Tithe

The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the third thing. The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the giving to the neighbour. The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the giving to the family. The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the giving to the missionary. The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the giving to the cause. The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the giving to the poor.

The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the overflow. The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the practice of the generosity. The 10% giving beyond the tithe is the practice that the Bible calls *the cheerful giver* (2 Corinthians 9:7).

### The 70% Living

The 70% living is the rest. The 70% living is the house, the food, the transport, the school, the medical aid, the clothing, the everyday life. The 70% living is the practice of the budgeting. The 70% living is the practice of the discipline.

The 70% living is the practice of the contentment. The 70% living is the practice of the wisdom. The 70% living is the practice that the Bible calls *the prudence and the good stewardship* (Luke 16:10-12).

## A Simple Monthly Budget for the South African Christian

A simple monthly budget that several of us in the Bible study have used:

**Income (100%)**
- 10% — Tithe (to the church)
- 10% — Savings (emergency fund, retirement, house deposit)
- 10% — Giving beyond the tithe (to the neighbour, the family, the missionary, the poor)
- 70% — Living (house, food, transport, school, medical aid, clothing, the rest)

The percentages are not the law. The percentages are the starting point. The percentages are the framework. The Christian who has learned to give the 10% and the 10% and the 10% has learned to live on the 70%. The Christian who has learned to live on the 70% has learned to live in the way the Bible teaches.

## The Three Practices That Have Helped the Bible Study Members

### Practice 1 — The Monthly Budget Meeting

The monthly budget meeting is the practice of the husband and the wife (or the single adult) sitting down once a month to review the money. The monthly budget meeting is the practice of the open conversation. The monthly budget meeting is the practice of the shared decision.

The monthly budget meeting is not the romantic dinner. The monthly budget meeting is the spreadsheet on the laptop. The monthly budget meeting is the conversation about the bank account. The monthly budget meeting is the conversation about the next month.

### Practice 2 — The Weekly Tithe

The weekly tithe is the practice of the giving of the tithe at the beginning of the week. The weekly tithe is the practice of the giving of the first 10% before the rest of the money is spent. The weekly tithe is the practice of the giving of the first fruits.

The weekly tithe is the practice of the discipline. The weekly tithe is the practice of the trust. The weekly tithe is the practice of the first.

### Practice 3 — The Annual Giving Review

The annual giving review is the practice of the review of the giving once a year. The annual giving review is the practice of the question. The annual giving review is the practice of the conversation with the spouse, the mentor, the pastor.

The annual giving review is not the legalism. The annual giving review is the stewardship. The annual giving review is the practice of the question that the Bible asks: *What have you done with what I have given you?*
