# Learning to Drive at Thirty-Eight: A Christian's Honest Reflection on the License That Took Too Long

## The Scene

The learner driver in Randburg is forty-one years old. The instructor is twenty-three. The Christian sits in the driver's seat and thinks: 'I should have done this twenty years ago.'

**Galatians 5:22-23** — *"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Bible and New Beginnings

The Bible is full of people who began things late. Abraham was seventy-five. Moses was eighty. The story of the faith is not the story of the early starter. It is the story of the person who begins when the circumstances finally make it possible.

## The Shame of the Late Learner

The adult learner in South Africa carries a particular shame. The shame is real. The Bible invites the adult learner to bring the shame to God and receive dignity instead.

## What the Bible Says About Patience in Learning

Galatians 5:22 lists the fruit of the Spirit. The adult learner needs all of these. The Bible is addressed to the struggling learner.

## Practical Steps for the Adult Learner

Get a patient instructor. Practice in a quiet area first. Celebrate small milestones. Do not compare yourself to the eighteen-year-old. Use the commute time — listen to the audio Bible.

## A Prayer for the Adult Learner

Father, we bring the shame of the late start. Give us patience with ourselves. Give us the courage to keep trying. Give us the license — eventually. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
