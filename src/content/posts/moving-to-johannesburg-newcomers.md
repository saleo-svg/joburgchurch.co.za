## The City That Takes a Year to Learn

There is a saying we use in our Wednesday Bible study about Johannesburg. It is something like this: *It takes a year to learn the city, three years to feel at home in the city, and five years to call the city your own.*

Most of the people in our Bible study community moved to Johannesburg at some point in their lives. Some of us moved for work. Some of us moved for a spouse. Some of us moved for a school. Some of us moved for a fresh start. Some of us moved because we were following a call we did not fully understand.

Almost all of us made the same mistakes in the first year. We underestimated the traffic. We underestimated the security. We underestimated the social distance. We underestimated how hard it is to find a church, a doctor, a plumber, a community, a friend.

This post is the guide we wish someone had given us when we first moved to Johannesburg. It is honest about the hard parts. It is practical about the everyday parts. It is rooted in the Bible.

## What the Bible Says About the New Place

The Bible is full of people who moved to new places. The Bible has a lot to say about the new place.

**Genesis 12:1** — *The Lord had said to Abram, 'Go from your country, your people and your father's household to the land I will show you.'*

**Ruth 1:16** — *But Ruth replied, 'Don't urge me to leave you or to turn back from you. Where you go I will go, and where you stay I will stay. Your people will be my people and your God my God.'*

**Jeremiah 29:4-7** — *This is what the Lord Almighty, the God of Israel, says to all those I carried into exile from Jerusalem to Babylon: Build houses and settle down; plant gardens and eat what they produce. Marry and have sons and daughters; find wives for your sons and give your daughters in marriage, so that they too may have sons and daughters. Increase in number there; do not decrease. Also, seek the peace and prosperity of the city to which I have carried you into exile. Pray to the Lord for it, because if it prospers, you too will prosper.*

What you notice when you read these verses together is that the new place is not presented as something to endure. The new place is presented as something to settle into. The new place is the place where God has put you. The new place is the place where you are to build, plant, marry, increase, seek the peace of the city, pray for the city.

This is the Joburg kind of settling. The Joburg kind of settling is not the tourist kind of settling. The tourist kind of settling is the kind where you stay for a week and you see the highlights. The Joburg kind of settling is the kind where you stay for a year and you learn the grocery store, the doctor, the mechanic, the church, the friend, the neighbourhood.

## The First 90 Days: A Practical Timeline

The first 90 days in Johannesburg are the hardest. They are also the most important. What you do in the first 90 days often determines what the next five years will look like.

### Days 1 to 30 — Survive and Settle

The first 30 days are about survival. You are unpacked. You are unsettled. You are tired. You are wondering if you made the right decision. You are wondering if Johannesburg is the right place for you.

Five things to do in the first 30 days:

1. **Get connected to water and electricity.** In Johannesburg, this is not automatic. You need to register with City Power for electricity. You need to register with Johannesburg Water for the water account. You need to register with the municipal rates account. Do this in the first week. Do not let it become the third-month problem.

2. **Set up your mobile and data.** South Africa is a mobile-first country. Your phone is your bank, your map, your taxi, your doctor's appointment, your grocery delivery. Get a South African SIM card. Get a data plan that is generous enough for the way you actually use the phone. Vodacom, MTN, Cell C, and Telkom Mobile are the main options. Each has different coverage in different suburbs. Ask the people in your neighbourhood which one works.

3. **Find a grocery store and a pharmacy.** In Johannesburg, the grocery store is the centre of the daily life. Find the closest Woolworths, Pick n Pay, Checkers, or Spar. Find the closest Clicks or Dis-Chem. The first time you successfully find everything you need at the closest store is the moment you start to feel settled.

4. **Find a doctor and a dentist.** Do not wait until you are sick. Ask your colleagues. Ask the church community. Ask the WhatsApp group. The doctor in Johannesburg is gold. Once you find one, hold onto them.

5. **Drive the city at different times of day.** The Joburg you see at 6am is not the Joburg you see at 8am. The Joburg you see on Saturday is not the Joburg you see on Tuesday. Drive the city. Learn the rhythm. Learn which roads to take at which times. Learn which roads not to take at any time.

### Days 31 to 60 — Connect and Understand

The second 30 days are about understanding. You are starting to settle. You are starting to know the grocery store, the pharmacy, the doctor. You are starting to know the rhythm of the city.

Five things to do in the second 30 days:

1. **Find a church.** This is the most important single thing on this list. Johannesburg has hundreds of churches. Some are large. Some are small. Some are liturgical. Some are charismatic. Some are Bible study only. Some are Sunday service only. The right church for you is the one where you can hear the Bible preached, where you can join a community that will know your name in a year, and where you can serve.

2. **Find a Bible study.** Beyond the Sunday service, the Bible study is where the Christian life actually happens. The Bible study is the smaller group. The Bible study is the place where the questions can be asked. The Bible study is the place where the prayer requests can be shared. The Bible study is the place where you will be known.

3. **Find a community beyond the church.** The gym. The hiking club. The book club. The cycling group. The parkrun. The South African community is made through shared activity. The job does not give you the community. The church does not give you the community. The shared activity gives you the community.

4. **Learn the load-shedding rhythm.** Eskom publishes the load-shedding schedule, but the schedule is not always followed. You will need to download the EskomSePush app or the Loadshedding Alert app. You will need to plan your day around the schedule. You will need to learn which shops have backup power. You will need to learn the closest restaurant or coffee shop that stays open during the load-shedding.

5. **Drive to the countryside.** Johannesburg is one hour away from some of the most beautiful countryside in South Africa. The Magaliesberg. The Cradle of Humankind. The Hartbeespoort Dam. The Pilanesberg. The first weekend you spend outside the city is the weekend you start to feel a different relationship with the city.

### Days 61 to 90 — Put Down Roots

The third 30 days are about putting down roots. You are starting to feel at home. You are starting to know the rhythm. You are starting to know the people.

Five things to do in the third 30 days:

1. **Get a South African bank account.** The South African bank account is the gateway to the South African life. The debit orders. The salary. The bond payment. The savings. The investment. You cannot live in Johannesburg without a bank account. The big four are FNB, Standard Bank, Absa, and Nedbank. Capitec is the newcomer for the simpler needs.

2. **Get a South African driver's license.** If you have a foreign license, you can convert it in some cases. If you do not, you will need to take the South African learner's test and the South African driver's test. The driving schools in Johannesburg are full of newcomers. The driving school is also a place to meet other newcomers.

3. **Open a medical aid or a hospital plan.** The South African public healthcare system is overstretched. The private healthcare system is excellent. The medical aid is how you access the private healthcare. Discovery, Bonitas, Momentum, Medihelp are the main options. The hospital plan is the cheaper version of the medical aid.

4. **Find a long-term rental or consider buying.** The first three months in a rental is the survival rental. You are not making a long-term decision. The third-month rent is the time to start looking at the long-term rental. The long-term rental is the rental you will stay in for two years. The long-term rental is the rental where you will start to put up the photos.

5. **Start a small daily Joburg habit.** The small daily habit is what makes the city yours. The morning coffee at the same coffee shop. The Saturday morning walk in the same park. The Sunday afternoon visit to the same market. The early-evening run around the same block. The small daily habit is what the city becomes about.

## The Hard Truths About Settling in Johannesburg

It would be dishonest to write a survival guide without naming the hard parts.

### The Hard Truth 1 — Joburg Is Not Safe in the Way Other Cities Are Safe

The security situation in Johannesburg is real. The hijackings. The burglaries. The smash-and-grabs. The car-jackings. The home invasions. These are not outliers. These are the daily reality of the city.

You will need to learn the security habits. The security habits are not paranoia. The security habits are common sense. Lock the doors when you drive. Lock the gates when you walk. Do not leave the laptop in the car. Do not leave the phone on the seat. Be aware of your surroundings. Park in the secured parking. Do not flash the jewellery. Do not walk alone at night.

This is not the Fun Part of the city. But the Fun Part is the part that does not work without the security habits.

### The Hard Truth 2 — Joburg Is a Long Way from Everyone

If you are moving to Johannesburg from Cape Town, you are an hour and a half flight from Cape Town. If you are moving from Durban, you are an hour flight. If you are moving from East London, you are an hour and a half flight. If you are moving from abroad, you are ten hours from London, eleven from New York, eight from Dubai.

The distance is the part of the city that no one warns you about. The distance is the part that catches up with you in the second year, when the newness has worn off and the family is still far away.

The Bible is not silent about the distance. The Bible says in Psalm 137:4 — *How can we sing the songs of the Lord while in a foreign land?* The psalm is named after the Babylonian exile. The psalm is the prayer of the people who were far from the place they called home. The prayer is honest. The prayer is named. The prayer is given to God.

### The Hard Truth 3 — Joburg Will Test Your Faith

Joburg will test your faith. The drive across the city at 6pm on a Friday will test your faith. The home invasion at 2am will test your faith. The funeral in the family will test your faith. The job loss will test your faith. The visa difficulty will test your faith. The marriage that is hard will test your faith.

The testing is not a sign that you have done something wrong. The testing is the way the city forms you. The testing is the way the city shapes you into the Christian you were meant to be.

The Bible says in 1 Peter 1:6-7 — *In all this you greatly rejoice, though now for a little while you may have had to suffer grief in all kinds of trials. These have come so that the proven genuineness of your faith — of greater worth than gold, which perishes even though refined by fire — may result in praise, glory and honour when Jesus Christ is revealed.*

## A Prayer for the New Joburg Arrival

If you have arrived in Johannesburg this year — or if you are helping someone who has — here is a prayer.

> *God of the new place,*
>
> *We thank you for the city. We thank you for the work that brought us here. We thank you for the people we have met. We thank you for the jacarandas in October. We thank you for the thunder in November. We thank you for the clear winter days in June.*
>
> *We ask you to help us to settle. To find the grocery store. To find the doctor. To find the church. To find the Bible study. To find the friend. To find the rhythm.*
>
> *We ask you to help us in the hard parts. The security. The distance. The loneliness. The testing.*
>
> *Make us people of the city. Make us people who love the city. Make us people who pray for the city. Make us people who seek the peace of the city.*
>
> *In the name of the one who had nowhere to lay his head, and who made the whole world his home.*
> *Amen.*
