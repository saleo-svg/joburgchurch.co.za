# When You Moved Here Last Year: A South African Christian's Guide to the Expat Who Is Still Trying to Understand the Country

## The Scene

The man in Sandton has been in South Africa for seven months. He moved from the United States. The job is good. The home is good. The country is not what he expected. He has not made a South African friend yet.

**2 Corinthians 12:9** — *"My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About the Foreign Land

Psalm 137:1-4 says: 'By the rivers of Babylon we sat and wept when we remembered Zion.' The Psalmist was a foreigner in a strange land. He grieved what he had lost. He could not sing the songs of the Lord in a foreign land. The new arrival in South Africa is invited to grieve what they have left, and to learn new songs.

## The Specific Challenges of the New South African

The new arrival faces: load-shedding (no warning), security culture (electric fence as standard), the price of medical aid, the language of the workplace, the unspoken racial history. These are not small things. They are significant adjustments that take years.

## Building a Life in the New Country

After ten years of Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore, we have learned that the new arrival in South Africa who builds a life well does several things. They learn the language. They find a church. They make a South African friend. They engage with the history. They give themselves permission to grieve the country they left.

## A Prayer for the New South African

Father, we bring the new arrival. We bring the one who is still grieving the country they left. We bring the one who is trying to understand the country they are in. We ask for the patience to learn. We ask for the friends to be made. We ask for the integration that is slow but real. Use the exile as a place of growth. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
