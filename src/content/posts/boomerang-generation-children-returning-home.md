# When the Adult Child Returns Home: A South African Christian Family's Guide to the Boomerang Generation

## The Scene

The parents in Fourways had just started to enjoy the empty nest. The holidays were their own. The Sunday lunch was quiet. The mortgage was almost paid. Then the phone call came. The child is coming home. The bedroom needs to be cleared again. The parents look at each other across the kitchen and wonder what happened.

**Proverbs 22:6** — *"Start children off on the way they should go, and even when they are old they will not turn from it."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Biblical Picture of the Returning Child

The parable of the prodigal son is the Bible's most famous story about the returning family member. Luke 15:20 says: 'So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him.' The father's response was not: 'Sit down. We need to talk about what happened.' The father's first response was to embrace.

The parent whose adult child has returned home is invited to be the father in the parable — full of compassion, not full of resentment.

## Setting Boundaries Without Closing the Door

Compassion does not mean no boundaries. The parent who welcomes the adult child home is also invited to set clear expectations about contribution to the household, timeline for the return, and respectful behaviour. These expectations are not unloving. They are loving. The adult child who is given no structure often becomes more, not less, dependent.

## The Parent's Own Grief

The parent whose adult child has returned home is often grieving something that no one acknowledges. The grief of the parent's own future — the retirement that was imagined differently, the couple-time that has been interrupted again. This grief is real and legitimate. It is not unspiritual to grieve the change. The Bible invites honest prayer about it.

## Practical Steps for the Re-Formed Household

First: have an honest conversation about timeline, contribution, and expectations — within the first month. Second: treat the adult child as an adult — they are not a teenager again. Third: maintain the couple relationship — do not let the returning child become the centre of the marriage. Fourth: pray for the child's next step — the goal is not permanent re-dependence, it is the next launch. Fifth: maintain your own social life and plans.

## A Prayer for the Family with the Returning Child

Father, we bring the family that has been re-formed. We bring the parent who is grieving the empty nest that is no longer empty. We bring the adult child who has come home with shame. We ask for the compassion of the father in the parable. We ask for the wisdom to set boundaries with love. We ask for the patience to see the long road. We ask for the faith to believe that this season, however difficult, is not the end of the story. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
