# The Neighbourhood Watch and the Christian: A South African's Guide to Community Safety, Vigilantism, and Doing Good Without Becoming What You Fear

## The Scene

The man in Linksfield joined the neighbourhood watch after the third burglary in the street. He is armed. He patrols on Thursday evenings. He has confronted people he suspects. He is not sure anymore whether he is protecting his neighbours or becoming the kind of person who uses power without accountability.

**Romans 13:1** — *"Let everyone be subject to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which God has established."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Justice and the State

Romans 13:1-4 says: 'Everyone must submit themselves to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which God has established. The authorities that exist have been established by God. Consequently, whoever rebels against the authority is rebelling against what God has instituted.' This passage has been used to justify both authoritarianism and vigilantism. It has been used to oppose both state violence and community defence. The correct reading is more nuanced: the state has the legitimate monopoly on the use of force. Community defence groups operate in a morally complex space.

## The Danger of the Armed Neighbourhood Watch

Proverbs 16:32 says: 'Better a patient person than a warrior, one with self-control than one who takes a city.' The armed neighbourhood watch member who confronts suspects is one bad decision away from becoming a killer. This does not mean neighbourhood watches are wrong. It means the people in them need extraordinary self-control, training, and accountability.

## What Responsible Neighbourhood Defence Looks Like

Responsible community defence is: communication (WhatsApp groups, street lighting, CCTV sharing), observation and reporting (not confrontation), cooperation with SAPS, and clear boundaries about what the group will and will not do. The neighbourhood watch that tries to be a police force is a neighbourhood watch that has crossed a line.

## A Prayer for Community Safety

Father, we bring the fear of crime that keeps us awake at night. We bring the neighbourhood watch that tries to do what the state cannot. We ask for wisdom about the use of power. We ask for self-control when the adrenaline is high. We ask for justice that does not become revenge. We ask for community safety that does not become vigilantism. We ask for the police to do their job. We ask for the community to be protected without becoming dangerous. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
