## The Twenty-Two Kilometres That Are Also a Thousand

There is a drive in Johannesburg that every Christian in the city has either made or avoided making. You get on the M1 North or the M1 South. You drive for between thirty and ninety minutes, depending on the time of day and the load-shedding stage. You drive past the universities, past the hospitals, past the malls, past the taxi ranks, past the same adverts on the same billboards in both directions. You get off at the other end. You arrive in a place that is also Johannesburg, but where the language on the street is different, the prices in the shops are different, the houses are different, the churches on the corners are different, the way people say good morning is different.

Sandton to Soweto is twenty-two kilometres. It is also a thousand kilometres. It is also a different country. Every Christian in Johannesburg knows this in some part of themselves, even if they have never had to put it into words.

This post is for the Christian who lives in the gap. It is for the Christian who has a meeting in Sandton in the morning and a braai in Soweto in the evening. It is for the Christian who has been asked the question by a child, by a colleague, by a quiet moment in the car — *how can one city be two cities?* — and who has begun to wonder what the Bible has to say about it.

The Bible has a great deal to say about it. More than most Christians realise.

## What the Bible Actually Says About Inequality

The Bible is not silent about the rich and the poor. The Bible is unusually clear about it.

**Proverbs 14:31** — *Whoever oppresses the poor shows contempt for their Maker, but whoever is kind to the needy honours God.*

**Proverbs 22:2** — *The rich and the poor have this in common: The Lord is the Maker of them all.*

**Proverbs 29:7** — *The righteous care about justice for the poor, but the wicked have no such concern.*

**Amos 5:24** — *Let justice roll on like a river, righteousness like a never-failing stream!*

**Micah 6:8** — *He has shown you, O mortal, what is good. And what does the Lord require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God.*

**Luke 6:20-21** — *Blessed are you who are poor, for yours is the kingdom of God. Blessed are you who hunger now, for you will be satisfied. Blessed are you who weep now, for you will laugh.*

**Luke 6:24** — *But woe to you who are rich, for you have already received your comfort. Woe to you who are well fed now, for you will go hungry. Woe to you who laugh now, for you will mourn and weep.*

**James 2:1-7** — *My brothers and sisters, believers in our glorious Lord Jesus Christ must not show favouritism. Suppose a man comes into your meeting wearing a gold ring and fine clothes, and a poor man in filthy old clothes also comes in. If you show special attention to the man wearing fine clothes and say, 'Here's a good seat for you,' but say to the poor man, 'You stand there' or 'Sit on the floor by my feet,' have you not discriminated among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts? Listen, my dear brothers and sisters: Has not God chosen those who are poor in the eyes of the world to be rich in faith and to inherit the kingdom he promised those who love him? But you have dishonoured the poor. Is it not the rich who are exploiting you? Are they not the ones who are dragging you into court?*

What you notice is that the Bible does not treat inequality as a private matter. The Bible does not treat inequality as a political matter that Christians should keep their opinions about to themselves. The Bible treats inequality as a spiritual matter — a matter that concerns the heart of God, the discipline of the people of God, and the witness of the church in the world.

This is important for the Christian in Johannesburg to hear. It is easy to assume that the Bible is only about personal salvation. It is easy to assume that the Bible is only about the next life. The Bible is not only about those things. The Bible has a great deal to say about how the people of God live in a city where the rich and the poor are twenty-two kilometres apart.

## What the Bible Says About the City That Holds Both

The second thing the Bible has a great deal to say about is the city. The Bible is not a rural book. The Bible is a book about cities. The first murder happens in a family outside a garden. The first city is built by the murderer. The rest of the Bible is largely about cities — Babel, Sodom, Gomorrah, Nineveh, Jerusalem, Antioch, Corinth, Rome, and the new Jerusalem at the end.

**Jeremiah 29:7** — *Also, seek the peace and prosperity of the city to which I have carried you into exile. Pray to the Lord for it, because if it prospers, you too will prosper.*

**Psalm 122:6-8** — *Pray for the peace of Jerusalem: 'May those who love you be secure. May there be peace within your walls and security within your citadels.' For the sake of my family and friends, I will say, 'Peace be within you.'*

**Matthew 5:14** — *You are the light of the world. A town built on a hill cannot be hidden.*

**Hebrews 11:10** — *For he was looking forward to the city with foundations, whose architect and builder is God.*

**Revelation 21:2** — *I saw the Holy City, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride beautifully dressed for her husband.*

The Christian in Johannesburg is part of a long tradition of Christians who have taken their cities seriously. The Christian in Johannesburg is part of a long tradition of Christians who have prayed for the peace of their city, who have been the light of their city, and who have looked forward to the city whose architect and builder is God.

The Christian in Johannesburg does not have to choose between Sandton and Soweto. The Christian in Johannesburg is called to be the light of both. The Christian in Johannesburg is called to pray for the peace of both. The Christian in Johannesburg is called to belong to the city whose architect and builder is God — the city that holds them both.

## Why This Is Hard for the Christian in Johannesburg

This is hard for the Christian in Johannesburg for three reasons.

### 1. The Gap Is Not Just Economic

The gap between Sandton and Soweto is not just a gap in income. It is a gap in history, in language, in education, in access to justice, in access to healthcare, in access to dignity. The Christian in Johannesburg who has tried to close the gap by writing a cheque has usually found that the cheque is welcome but is not the whole story. The Christian in Johannesburg who has tried to close the gap by working in a school or a clinic in Soweto has usually found that the work is welcome but is not the whole story either.

The Bible is honest about this. The Bible knows that the gap between the rich and the poor is a gap that runs deeper than money. The Bible knows that the gap is also a gap in hearts, in habits, in histories. The Bible does not promise that the gap will be closed in one generation. The Bible promises that the Christian is called to work at it patiently, faithfully, with hope.

### 2. The Christian Has Often Been on the Wrong Side of the Gap

The Christian in Johannesburg has often been on the wrong side of the gap. Not always. Not only. But often. The history of the church in South Africa is a complicated history. The Bible does not pretend otherwise.

**Luke 11:46** — *Jesus said, 'Woe to you experts in the law, because you load people down with burdens they can hardly carry, and you yourselves will not lift one finger to help them.'*

**Matthew 23:4** — *They tie up heavy, cumbersome loads and put them on other people's shoulders, but they themselves are not willing to lift a finger to move them.*

The Christian in Johannesburg who is reading this honestly may have to confess that the church in Johannesburg has sometimes been on the wrong side of the gap. The Christian in Johannesburg who can confess this honestly is in a position to do something different. The Christian in Johannesburg who cannot confess this honestly is in a harder position.

### 3. The Christian Often Does Not Know Where to Begin

The Christian in Johannesburg who has begun to see the gap honestly often does not know where to begin. The gap is so large. The problems are so many. The needs are so deep. The Christian who has tried to give money, time, expertise, or relationships has often ended up feeling that none of it is enough.

The Bible is honest about this too. The parable of the sheep and the goats in Matthew 25 ends with the King saying, *'Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me.'* The parable is not a programme. It is a description. The Christian in Johannesburg is not called to fix the gap. The Christian in Johannesburg is called to notice the least of these in the gap, and to do for them what can be done.

## What the Bible Says About Reconciliation

The third thing the Bible has a great deal to say about is reconciliation. The Bible does not treat reconciliation as a programme. The Bible treats reconciliation as the work of God in the world.

**2 Corinthians 5:18-19** — *All this is from God, who reconciled us to himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation: that God was reconciling the world to himself in Christ, not counting people's sins against them. And he has committed to us the message of reconciliation.*

**Ephesians 2:14** — *For he himself is our peace, who has made the two groups one and has destroyed the barrier, the dividing wall of hostility.*

**Colossians 1:20** — *and through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether things on earth or things in heaven, by making peace through his blood, which was poured out on a cross.*

**Romans 12:18** — *If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone.*

The Christian in Johannesburg is part of a long tradition of Christians who have been called to the ministry of reconciliation. The Christian in Johannesburg who lives between Sandton and Soweto is in a unique position to do this work. The Christian who has a foot in both worlds is not the Christian who has solved the gap. The Christian who has a foot in both worlds is the Christian who has begun to understand that the gap is real, that the gap is not the last word, and that the gospel is larger than the gap.

## What the Sandton Bible Study Has Tried to Do

In the last few years, the Wednesday evening Bible study has tried to take the gap seriously. Not always well. Sometimes badly. But we have tried. Here is what we have done, for what it is worth.

### We Have Visited

We have organised four visits in the last two years to ministry partners in Soweto — a feeding scheme, a youth programme, a Bible study in Diepkloof, a women's prayer group in Meadowlands. We have brought small donations. We have brought more presence than donations. We have listened. We have learned. The Bible study has not been the same since.

### We Have Read the Prophets

For one Wednesday a month, we read the prophets. Amos. Micah. Isaiah. We let the prophets say what the prophets say. We have discovered that the prophets do not let the comfortable Christian stay comfortable. We have discovered that the prophets do not let the uncomfortable Christian stay despairing. The prophets do both at the same time.

### We Have Mixed the Room

We have tried, slowly and imperfectly, to make sure that the Wednesday evening is a mixed room. Not in a forced way. Not in a quota way. In a way that makes it possible for a Christian from Parkmore and a Christian from Soweto to read the same passage and discover that they have more in common than they thought. The Bible makes this easier than the politics makes it.

### We Have Confessed Our Complicity

We have done a few Wednesdays where we read Luke 11 and Matthew 23 honestly. We have named the ways the church in Johannesburg has been on the wrong side of the gap. We have not done this performatively. We have done it as a practice. The Christian who confesses the wrong side of the gap is the Christian who has begun to be on the right side.

## Five Practices for the Christian Who Lives Between the Two

If you are a Christian in Johannesburg who lives between Sandton and Soweto — and many of us do, even if we do not always name it — here are five practices you can try.

### 1. Drive the M1 Both Ways

Drive the M1 both ways in the same week. Drive north to a meeting in Sandton. Drive south to visit a ministry partner. Drive the same road. Notice what you notice. The Bible says the Lord is the maker of the rich and the poor. Drive the road and ask God to show you both sides as his.

### 2. Read Amos for a Month

Read Amos for a month. One chapter a day for nine days, then repeat. Let the prophet who said *'let justice roll on like a river'* disturb you. Let the prophet who said the Lord hates the worship of the unjust disturb your Sunday. Let the prophet disturb your Wednesday.

### 3. Find a Partner Across the Gap

Find one ministry partner across the gap. Not a charity to support from a distance. A partner who knows your name and who will let you know theirs. Visit them. Listen to them. Learn from them. Support them. The Christian who has one partner across the gap has begun the ministry of reconciliation.

### 4. Confess the Wrong Side of the Gap

Confess, honestly and privately, the ways the church in Johannesburg has been on the wrong side of the gap. The Christian who can confess honestly is in a position to act differently. The Christian who cannot confess honestly is in a harder position.

### 5. Bring the Two to the Same Table

Find one place in your life — a meal, a Bible study, a project, a friendship — where the two worlds meet. Do not do it performatively. Do not do it for the photograph. Do it for the gospel. The Christian who has brought the two to the same table has begun to live out the prayer that the new Jerusalem is one city, not two.
