# The Christian Employer and the Domestic Worker: A South African's Honest Guide to the Most Intimate Employment Relationship

## The Scene

The woman in Sandton employs a domestic worker three days a week. They have known each other for eight years. The domestic worker's children go to the same school as the employer's children. They are both Christians. They also have a labour dispute about pay that neither of them has raised directly.

**James 4:6** — *"God opposes the proud but shows favour to the humble."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What James 5 Says to Employers

James 5:1-6 is one of the most direct passages in the Bible about economic injustice. It says: 'Now listen, you rich people, weep and wail because of the misery that is coming on you. Your wealth has rotted and moths have eaten your clothes. The wages you failed to pay the workers who mowed your fields are crying out against you.' This passage was addressed to wealthy landowners who exploited their workers. In Johannesburg in 2026, it applies to the employer who underpays the domestic worker.

## The Fair Wage Question in South Africa

South Africa's minimum wage for domestic workers increases every year. The Christian employer is invited to go beyond the minimum wage — not as charity, but as justice. Colossians 4:1 says: 'Masters, provide your slaves with what is right and fair.' The domestic worker relationship in Johannesburg is not identical to the master-slave relationship in the ancient world. But the principle is the same: what is right and fair, not what is minimum and exploitative.

## The Witness of the Employer-Worker Relationship

After ten years of Bible study in Parkmore, we have learned that the most powerful witness the Christian employer can give in Sandton is not the church meeting they attend. It is the way they treat the domestic worker. The respect, the fairness, the recognition that both are made in the image of God — this is the gospel that the domestic worker takes home to her family.

## Practical Steps for Both Sides

For employers: pay above the minimum wage if you can. Provide UIF. Give the agreed-upon benefits. Treat the domestic worker with the same respect you would give a professional colleague. For domestic workers: know your rights under South African law. Join a domestic workers' union. Do not accept verbal-only contracts. For both: have the difficult conversations directly and respectfully.

## A Prayer for the Employment Relationship

Father, we bring the employment relationship that is too close to be purely professional and too unequal to be purely equal. We bring the employer who has not always been fair. We bring the domestic worker who has not always been heard. We ask for the courage to have the difficult conversation. We ask for the generosity to go beyond the minimum. We ask for the respect that both are owed. We ask that this relationship would be a witness to your kingdom in Johannesburg. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
