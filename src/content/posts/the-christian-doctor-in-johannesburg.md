# The Christian Doctor in the Johannesburg Hospital: A South African's Guide to Faith, Medicine, and the God Who Works Through Both

## The Scene

The doctor in Charlotte Maxeke Hospital has been on call for thirty-six hours. A child died on her watch. She did everything the protocol required. The parents were kind to her. She is not sure what she believes right now.

**James 5:14-15** — *"Is anyone among you sick? Let them call the elders of the church to pray over them and anoint them with oil in the name of the Lord. The prayer offered in faith will make the sick person well."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Bible and Medicine

Luke 10:34 says: 'He went to him and bandaged his wounds, pouring on oil and wine.' The Good Samaritan used medical intervention — cleaning and bandaging — as an act of compassion. The Bible does not distinguish between spiritual healing and medical healing. Both are gifts from God. The doctor who treats patients is doing holy work.

## What to Say and Not Say to Patients

The Christian doctor faces the question: when a patient is dying, what do I say? The answer from ten years of Bible study in Parkmore is: say what is true, with kindness. 'I am doing everything I can' is true. 'God is with you' is true. 'You will be fine' may not be true. 'It is God's will' can sound like blame. Be careful with the theological explanations for suffering — patients in pain need presence, not theology.

## Prayer in the Hospital

The question of prayer in the hospital context is sensitive. Some patients want it. Some do not. Some are from a different religion. The wise doctor asks: 'Would you like me to say a prayer?' rather than assuming. The prayer itself should be simple: 'May you find comfort, may you be free from pain, may you know peace.'

## Caring for Yourself as a Healer

Ezekiel 34:12 says: 'He tends his flock like a shepherd: He gathers the lambs in his arms.' The Christian doctor who does not tend to themselves will eventually collapse. The medical profession has high rates of depression and suicide. The Christian doctor is invited to receive the care they offer to others.

## A Prayer for the Christian Doctor

Father, we bring the doctor who is tired. We bring the doctor who has lost a patient and is not sure what they believe. We bring the doctor who works in a system that does not give them enough. We ask for the skill to heal. We ask for the humility to know the limits of medicine. We ask for the compassion to stay present with the suffering. We ask for the rest that prevents burnout. We ask for the faith that holds when the outcome is not what was hoped for. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
