# In Traffic on the N1: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Road Rage, Patience, and the Theology of the Morning Commute

## The Scene

The man in Midrand was late for the meeting. The N1 was stopped. A taxi had cut across three lanes without indicating. He leaned on the hooter. He said words he would not repeat in church. He was not a bad person. He was just a person in Johannesburg traffic.

**Galatians 5:22-23** — *"But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Anger

Proverbs 15:1 says: 'A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.' This is written in the context of daily life, not specifically about traffic. But it applies perfectly to the driver who cuts you off. The response to the cutting-off is not to cut them off back, or to gesture, or to shout. The response — the faithful response — is to let it go.

## The Particular Temptation of Road Rage

Road rage is a specific sin because it combines the anonymity of the car (no one knows who you are), the stress of the delay, and the sense of personal violation (they disrespected me). These three factors together can produce behaviour in the driver that the same person would never display in any other context. This is why road rage is so revealing about character.

## Practical Steps for the Angry Driver

First: leave earlier. The single biggest cause of road rage is being late. Second: use the commute as prayer time. The commute is unavoidable. It might as well be used for something productive. Third: in the moment of anger, take three deep breaths before reacting. Fourth: remember that the other driver is a person. They may be rushing to a hospital. They may be having the worst day of their life.

## A Prayer for the Morning Commute

Father, we bring the traffic that is unavoidable. We bring the anger that rises in the small space of the car. We ask for patience that does not come from resignation but from grace. We ask for the three deep breaths before the word is spoken. We ask for the commute to be a place of prayer rather than rage. We ask for the other drivers to arrive safely, whoever they are. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
