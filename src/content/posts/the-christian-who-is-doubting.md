# When the Faith Is Not Working: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Doubt, Questions, and the God Who Is Not Afraid of the Honest Heart

## The Scene

The man in Roodepoort has been a Christian his whole life. He is now fifty-two. He has started having doubts. He is afraid to tell anyone. He is not sure if the doubts mean he is losing his faith or finally arriving at it.

**Hebrews 11:1** — *"Now faith is confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Bible and Doubt

The Bible is full of doubt. Thomas doubted the resurrection until he saw the wounds (John 20:24-29). Job doubted the justice of God (Job 30:20). The Psalmist doubted God's presence (Psalm 42:9). Doubt is not the opposite of faith. Doubt is the honest acknowledgement of the questions.

## The Different Kinds of Doubt

Doubt of the head is intellectual. The Christian who cannot reconcile the Bible with the age of the universe. Doubt of the heart is emotional. The Christian who cannot feel God's presence after a tragedy. Doubt of the will is volitional. The Christian who chooses to doubt as a way of avoiding commitment. Each kind requires a different response.

## What to Do with the Doubt

First: do not pretend the doubt is not there. Second: bring the doubt to God — Job did. The Psalms do. The Bible is not afraid of the honest question. Third: find a community of safe doubters — people who will not judge you for the question. Fourth: read the books that address the doubt. Fifth: be patient. Doubt is not a permanent state. It is a season.

## A Prayer for the Doubting Christian

Father, we bring the doubt that is not unfaithfulness. We bring the questions that are not rejection. We ask for the honesty that is welcomed. We ask for the community that does not judge. We ask for the journey that is slow but real. We ask for the faith that is not naive but is yours. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
