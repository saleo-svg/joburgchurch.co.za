## The Sandwich Generation at 29

If you are a young professional in Johannesburg between the ages of 25 and 35, you probably know the feeling. You are working a job that requires 50 to 60 hours a week, sometimes more. You are paying rent in Sandton, Rosebank, or Parkhurst that takes a third of your salary. You are sending money home to your family in Limpopo, the Eastern Cape, KZN, or Zimbabwe. You are dating, or you are not dating, but either way you are wondering what the future looks like. You are on social media, which means you are constantly comparing your behind-the-scenes to everyone else's highlight reel.

You are also, statistically, less likely to be part of a church community than your parents were at your age.

This is not a South African trend alone. It is happening across the global church. The under-35 generation in particular has stepped back from traditional church in significant numbers. Some have left the faith entirely. Some have kept the faith but stepped away from organised religion. Some are looking for something, and the church they grew up in does not feel like the something they are looking for.

This post is for that person. It is also for the Bible study community in Johannesburg — in Sandton, in Rosebank, in Parkhurst, in Melrose, in Fourways — that is trying to reach them.

## What Young Professionals in Johannesburg Actually Need

Before we talk about the Bible study format, we need to talk about the need. Young professionals in Johannesburg — and especially the UseCan age group we work with — have a particular set of pressures that a Bible study needs to understand before it can be useful.

### Career Pressure

The Johannesburg career market is brutal. The big four banks, the consulting firms, the law firms, the tech companies, the mining houses — they pay well, and they take everything. The expectation is that you will be available after hours, on weekends, on Christmas. The bonus structure rewards people who never say no. The performance review rewards people who look like they are thriving.

A Bible study that does not understand this pressure will not survive the first six months of contact with a young professional in Jozi. The Bible study needs to understand that Wednesday evening at 7:30pm might be the only evening this person has had to themselves all week. The Bible study needs to make that evening worth showing up for.

### Financial Pressure

The cost of being a young professional in Johannesburg in 2026 is genuinely high. Rent in Sandton is between R12,000 and R22,000 a month for a one-bedroom. A car payment is between R5,000 and R8,000. Petrol is R25 a litre. Insurance is what it is. The young professional in Jozi is paying R35,000 a month to exist, on a salary that probably sits between R45,000 and R80,000 after tax. They are not failing. They are also not saving. They are one bad month away from real trouble.

A Bible study that assumes the young professional in Jozi has disposable time and disposable money is a Bible study for someone else. The Bible study for young professionals needs to be free, needs to be online-friendly, and needs to respect that the young professional is making a real sacrifice by showing up.

### Relational Pressure

Dating in Johannesburg in your late twenties and early thirties is its own special kind of pressure. The dating apps have made everyone replaceable. The social scene is exhausting. The marriage market is competitive. The biological clock is real, even if we are not supposed to say so.

A Bible study for young professionals will eventually get asked about relationships. It will get asked about dating. It will get asked about marriage. It will get asked about whether it is okay to sleep with someone you are dating. It needs to have thought about these questions before they come up.

### Identity Pressure

This is the deepest pressure. The young professional in Jozi has been told, since they were a child, that they are the future of South Africa. They are the born-free generation. They are the ones who will fix the country. They are the ones who will inherit the economy. The reality of being 29 in Johannesburg in 2026 is somewhat less triumphant than the narrative. Unemployment is high. The economy is sluggish. The country is not what it was promised to be.

A Bible study for young professionals needs to make room for the gap between the promise and the reality. It needs to be a place where the young professional can say *"I do not know if I am going to be okay"* without being told to just trust God more.

## What Bible Study Looks Like for Jozi Young Professionals

After ten years of running a Bible study in Parkmore and five years of running the UseCan youth programme, here is what we have learned about what works.

### 1. Online, With a Once-a-Month In-Person Option

The midweek Bible study needs to be online. There is no way around this. The young professional in Jozi is not going to drive across Sandton at 7pm on a Wednesday for a Bible study. The traffic, the cost of petrol, the time — none of it works.

What does work is a Google Meet link that gets sent out at lunchtime on Wednesday. The young professional can join from home, from the office, from the airport, from a hotel room in Cape Town. The link works on data. The discussion works on mute.

Then, once a month, the same Bible study has an in-person evening. A Saturday. A braai. A park. A meal. This is where the relationships deepen. The online Bible study is the substance. The in-person evening is the glue.

### 2. Short, Focused, Honest

The Bible study should be seventy-five minutes, not two hours. It should open with a quick check-in. It should have one passage, not three. It should have one main question, not five. It should have space for honest discussion, not space for a lecture.

The young professional in Jozi has been in meetings all week. They do not need another meeting. They need a conversation. The Bible study should feel like a conversation, not a meeting.

### 3. Permission to Doubt

This is the most important thing. The Bible study for young professionals in Johannesburg needs to be a place where doubt is not just tolerated but welcomed. The room needs to make space for the question *"is this actually true?"* without panic. The leader needs to be the kind of person who has asked that question themselves.

The young professional in Jozi has been marketed at their whole life. They know when they are being sold to. They know when sincerity is being performed. The Bible study needs to be the place where performance stops, and that only happens when the leader stops performing too.

### 4. Real Theology, Not Self-Help

The young professional in Jozi does not need another productivity book repackaged as a Bible study. They do not need to be told that God wants them to be successful. They do not need to be told that their best life is now.

What they need is real theology. The book of Job. The Psalms of lament. The prophet Habakkuk. Paul's letter to the Romans, including the uncomfortable parts about the body and the spirit. The Sermon on the Mount, including the parts about loving your enemy and giving away your coat.

This is what the Bible actually says. It is also what the young professional in Jozi is actually hungry for. They have had enough self-help. They are ready for something that costs them something.

### 5. A Community That Knows Their Names

The biggest predictor of whether a young professional will keep coming back to a Bible study is whether anyone in the Bible study knows their name. Not their role. Not their company. Their name.

In our Wednesday evening, we make a point of saying every name out loud at least once in the first ten minutes. We follow up on things people shared the previous week. We notice when someone is missing. We text. We call. We ask.

This is not complicated. It is just consistent. The young professional in Jozi has been through enough environments where nobody noticed them. The Bible study is the place where someone notices.

### 6. Practical Service Opportunities

The young professional in Jozi is often desperate for a way to make a difference outside their day job. They have skills. They have energy. They have a vague feeling that their life should be about more than their career. The Bible study is a good place to channel that.

We have run service projects through the UseCan programme that have included:
- Tutoring at a primary school in Soweto
- Building a feeding scheme kitchen in Alexandra
- Mentoring Grade 11 and 12 students from under-resourced schools
- Running a financial literacy workshop for young adults in Mamelodi

The young professional in Jozi shows up for these. They come back changed. They bring friends.

## The UseCan Programme

We have written elsewhere about UseCan — the youth programme we run out of the Bible study. For young professionals specifically, UseCan is structured around four things:

- Wednesday evening online Bible study
- Saturday in-person gathering once a month
- Service project once a quarter
- One-on-one mentorship once a month

The mentorship piece is the most underrated. Every UseCan member is paired with an older member of the broader Bible study community for a monthly conversation. The conversation is not therapy. It is not Bible study. It is the kind of conversation the young professional might have had with an uncle or an older cousin, if they had one in Johannesburg.

The mentorship has been the single biggest reason young professionals stay connected to the Bible study community. They came for the Wednesday evening. They stay for the relationship with the mentor.

## A Note to the Churches Already Trying

If you are part of a church in Johannesburg that has been trying to reach young professionals and you have not been succeeding, please hear this with some grace.

It is not because your theology is wrong. It is not because your worship is wrong. It is usually because of one of three reasons.

The first reason is that the Bible study you are offering is at the wrong time. Tuesday lunchtimes do not work. Saturday mornings do not work. Wednesday at 7:30pm, online, with an in-person evening once a month, works.

The second reason is that the language is wrong. If your Bible study is conducted in a register that sounds like a 1987 sermon, the young professional will hear it as a 1987 sermon and quietly unsubscribe. Translate the register. Keep the substance.

The third reason is that the welcome is not real. The young professional in Jozi has been welcomed by enough church greeters to know what a greeter welcome looks like. They want the welcome that knows their name. They want the welcome that follows up. They want the welcome that shows up at their office with a coffee when they have had a hard week.
