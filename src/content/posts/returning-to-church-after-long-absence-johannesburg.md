## The Sunday That Was Sought

There is a Sunday morning that every South African Christian has either lived, or is living, or will live. The Sunday morning that has been a long time coming. The Sunday morning that the Christian has been thinking about for a year, or for five years, or for fifteen years. The Sunday morning when the Christian gets in the car on a Sunday morning and drives to the church that the Christian has not been to since the wedding of the cousin in 2014.

The Sunday morning is hard. The parking lot is full of the people who never stopped coming. The hymns are different. The pastor knows the children's faces. The clothes are different. The coffee after the church is the same kind of coffee, but the people who drink the coffee are different. The Christian who has come back is the Christian who is, in some ways, the newest member of the church. The Christian who has come back is, in some ways, the most vulnerable member of the church.

The Christian in Johannesburg has to decide how to make the Sunday morning. The Christian has to decide what to wear. The Christian has to decide what to say when the person at the door says, "It's so good to see you again." The Christian has to decide what to say when the person at the door does not say, "It's so good to see you again."

This post is for the Christian in Johannesburg or Sandton who has been thinking about the Sunday morning. The Sunday morning is real. The Bible is real.

## What the Bible Actually Says About Returning

The Bible has a great deal to say about returning. The Bible is, in one sense, a story of return. The Bible is the story of the human race that walked away from God. The Bible is the story of the human race that came back to God. The Bible is the story of the Father who ran to meet the child who came back.

**Luke 15:18-20** — *I will set out and go back to my father and say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me like one of your hired men. So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him.*

**Hosea 14:1** — *Return, Israel, to the Lord your God. Your sins have been your downfall!*

**Joel 2:12-13** — *Even now, declares the Lord, return to me with all your heart, with fasting and weeping and mourning. Rend your heart and not your garments. Return to the Lord your God, for he is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and abounding in love.*

**Jeremiah 31:18-19** — *I have surely heard Ephraim's moaning: You disciplined me like an unruly calf, and I have been disciplined. Restore me, and I will return, because you are the Lord my God.*

**James 4:8** — *Come near to God and he will come near to you. Wash your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded.*

**1 John 1:9** — *If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.*

The Bible treats the return as one of the great Christian acts. The Bible treats the return as an act of courage. The Bible treats the return as an act of grace. The Bible treats the Christian who has returned as a person to be welcomed, not a person to be judged.

**Galatians 6:1-2** — *Brothers and sisters, if someone is caught in a sin, you who live by the Spirit should restore that person gently. But watch yourselves, or you also may be tempted. Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfill the law of Christ.*

**2 Corinthians 2:7-8** — *Now instead, you ought to forgive and comfort him, so that he will not be overwhelmed by excessive sorrow. I urge you to reaffirm your love for him.*
