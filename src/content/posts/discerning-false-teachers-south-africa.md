## A Conversation the South African Church Needs to Have

There is a conversation the South African church has been avoiding for some time. It is the conversation about how to recognise a false teacher. The conversation is avoided because it requires naming names. The conversation is avoided because it requires the Christian to make a judgment. The conversation is avoided because the false teacher often comes in the name of prosperity, blessing, healing, breakthrough — and who wants to question those things?

This post is an attempt to have the conversation the Bible way. The way that does not name names. The way that does teach the biblical markers. The way that gives the Christian in Johannesburg — in Sandton, in Soweto, in Randburg, in Roodepoort — the tools to make the judgment themselves. The way that does the discernment work the Bible commands.

## Why This Conversation Is Particularly Urgent in South Africa Right Now

South Africa has one of the most active religious marketplaces in the world. The marketplace includes the historic mainline churches. The marketplace includes the African Independent Churches. The marketplace includes the Pentecostal and charismatic movements that have grown rapidly over the last forty years. The marketplace includes the new marketplace ministries that meet in hotels, in conference centres, in rented warehouses, in stadiums. The marketplace includes the online ministries that reach the country through YouTube and TikTok and Instagram.

Most of these ministries are sincere. Most of these ministries are trying to follow the Bible. Most of these ministries are doing the slow work of the Christian life in South Africa.

But not all of them are.

The Bible is clear that false teachers will come. The Bible is clear that the Christian is to be on guard. The Bible is clear that the Christian is to test what is being taught against the Bible itself. The Bible does not say the Christian is to be naive. The Bible does not say the Christian is to accept whatever is taught from the pulpit. The Bible says the Christian is to *test the spirits* (1 John 4:1).

## What the Bible Actually Says About False Teachers

The Bible has a great deal to say about false teachers. More than most Christians realise.

**Matthew 7:15-20** — *Watch out for false prophets. They come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ferocious wolves. By their fruit you will recognise them. Do people pick grapes from thornbushes, or figs from thistles? Likewise, every good tree bears good fruit, but a bad tree bears bad fruit. A good tree cannot bear bad fruit, and a bad tree cannot bear good fruit. Every tree that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire. So then, by their fruit you will recognise them.*

**1 John 4:1** — *Dear friends, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.*

**2 Timothy 4:3-4** — *For the time will come when people will not put up with sound doctrine. Instead, to suit their own desires, they will gather around them a great number of teachers to say what their itching ears want to hear. They will turn their ears away from the truth and turn aside to myths.*

**2 Corinthians 11:13-15** — *For such people are false apostles, deceitful workers, masquerading as apostles of Christ. And no wonder, for Satan himself masquerades as an angel of light. It is not surprising, then, if his servants also masquerade as servants of righteousness.*

**2 Peter 2:1-3** — *But there were also false prophets among the people, just as there will be false teachers among you. They will secretly introduce destructive heresies, even denying the sovereign Lord who bought them — bringing swift destruction on themselves. Many will follow their depraved conduct and will bring the way of truth into disrepute. In their greed these teachers will exploit you with fabricated stories.*

**Galatians 1:8-9** — *But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach a gospel other than the one we preached to you, let them be under God's curse! As we have already said, so now I say again: If anybody is preaching to you a gospel other than what you accepted, let them be under God's curse!*

**Acts 17:11** — *Now the Berean Jews were of more noble character than those in Thessalonica, for they received the message with great eagerness and examined the Scriptures every day to see if what Paul said was true.*

**1 Thessalonians 5:21** — *Test everything. Hold on to what is good.*

**Matthew 24:4-5** — *Jesus answered, 'Watch out that no one deceives you. For many will come in my name, claiming, "I am the Messiah," and will deceive many.'*

What you notice is that the Bible does not treat the false teacher as a rare curiosity. The Bible treats the false teacher as a regular feature of the Christian life. The Bible treats the Christian as the one who is responsible for the testing. The Bible does not say the testing is a sign of weak faith. The Bible says the testing is the act of the Berean Jews in Acts 17 — the noble thing.

## The Seven Biblical Markers of a False Teacher

Over the years, our Wednesday Bible study community in Sandton has developed a list of seven biblical markers that we have found helpful. The markers are not new inventions. The markers are drawn directly from the Bible passages above. The markers are not the last word. The markers are the first word — the questions every Christian should ask before committing to a ministry, before giving money to a ministry, before sending children to a ministry school.

### Marker 1 — What Is the Gospel?

The first question is the most important. What is the gospel being preached? Is the gospel the historic Christian gospel — that Jesus Christ is the Son of God, that he died for our sins, that he was raised on the third day, that salvation is by grace through faith? Or is the gospel a different gospel — a gospel of personal breakthrough, a gospel of national blessing, a gospel of generational wealth, a gospel that has very little to say about the cross?

Galatians 1:8-9 is severe about this marker. The verse says that even if an angel from heaven preaches a different gospel, let the angel be accursed. The verse is severe because the gospel is the centre. If the centre is shifted, the whole thing becomes something else.

A ministry that preaches the historic gospel is not exempt from the other markers. A ministry that does not preach the historic gospel has failed the first marker and should be approached with great care.

### Marker 2 — What Is Said About Money?

The second question is the question about money. What does the ministry say about money? Is the Bible's teaching on money — stewardship, generosity, contentment, the danger of the love of money — being taught? Or is the Bible's teaching on money being replaced by a different teaching — that the Christian is to be rich, that giving money to the ministry is a transaction with God, that the more you give the more God will give you?

2 Peter 2:3 is specific about this marker. *In their greed these teachers will exploit you with fabricated stories.* The marker is not that the ministry asks for money. Many good ministries ask for money. The marker is whether the ministry's teaching about money is consistent with the Bible's teaching about money. The marker is whether the asking for money is paired with the Bible's warnings about the love of money.

### Marker 3 — How Is the Leader Living?

The third question is the question about the leader's life. How is the leader living? Is the leader living in a way that is consistent with what the leader is preaching? Is the leader's family in order? Is the leader's money in order? Is the leader's ego in order? Is the leader approachable? Is the leader accountable to anyone?

Matthew 7 is clear about this marker. *By their fruit you will recognise them.* The fruit is not the size of the ministry. The fruit is not the number of miracles claimed. The fruit is the life of the leader, the life of the leadership team, the life of the people being produced by the ministry. The fruit is the long-term fruit, not the conference fruit.

This marker is the one that requires the most care. The Christian is not to judge by appearance. The Christian is to look at the long-term fruit. The Christian is to look at the marriages, the children, the businesses, the reputations of the people who have been in the ministry for ten or twenty years.

### Marker 4 — Is the Bible Actually Preached?

The fourth question is the question about the Bible. Is the Bible actually being preached? Or is the Bible being replaced by personal revelation, by prophetic words, by stories about what God told the leader, by visions and dreams that have very little grounding in the text?

Acts 17:11 — the Berean Jews — is clear about this marker. The noble thing is to examine the Scriptures daily to see if what is being taught is true. The noble thing is the Christian who brings the Bible to the sermon. The noble thing is the Christian who says *show me in the Bible*. The noble thing is the Christian who is not satisfied with *God told me*.

A ministry that does not preach the Bible verse by verse is not automatically a false ministry. A ministry that does not even own the Bible as the authority is a ministry that has failed this marker.

### Marker 5 — How Are the Vulnerable Treated?

The fifth question is the question about the vulnerable. How does the ministry treat the vulnerable people in its community — the widows, the orphans, the divorced, the unemployed, the single mothers, the ones who cannot give money, the ones who cannot attend every conference?

James 1:27 is clear about this marker. *Religion that God our Father accepts as pure and faultless is this: to look after orphans and widows in their distress and to keep oneself from being polluted by the world.* The marker is not that the ministry has a programme for the vulnerable. Many false ministries have programmes for the vulnerable. The marker is whether the vulnerable are treated as the central concern of the ministry, or as the marketing of the ministry.

### Marker 6 — Is There Accountability?

The sixth question is the question about accountability. Is the leader accountable to anyone? Is the ministry accountable to anyone? Is there a board? Is there an elder team? Is there a denomination? Is there a respected older minister who can speak into the leader's life?

Hebrews 13:17 is clear about this marker. *Have confidence in your leaders and submit to their authority, because they keep watch over you as those who must give an account.* The verse assumes that the leaders are accountable. The verse assumes that the leaders are watched over by someone. The verse does not assume the leaders are watching over themselves.

A ministry where the leader is the final word on everything — where the leader is not accountable to a board, not accountable to an elder team, not accountable to a denomination, not accountable to any older minister — is a ministry where the leader has set up a structure that the Bible does not endorse.

### Marker 7 — What Happens to the Questioner?

The seventh question is the question about the questioner. What happens to the Christian who asks a hard question? What happens to the Christian who says *show me in the Bible*? What happens to the Christian who points out that the leader's life does not match the teaching? What happens to the Christian who decides to leave the ministry and go somewhere else?

The Bible assumes the Christian is free to ask. Acts 17:11 assumes the noble thing is the asking. 1 Thessalonians 5:21 assumes the testing is the Christian's right. A ministry that responds to a hard question by shaming the questioner, by labelling the questioner as having a spirit of criticism, by suggesting that the questioner should leave, by isolating the questioner from the community — that ministry has failed this marker. That ministry is the ministry the Berean Jews would have walked out of.

## What the Bible Does Not Require

It is just as important to be clear about what the Bible does not require of the Christian.

The Bible does not require the Christian to find a perfect ministry. There is no perfect ministry. Every ministry has weaknesses. Every ministry has blind spots. Every ministry has personalities that the Christian has to navigate.

The Bible does not require the Christian to stay in a ministry that has clearly failed the seven markers. The Christian is free to leave. The Christian is free to leave without drama. The Christian is free to leave without making the leaving the centre of the conversation. The Christian is free to leave and find a Bible study, a house church, an online community, a denomination.

The Bible does not require the Christian to be suspicious of every ministry. Most ministries are trying to follow the Bible. Most ministries are doing the slow work of the Christian life. Most ministries deserve the benefit of the doubt. The seven markers are not for the ministry the Christian has been attending for twenty years without serious concern. The seven markers are for the new ministry the Christian is considering. The seven markers are for the ministry that has raised a question.

## A Prayer for the Discerning South African Christian

If you are choosing a ministry, leaving a ministry, or sitting in a ministry that has begun to trouble you — here is a prayer.

> *God of the truth,*
>
> *We thank you for your word. We thank you for the Berean Jews who examined the Scriptures daily. We thank you for the Spirit who guides us into all truth.*
>
> *We pray for discernment. We pray for the wisdom to test what is being taught. We pray for the courage to ask the hard question. We pray for the humility to leave when the Bible says leave. We pray for the grace to stay when the Bible says stay.*
>
> *We pray for the ministries that are faithfully preaching your word. We pray for the leaders who are accountable to you and to your people. We pray for the vulnerable in every congregation in South Africa. We pray for the ones who are caught in the false ministries, who do not yet know they are caught. We pray for the ones who are about to be caught. We pray that they will read the Bible before they sign the form. We pray that they will test the fruit before they send the money.*
>
> *Give us the wisdom of the serpent and the innocence of the dove (Matthew 10:16). Give us the courage of the Bereans. Give us the patience to wait for the long-term fruit.*
>
> *In the name of the one who is the way, the truth, and the life.*
> *Amen.*
