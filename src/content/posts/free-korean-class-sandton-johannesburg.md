## The Class That Started Because One Person Asked

In 2022, a young woman named Grace moved to Johannesburg from Cape Town for a marketing job. She did not know anyone in the city. She was watching K-dramas in her spare time and thought it would be useful to actually understand what people were saying.

She asked Mr. Sim if he knew anyone who taught Korean.

He did not. But he knew Ms. Dora, who is Korean, and who said she would be happy to teach a class if there was interest.

That first Sunday, four people showed up. By the third week, there were eleven. Two years later, the Sunday Korean class at Johannesburg Bible Study Church in Parkmore, Sandton, runs every week with 8 to 15 adults, and there is a waiting list for babysitting slots.

## What the Class Actually Is

This is not a formal language school with exams. There is a simple weekly rhythm: worksheets, conversation practice, and a teacher who grew up speaking Korean.

Ms. Dora teaches the way you would learn if a Korean friend invited you over for coffee and decided to show you a few things.

Week one: Hangul — the Korean alphabet. You will be reading Korean by the end of the session. That is the entire point of week one. Hangul is designed to be learnable in an afternoon, and it is.

Week two through four: Basic conversation. Greetings, numbers, ordering food, asking for directions. The things you actually need if you are going to a Korean restaurant or watching a K-drama without subtitles.

Week five through eight: Cultural context. Why Korean has formal and informal speech. How age determines how people speak to each other. What to actually do when you meet a Korean elder.

By week eight: You can introduce yourself in Korean, ask where the bathroom is, order at a Korean restaurant, and understand why your favorite K-drama character is being politely rude to someone older.

## Who Comes to the Korean Class

The class attracts three main types of people:

**K-drama and K-pop fans** — They have been watching content with subtitles for years. They want to understand without reading. They usually progress fastest because they already have vocabulary from exposure.

**People preparing for Korea** — Job transfers, university exchanges, missionary work, gap years. They need functional Korean quickly. The class is intensive enough to give them a foundation.

**Curious community members** — They heard about the class, it sounded interesting, and they showed up once. Some of them keep showing up because the community is good, even if they do not particularly care about Korea.
