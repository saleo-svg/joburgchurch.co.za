# When the Church Changes the Language: A South African Christian's Guide to Congregational Language Shift and the God Who Speaks Every Language

## The Scene

The elderly Afrikaner woman in Randburg has attended the same church for thirty years. The church is now conducting services mostly in English. She cannot follow. She feels like a foreigner in her own church. She has not said anything. She is considering leaving.

**Acts 2:42-47** — *"They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What Acts 2 Says About Language and the Church

Acts 2:5-11 describes the Day of Pentecost when people from many nations heard the gospel in their own language. The miracle was not that everyone spoke one language. The miracle was that the gospel was comprehensible to each person in their own tongue. The church that worships in only one language in a multilingual city is missing something of what the Spirit did at Pentecost.

## The Grief of the Language Shift

The person who has worshipped in a language and finds that language gone is experiencing real grief. Language carries identity, memory, and belonging. The church that does not acknowledge this grief is failing its older members.

## Practical Solutions for the Multilingual Church

First: provide translation in the service — headsets, real-time translation. Second: have some services in the original language. Third: make the new language an addition, not a replacement. Fourth: ask the older members what they need. Fifth: celebrate the multilingual reality as a strength, not a problem.

## A Prayer for the Church in Language Transition

Father, we bring the church that is changing languages. We bring the grief of the person who feels displaced. We bring the excitement of the person who feels seen. We ask for the wisdom to honour the old and embrace the new. We ask for the translation that makes the gospel comprehensible to all. We ask for the community that transcends language. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
