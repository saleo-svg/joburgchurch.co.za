## The Quiet Middle of the Year

It is July. In Johannesburg, that means the Highveld is at its coldest. The mornings come late. The sun sets at 5:30pm. The jacarandas are a memory. The summer rain is four months away.

It is also, in the life of the South African Christian, the quiet middle of the year. The Easter energy of April is gone. The December anticipation has not yet started. The mid-year slump is real. The New Year resolutions have been quietly abandoned. The Bible reading plan from January is half-finished. The church attendance has settled into the second-half rhythm, which is the rhythm most people settle into for the rest of the year.

This post is for the Christian in Johannesburg who has noticed that it is July and they are not where they thought they would be. It is for the Bible study member who made a commitment in January to read the Bible every day and has slipped to three times a week. It is for the Sandton professional who made a commitment in January to be more generous and has slipped back into the default. It is for the young adult who committed in January to pray before making decisions and has slipped back into the reactivity of the week.

This is the post I wish someone had given me in July of every year of my adult life. It is not a sermon. It is a practical guide for a mid-year reset.

## Why July Is the Right Time for a Reset

Most Christian reflection guides are written for January. The new year. The fresh start. The resolutions. The list of changes you are going to make. The list of things you are going to do this year that you did not do last year.

The problem with January is that it is too early. You do not yet know what the year is going to ask of you. You do not yet know which of the changes you planned are actually going to be possible. You do not yet know which of the relationships are going to need your attention. You do not yet know which of the resolutions are going to hold and which are going to fall apart by February.

By July, you know. You know what the year has been like. You know which of the changes stuck. You know which of the resolutions were the right ones and which were the wrong ones. You know where the gaps are. You know what needs to change in the second half of the year.

July is the right time for a reset. Not because the new year is the only time for new beginnings. Because the middle of the year is the time when new beginnings are most honest.

## The Biblical Case for the Middle of the Year

The Bible is full of middles. Here are five.

### The Israelites in the Wilderness — Exodus 16

The Israelites are six weeks into the Exodus. They have left Egypt. They are at Sinai. They are out of food. They complain. God sends manna. The instruction is that the manna is gathered fresh each morning, and that no one is allowed to store it for the next day.

The mid-year of the Exodus is the moment when the Israelites have to learn to depend on God for the day's provision, not for the year's provision. They cannot store up. They can only gather what they need for the day. The discipline of the middle is the discipline of the day.

### The Disciples in the Storm — Mark 6:45-52

Jesus sends the disciples across the Sea of Galilee while he goes up the mountain to pray. They are halfway across. The wind picks up. They are straining at the oars. Jesus comes to them walking on the water. He gets into the boat. The wind dies down.

The middle of the journey is the moment of the storm. The middle is not a sign that you are lost. The middle is the place where the miracle happens.

### Paul in the Corinthian Wilderness — 2 Corinthians 1:8-10

Paul writes to the Corinthians about a time he was under such intense pressure that he despaired even of life. He says, *"We felt we were under the sentence of death."* Then he adds, *"This happened that we might not rely on ourselves but on God, who raises the dead."*

The middle of the Christian life is the place where you stop relying on yourself. The middle is the wilderness. The middle is the storm. The middle is the place where the self-sufficiency you brought in January runs out, and the God-sufficiency you did not know you needed shows up.

### The Psalm of the Middle — Psalm 102

The psalmist writes in Psalm 102, *"My days are like the evening shadow; I wither away like grass."* This is a mid-year psalm. The energy of the morning is gone. The psalmist is in the evening of his life. He writes, *"But you, Lord, sit enthroned forever."* The middle of the psalm is the place where the present moment is small and the eternal God is large.

### Jesus at Twelve — Luke 2:41-52

Mary and Joseph lose Jesus for three days on the way home from Jerusalem. They find him in the temple, listening to the teachers and asking them questions. He says to his mother, *"Did you not know I had to be in my Father's house?"*

The middle of Jesus's childhood is the moment when he begins to understand who he is. The middle is not a failure of the family. The middle is the first sign of the vocation that will define the rest of his life.

## A Practical July Reset for the Christian in Johannesburg

Here is the reset we use in our Wednesday Bible study community in Parkmore. We do it every July. It is built for the mid-year, and it takes about three weeks to complete.

### Week 1 — The Review

For the first week of July, do not try to change anything. Just review.

Pick up your journal from January, if you have one. If you do not have a journal, open the notes app on your phone. Look at what you wrote. Look at what you planned. Look at the resolutions you made, the Bible reading plan you started, the prayer commitments you made.

Now ask three questions.

- **What worked?** What did I actually do this year? What stuck from the January plans? What did I start that has continued?
- **What did not work?** What did I plan that did not happen? What slipped? What did I commit to in January that I have quietly let go of?
- **What changed?** What did I not plan that happened anyway? What changed in my life this year that I did not see coming? What is the year actually about, now that I am halfway through it?

The first week of July is the week you stop pretending. You stop pretending the January plan is still in place. You stop pretending you read the Bible every day when you have actually read it three times this week. You stop pretending the quiet time is at 6am when it has been at 10pm for months. The first week is the week you tell the truth.

### Week 2 — The Reset

For the second week of July, do not start anything new. Just reset what you have.

- If the Bible reading plan is broken, do not start a new one. Find the day you actually stopped reading. Go back to that day. Pick up from there.
- If the quiet time has slipped, do not promise to do it at 5am. Promise to do it at the time of day that has actually been happening. Five minutes at 10pm is a quiet time. Forty-five minutes at 5am that you keep skipping is not.
- If the prayer commitments have lapsed, do not recommit to praying for the entire list every day. Pick three names. Pray for them this week. Add three more next week. Build back up.
- If the giving has slipped, do not promise to double the amount. Promise to give the same amount you have been giving, and add one more thing. A meal for someone. A gift for the Bible study community. A donation to a charity you have been meaning to support.

The second week is the week you rebuild the habit. The habit is what matters. The size of the habit matters less than the consistency of it.

### Week 3 — The Second-Half Plan

For the third week of July, plan the second half of the year.

Ask three more questions.

- **What does the second half need?** Not what do I want the second half to be. What does the second half of 2026 actually need? What are the conversations I have been avoiding? What are the relationships that need attention? What are the parts of my life that are quietly broken?
- **What is the one thing I can commit to for the rest of the year?** Not ten things. One thing. The one thing that, if I do it, will change the second half of my year.
- **Who do I need to ask for help?** Most mid-year resets fail because the person tries to do them alone. The Christian tradition has always understood that the disciplines of the faith are not solo activities. They are community activities. The Bible study is the place where the second-half plan gets shared.

The third week is the week you commit. You commit to the one thing. You commit to the community that will help you do it. You commit to the rest of the year.

## How the Wednesday Bible Study Does the Mid-Year Reset

For our Wednesday Bible study community in Parkmore, the last Wednesday in July is our mid-year reset evening. The format is different from a normal Bible study.

The first thirty minutes are a review. We go around the room and each person shares one thing that has happened in their year so far that has shaped their faith. Not the highlight reel. The real thing. The hospital visit. The job loss. The marriage struggle. The child who has gone off the rails. The parent who has died. The friend who has come back.

The second thirty minutes are a prayer. We pray for the second half of the year. We pray for each other. We pray for South Africa. We pray with the honesty that only July can hold.

The third thirty minutes are a Psalm. We read a Psalm together. We usually pick Psalm 131 — *"My heart is not proud, O Lord, my eyes are not haughty; I do not concern myself with great matters or things too wonderful for me."* It is the shortest psalm in the Bible. It is the psalm for the middle of the year.

## A Mid-Year Prayer for the Christian in Johannesburg

If you would like a prayer for your own mid-year reset, here is one we have used on the last Wednesday in July in Parkmore.

> *God of the middle,*
>
> *We come to you at the middle of the year. We come tired. We come honest. We come with the plans that worked and the plans that did not. We come with the relationships that grew and the ones that broke. We come with the Bible reading that happened and the quiet time that did not.*
>
> *Forgive us for the resolutions we made in January that we abandoned by March. Forgive us for the prayer commitments we have let slide. Forgive us for the Bible reading plan that is six weeks behind.*
>
> *Forgive us for the comparison. For the way we have looked at the people in our Bible study and thought they were doing better than we are. Forgive us for the way we have looked at the people in our Bible study and thought they were doing worse than we are. We are all in the middle. We all need you.*
>
> *For the second half of this year, give us the grace to begin again. Not from January. From now. From the place we actually are. From the habit we actually have. From the prayer time we actually keep.*
>
> *Give us the honesty to admit where we are. Give us the humility to ask for help. Give us the courage to commit to the one thing that matters. Give us the wisdom to let the other things go.*
>
> *We pray for South Africa in the second half of 2026. For the families under pressure. For the marriages that are struggling. For the young people who are tired. For the elderly who are lonely. For the unemployed who have given up. For the employed who have given in.*
>
> *May the second half of this year be different. Not because we are different. Because you are faithful.*
>
> *We pray in the name of the one who walks with us in the middle of the storm.*
> *Amen.*
