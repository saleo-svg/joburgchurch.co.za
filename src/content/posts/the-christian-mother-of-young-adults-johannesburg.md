# When the Children Have Grown: A Christian Mother's Honest Reflection on the Empty Nest in Johannesburg

## The Scene

The last child has moved out on a Tuesday in October. The room is empty. The uniform is in the cupboard. The mother in Fourways sits in the kitchen with a cup of tea that is going cold and a feeling she cannot name.

**Psalm 136:1** — *"Give thanks to the Lord, because he is good. His love endures forever."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Bible's Picture of Motherhood

The Bible has the highest view of motherhood. The children are a heritage from the Lord (Psalm 127:3). The mother holds the children for a season and then releases them.

## The Honest Grief of the Empty Nest

The mother who feels the grief is not being unspiritual. She is being human. The Bible does not say that the mother should be relieved when the children leave. The grief is real, named, and legitimate.

## What the Mother Can Hold Onto

The covenant does not end. The mother is still a mother. The mother has a relationship with God that is entirely her own.

## Practical Steps for the Season

Allow the grief. Find a new rhythm. Maintain the connection with the children, but change the nature of the connection. Join a community. Pray differently.

## A Prayer for the Mother in the Empty Season

Father, you know this season. We thank you for the children you have given us. We release them into your hands. We ask for the courage to find ourselves again. We ask for the community to sustain us. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
