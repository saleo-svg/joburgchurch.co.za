# The Christian Father Who Works Too Much: A South African's Honest Reflection on the Office That Has Stolen the Home

## The Scene

The father in Sandton comes home at eight at night for the third time this week. The children are asleep. The wife has left a plate in the microwave.

**Mark 10:45** — *"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Biblical Command to Fathers

Ephesians 6:4: 'Fathers, do not exasperate your children; instead, bring them up in the training and instruction of the Lord.' The command is not 'provide financially.' The command is 'be present.'

## The Culture That Enables the Absent Father

The South African economy in 2026 requires long hours. The Sandton office culture rewards presence. The job that provides the school fees also takes the father away. The Bible does not pretend this tension does not exist.

## Practical Steps for the Father Who Wants to Be Present

One dedicated time every day, even fifteen minutes. The bedtime routine. Have the honest conversation with the employer. Give up something work-related that was not really necessary.

## A Word to the Father Who Feels Guilty

The guilty father is often the father who cares. The guilt is a signal, not a sentence. The father who has missed years and is now trying is better than the father who never tried.

## A Prayer for the Tired Father

Father, we confess the ways we have let work replace home. Give us the wisdom to work hard and love well at the same time. Give us the grace to be present, even when we are tired. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
