# When the Church Hurts You: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Conflict, Pain, and Staying in Community

## The Scene

The woman in Randburg was hurt by something the pastor said from the pulpit. It was not directed at her by name, but everyone knew who it was about. She has not been back to church in three months. She is not sure if she is running away from God or protecting herself.

**Colossians 3:13** — *"Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What Matthew 18 Actually Says About Church Conflict

Matthew 18:15-17 is one of the most important passages about conflict in the church. It says: 'If your brother or sister sins, go and point out their fault, just between the two of you. If they listen to you, you have won them over.' The first step is private confrontation, not public complaint. The second step is bringing one or two witnesses. The third step is telling the church. The fourth step is treating them as a pagan or tax collector.

This process is almost never followed in South African churches. People either say nothing (and leave) or say everything publicly (and cause division). The Bible's middle path — direct, private, then escalating — is the right one.

## The Difference Between Forgiveness and Reconciliation

Forgiveness is something you do in your own heart — you release the right to revenge. Reconciliation is something two people do together — it restores the relationship. Forgiveness is always possible. Reconciliation requires both parties. The person who has been hurt in the church can forgive the pastor in their heart without returning to that pastor's church.

## When Leaving Is the Right Answer

Leaving a church is not always the wrong answer. The letter to the Hebrews says: 'Let us leave the elementary teachings about Christ and go on to maturity' (Hebrews 6:1). This implies that sometimes leaving is necessary for growth. The Christian who has been genuinely abused, or who is in a church that is teaching false doctrine, should leave. But the Christian who has been hurt by an imperfect church over an imperfect sermon should consider staying and working it through.

## Practical Steps Before You Leave

First: have the private conversation (Matthew 18:15). Second: bring a witness if needed. Third: give it time — immediate departure after a wound is usually driven by emotion rather than wisdom. Fourth: talk to a mature Christian you trust before deciding. Fifth: if you do leave, leave gracefully — do not cause division in your departure.

## A Prayer for the Person Who Has Been Hurt by the Church

Father, we bring the hurt that the church has caused. We bring the person who is not sure if they should stay or go. We bring the anger that is hard to release. We ask for the wisdom to know the difference between a wounding church and a wounding moment. We ask for the courage to confront directly. We ask for the grace to forgive even when reconciliation is not possible. We ask for the strength to stay in community, if staying is right, and to leave gracefully, if leaving is right. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
