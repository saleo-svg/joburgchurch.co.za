# The N1 Traffic and the Soul: A South African Christian's Guide to the Commute as a Place of Spiritual Practice

## The Scene

The woman in Midrand has a forty-five-minute commute each way. She has done this for four years. She has learned to use the time. The commute is not wasted. It is the most consistent spiritual practice she has.

**Jeremiah 29:12-13** — *"Then you will call on me and come and pray to me, and I will listen to you. You will seek me and find me when you seek me with all your heart."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Time

Ephesians 5:15-16 says: 'Be very careful, then, how you live — not as unwise but as wise, making the most of every opportunity.' The word 'opportunity' in the Greek is 'kairos' — not just time, but significant time. The commute is not a wasteland between work and home. It is an opportunity.

## Three Spiritual Practices for the Commute

First: the commute can be a prayer walk in a car. The Lord\'s Prayer, the Jesus Prayer, or simple breath prayers can be prayed in traffic without a Bible or a phone. Second: the commute can be an audio Bible time. The Bible App has an audio feature. An hour of driving equals an hour of Bible reading. Third: the commute can be a gratitude practice. On the drive in, name three things you are grateful for. On the drive home, name three things that happened today that you are grateful for.

## A Word to the Commuter Who Is Too Tired

The commuter who is too tired for spiritual practice is being honest. The commute is a place of recovery as well as practice. If you are too tired, rest. Do not add guilt to exhaustion. But if you have even ten minutes of alertness in the commute, use them for something that feeds the soul.

## A Prayer for the Daily Commute

Father, we bring the commute that is long and the driver who is tired. We ask for the commute to be a prayer, even a wordless one. We ask for the commute to be a place of your presence, not just a place of frustration. We ask for safe roads, patient hearts, and the wisdom to use the time you have given us well. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
