# When the Exam Paper Is Blank: A South African Christian's Guide to Exam Anxiety, Performance, and the God Who Is Not Disappointed

## The Scene

The eighteen-year-old in Pretoria has been studying since six in the morning. The exam is tomorrow. The notes are everywhere. She cannot remember what she read an hour ago. Her mother is praying downstairs. She is sitting on the bed staring at a wall of equations that will not stay in her head.

**Philippians 4:6-7** — *"Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Performance and Worth

The South African student lives in one of the most academically pressurised environments in the world. The matric results determine university access. The university results determine the career. The career determines the family's future. This pressure is real. But the Bible makes a radical counter-proposal: your worth is not determined by your performance.

Romans 5:8 says: 'While we were still sinners, Christ died for us.' This means God loved you before the exam results, not because of them. Your identity is not 'the student who passed' or 'the student who failed.' Your identity is beloved child of God.

## The Specific Sin of Making Exam Results an Idol

Matthew 6:33 says: 'Seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well.' The student who makes exam results an idol has reversed the order. They are seeking first the marks, and hoping God will be added as a reward. This does not work. The student who seeks God first — who studies with integrity, works hard, and then trusts the outcome — is the student who has the right order.

## Practical Steps for the Anxious Student

First: prepare practically — study, rest, eat, exercise. God helps those who help themselves. Second: recognise the physical symptoms of anxiety — rapid heartbeat, shallow breathing, intrusive thoughts. These are physical, not spiritual. Third: pray specifically — not 'God help me pass' but 'God give me peace, give me focus, help me remember what I have studied.' Fourth: have a plan for failure. Know that if the results are not what you hoped, the world has not ended.

## A Word to the Student Who Failed

The student who did not pass has not failed God. Proverbs 3:5-6 says: 'Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding.' The failed exam is not the end of the story. It is a redirection. Many of the most faithful Christians in Johannesburg have a story about the exam they failed and what happened next.

## A Prayer for the Student Before the Exam

Father, we bring the student who is anxious. We bring the student who has studied and is still afraid. We bring the student who has not studied and is afraid. We ask for focus in the last hours of preparation. We ask for calm on the day of the exam. We ask for the peace that is not dependent on the outcome. We ask for the faith to know that you love us whether we pass or fail. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
