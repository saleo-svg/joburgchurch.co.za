## You Are Not the Only One Who Feels Nervous

When people search for "Bible study for beginners Johannesburg" or "first time Bible study," they usually imagine a room full of people who already know the Bible, each other, and what they are doing.

That is not what it looks like here.

Last Wednesday, a man named David joined for his first time. He had not opened a Bible since school. He said that out loud, in the chat, before we started. Three people responded with emojis. Mr. Sim said, "Good, that means you are in the right place." Nobody made a big deal of it. We moved on to the passage.

Twenty minutes later, David asked the question that five other people had apparently been sitting with for weeks. The room lit up. Three people responded. Mr. Sim followed the thread for ten minutes. By the end of the night, David had three people offering to send him resources.

That is a typical first session.

## What You Actually Need to Know Before You Come

Nothing.

You do not need to know the Bible. You do not need to have read the passage beforehand. You do not need to know what a chapter or verse is. You do not need to have any religious background, any belief system, or any particular level of faith.

The passage is shared in the WhatsApp group on Monday or Tuesday. If you want to read ahead, you can. If you want to wait and hear it fresh on Wednesday, that works too.

You do not need to bring a Bible. If you have one, bring it. If you do not, Mr. Sim will share the text in the chat and we read it on screen.

You do not need to pray out loud. You do not need to introduce yourself unless you want to. You do not need to say anything at all.

## What Actually Happens on a Wednesday

7:25pm — You join the Google Meet. Cameras are on or off. You see 10 to 20 names on the screen. Someone says hello in the chat. Someone else shares a meme from earlier that day. It is casual.

7:30pm — We start. Mr. Sim or a volunteer reads the passage out loud. Everyone follows along. If you do not have a Bible open, you will see it shared on screen.

7:35pm — We go around the room. Anyone can share a word, a phrase, or nothing. Sometimes there is silence for a minute. Sometimes someone asks a question immediately. It depends on the passage and who shows up.

7:45pm — Discussion opens. This is the main part. Mr. Sim asks a question about the passage. People respond. Questions come back. Sometimes the question on the table is obvious. Sometimes it takes 20 minutes to find the real question.

8:30pm — Someone asks the thing that three other people were too shy to ask. This is usually the most useful part of the evening.

9:00pm — Mr. Sim closes. People say goodnight in the chat. The room empties.

## What If I Ask Something Stupid?

There is no such thing as a stupid question in this room.

Last month we spent 25 minutes on the sentence "What does 'my right hand' mean in this context?" It turned out to be a genuinely interesting question about ancient Hebrew poetry structure that led us somewhere useful.

We have also spent entire sessions on "I do not understand what is happening in this chapter." That is fine too.

The people who ask questions here are not Bible scholars who already know the answers. They are people who are reading the text carefully and genuinely trying to understand it. If you are doing the same thing, you belong here.

## Why Wednesday, Why Online

Wednesday works because it breaks the week. Tuesday is too soon after Monday. Thursday is too close to the weekend. Wednesday at 7:30pm is the mid-point. You have gotten through the worst of the week. You have two more days to go. This is a reasonable way to spend 90 minutes.

Online works because it removes friction. No commute. No parking. No commitment beyond 90 minutes. If you try it once and do not like it, you have lost 90 minutes and a Wednesday evening. That is a low-risk experiment.

## Common Objections We Hear

**"I do not know enough to join a Bible study."**
You do not need to know anything. That is the point.

**"Everyone will know I am new."**
Nobody will treat you differently. We were all new once. Mr. Sim was new once.

**"I do not believe in God / I am not religious / I am just curious."**
That is fine. Several people in the room are in the same position. You do not have to declare anything. You can just listen.

**"I will feel pressured to join or commit."**
You will not. The only pressure is whatever you put on yourself.

**"I do not have time every Wednesday."**
You do not have to come every week. Come when you can. Skip when you cannot.
