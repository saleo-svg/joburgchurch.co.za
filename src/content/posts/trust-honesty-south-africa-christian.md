## The Quiet Withdrawal of Trust

It did not happen all at once. There was no single morning when South Africans decided to stop trusting each other. There was a series of small withdrawals, spread across a decade, that have added up to something we now call the trust crisis.

The plumber who said he would come on Tuesday and came on Friday. The contractor who quoted one price and billed another. The salesperson who said the car had a full service history. The landlord who said the geyser was new. The mechanic who said the part was original. The colleague who said they would send the document and did not. The cousin who said they would return the money on payday and did not. The politician who said something on Sunday and did something else on Monday. The pastor who said the money was for the church and it turned out it was for the house.

None of these is a national scandal. None of them is a major corruption case. All of them, taken together, have produced the moment we are living in — a moment in which South Africans have begun to assume, before the evidence is in, that the other person is not going to do what they said they would do.

This is the quiet withdrawal of trust. It is more corrosive than a major scandal. It does not make the news. It changes the temperature of every small interaction in every day in every city in the country.

This post is for the Christian in Johannesburg who has noticed this. It is for the Christian who has been lied to at work, who has been let down at the workshop, who has been disappointed by the colleague, who has watched the news and wondered if any of it is real. It is for the Christian who wants to do something different, but is not sure what the Bible has to say about it.

The Bible has a great deal to say about it.

## What the Bible Actually Says About Truth

The Bible treats truth as one of the central things that the people of God are called to embody. Truth is not an abstract value in the Bible. Truth is a way of life.

**Exodus 20:16** — *You shall not give false testimony against your neighbour.*

**Exodus 23:1** — *Do not spread false reports. Do not help a guilty person by being a malicious witness.*

**Leviticus 19:11** — *Do not steal. Do not lie. Do not deceive one another.*

**Psalm 15:1-2** — *Lord, who may dwell in your sacred tent? Who may rest on your holy mountain? The one whose walk is blameless, who does what is righteous, who speaks the truth from their heart.*

**Proverbs 12:22** — *The Lord detests lying lips, but he delights in people who are trustworthy.*

**Proverbs 19:5** — *A false witness will not go unpunished, and whoever pours out lies will not go free.*

**Proverbs 25:18** — *Like a club or a sword or a sharp arrow is one who gives false testimony against a neighbour.*

**Zechariah 8:16-17** — *These are the things you are to do: Speak the truth to each other, and render true and sound judgment in your courts; do not plot evil against each other, and do not love to swear falsely. I hate all this, declares the Lord.*

**Ephesians 4:25** — *Therefore each of you must put off falsehood and speak truthfully to your neighbour, for we are all members of one body.*

**Ephesians 4:15** — *Instead, speaking the truth in love, we will grow to become in every respect the mature body of him who is the head, that is, Christ.*

**Colossians 3:9-10** — *Do not lie to each other, since you have taken off your old self with its practices and have put on the new self, which is being renewed in knowledge in the image of its Creator.*

**John 8:32** — *Then you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.*

**John 14:6** — *Jesus answered, 'I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.'*

Two things stand out. First, the Bible treats lying as something God hates. Not as a small thing. Not as a forgivable social habit. As something that is incompatible with the character of God. Second, the Bible treats truth-telling as part of the new creation. The Christian who has put on the new self speaks truthfully. The Christian who has been renewed speaks truthfully. Truth-telling is not a virtue reserved for the especially holy. Truth-telling is part of what it means to follow Jesus.

## What the Bible Says About the Person Whose Word Can Be Trusted

The second thing the Bible has a great deal to say about is the person whose word can be trusted. The Bible has a name for this person. The Bible calls the person faithful.

**Proverbs 20:6** — *Many claim to have unfailing love, but a faithful person who can find?*

**Proverbs 28:20** — *A faithful person will be richly blessed, but one eager to get rich will not go unpunished.*

**1 Corinthians 4:2** — *Now it is required that those who have been given a trust must prove faithful.*

**Luke 16:10** — *Whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much.*

**Galatians 5:22-23** — *But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control. Against such things there is no law.*

The Christian in Johannesburg is not called to be famous. The Christian in Johannesburg is not called to be successful. The Christian in Johannesburg is called to be faithful. The Christian whose word can be trusted at the office, at the workshop, at the family table, at the small daily interaction — that Christian is doing the long work of the kingdom.

The Christian whose word can be trusted is not doing it for the credit. The Christian whose word can be trusted is doing it because the Christian has been made new in Christ. The Christian whose word can be trusted is not performing honesty. The Christian whose word can be trusted has been given honesty as a gift, and is trying, imperfectly, to live into the gift.

## Why This Is Hard for the Christian in Johannesburg

This is hard for the Christian in Johannesburg for three reasons.

### 1. The System Often Rewards the Opposite

The Christian in Johannesburg who has tried to be honest in business has often discovered that the honest version of business is slower, more expensive, and less competitive than the dishonest version. The Christian who has tried to be honest in the workplace has often discovered that the colleague who cuts corners is the colleague who gets promoted. The Christian who has tried to be honest in the family has often discovered that the relative who exaggerates is the relative who gets the help.

The Bible is honest about this too. The Psalm 73 psalm is the psalm of the person who has noticed that the wicked prosper. The Christian in Johannesburg who has noticed that the dishonest succeed is in good company with the Psalms.

**Psalm 73:2-3** — *But as for me, my feet had almost slipped; I had nearly lost my foothold. For I envied the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked.*

**Psalm 73:25-26** — *Whom have I in heaven but you? And earth has nothing I desire besides you. My flesh and my heart may fail, but God is the strength of my heart and my portion forever.*

The Christian in Johannesburg is not called to win the system. The Christian in Johannesburg is called to be faithful within the system. The Christian in Johannesburg who keeps their word when the system rewards breaking it is doing something the Bible has been asking the people of God to do for three thousand years.

### 2. The Family Sometimes Expects the Opposite

The Christian in Johannesburg who has tried to be honest in the family has sometimes discovered that the family expects a certain amount of performance. The Christian who admits to losing the job is treated differently than the Christian who says they have resigned to focus on new opportunities. The Christian who admits to the affair is treated differently than the Christian who says they have been on a long retreat.

The Bible is honest about this too. The Bible has a great deal to say about the difference between performance and truth. The Psalm 51 is the prayer of a person who has stopped performing.

**Psalm 51:6** — *Yet you desired faithfulness even in the womb; you taught me wisdom in that secret place.*

**Psalm 51:10** — *Create in me a pure heart, O God, and renew a steadfast spirit within me.*

**Psalm 51:17** — *My sacrifice, O God, is a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart you, God, will not despise.*

The Christian in Johannesburg who can be honest with the family has begun to live the prayer of Psalm 51. The Christian who can be broken before God has begun to be free before the family.

### 3. The Christian Often Does Not Know How to Begin

The Christian in Johannesburg who has decided to be a person of their word often does not know how to begin. The damage already done in the family, the workplace, the small daily interactions of the last decade is hard to reverse. The Christian who tries to be honest is sometimes told that they have changed. The Christian who tries to be honest is sometimes told that they are being difficult.

The Bible is honest about this too. The Bible does not promise the Christian that honesty will be easy. The Bible does not promise the Christian that honesty will be rewarded in the short term. The Bible promises the Christian that honesty is the way of the kingdom. The Bible promises the Christian that honesty is the way of the Spirit. The Bible promises the Christian that the fruit of the Spirit is faithfulness.

The Christian who has decided to be a person of their word has begun a long work. The work is not rewarded in the next quarterly review. The work is rewarded over a lifetime. The work is rewarded in the eyes of the God who sees.

## What the Sandton Bible Study Has Tried to Do

In the last few years, the Wednesday evening Bible study has tried to take honesty seriously. Here is what we have done, for what it is worth.

### We Have Done a Psalm 51 Wednesday

We have set aside one Wednesday a year to read Psalm 51 in full. We have asked the room to confess honestly the ways we have lied, exaggerated, performed, or pretended. We have not asked for detail. We have not asked for performance. We have asked for honesty. The Christian who can confess the small lies is in a position to do something about the big ones.

### We Have Read the Sermon on the Mount Slowly

We have spent four Wednesdays in a row reading Matthew 5-7. The Sermon on the Mount is mostly about truth-telling. The Christian who reads the Sermon on the Mount carefully has to reckon with the gap between what Jesus asks and what the Christian does. The Christian who can reckon with the gap honestly has begun the long work of closing it.

### We Have Asked Each Other the Hard Question

We have, on a few Wednesdays, asked each other the hard question. Are there ways you are not being truthful at work? Are there ways you are not being truthful with your family? Are there ways you are not being truthful with yourself? We have done this with care. We have done it in pairs, not in the open room. The Christian who has been asked the hard question by a friend is in a different position than the Christian who has only asked it of themselves.

### We Have Made One Small Commitment

For one week, we have asked each member of the Bible study to make one small commitment to truth. One conversation that needs to happen. One lie that needs to be taken back. One thing that needs to be said honestly. We have asked the room to come back the next Wednesday and tell the room how it went. The Christian who can report on one small act of truth has begun the long work.

## Five Practices for the Christian Who Wants to Be a Person of Their Word

If you are a Christian in Johannesburg and you want to be a person of your word, here are five practices you can try.

### 1. Keep One Promise This Week

Keep one promise this week. Just one. The small one you almost forgot. The small one that does not seem to matter. Keep it. Tell the person you kept it. The Christian who can keep one small promise is the Christian who can keep the next one.

### 2. Admit One Thing You Got Wrong

Admit one thing you got wrong this week. To the colleague. To the family. To the friend. The admission does not have to be big. It does have to be honest. The Christian who can admit one thing is in a different position than the Christian who cannot.

### 3. Stop the Exaggeration

Stop the exaggeration. The small exaggerations that have crept into the conversation. *I waited for an hour. I told them a thousand times. I am starving. I am dying.* The Christian who stops the exaggeration is practising the truth. The Christian who stops the exaggeration has begun the long work of being believed.

### 4. Read Proverbs 12 Slowly

Read Proverbs 12 once this week. One chapter. Thirty-one verses. Read it for the words about lying, truth, faithfulness, and trustworthiness. The Bible has a great deal to say about these virtues. The Christian who reads Proverbs 12 once is the Christian who has been warned.

### 5. Find One Person to Be Honest With

Find one person to be honest with. Not the whole family. Not the whole office. One person. Tell them the truth about one thing. The Christian who has one honest relationship is in a different position than the Christian who has none. The Christian who has one honest relationship has begun the long work of being a person of truth.
