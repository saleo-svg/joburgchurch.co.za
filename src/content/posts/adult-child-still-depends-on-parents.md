# When the Twenty-Five-Year-Old Still Calls for Money: A South African Christian's Guide to the Adult Child Who Has Not Left and the Parents Who Cannot Say No

## The Scene

The man in Roodepoort paid his daughter's rent again this month. She is twenty-six. She works. She chooses to spend her salary on experiences rather than rent. He is paying R9,500 a month and has been doing so for three years. His wife is frustrated. He is tired. The daughter does not understand why he is making such a big deal about it.

**James 1:5** — *"If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given to you."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Adult Children and Parents

Proverbs 13:22 says: 'A good person leaves an inheritance to their children's children.' The inheritance is meant to go to the children — not to fund the children's lifestyle indefinitely. The parent who gives money to an adult child who could be earning it themselves is not leaving an inheritance. They are enabling dependency.

## The Difference Between Help and Enablement

Help is giving someone what they cannot provide for themselves. Enablement is giving someone what they could provide for themselves but choose not to. The line between them is often uncomfortable. The parent who enables an adult child to remain financially dependent is not helping that child to grow. They are keeping that child in a child-like state.

## The Specific Situation in South Africa

The South African context complicates this. High youth unemployment means that many adult children genuinely cannot support themselves. The parent who has the money and does not help is not being wise either. The question is not 'should I help?' but 'how do I help in a way that builds up rather than keeps down?'

## Practical Steps for the Parent Who Wants to Help Wisely

First: have an honest conversation about the timeline. How long will this continue? Second: charge fair market rent if they are living at home. Third: give money as a loan, not a gift, where appropriate. Fourth: require accountability — not control, but transparency. Fifth: pray about when to stop and be prepared for the difficult conversation.

## A Prayer for the Parent and the Adult Child

Father, we bring the parent who does not know where the giving should stop. We bring the adult child who has not grown up. We ask for the wisdom to distinguish between help and enablement. We ask for the courage to have the difficult conversation. We ask for the grace to set boundaries with love. We ask for the adult child to grow into full maturity. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
