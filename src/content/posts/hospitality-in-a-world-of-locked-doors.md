# Opening Your Home in Johannesburg: A South African Christian's Practical Guide to Hospitality in the Age of the Electric Fence

## The Scene

The woman in Sandton wants to invite people for dinner. She has a beautiful home. She also has an alarm system, electric fencing, and a security gate. The logistics of hospitality have become complicated. She has not had anyone for dinner in two years.

**1 Corinthians 13:4-7** — *"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It does not dishonour others, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Hospitality

Romans 12:13 says: 'Share with the Lord's people who are in need. Practise hospitality.' Hebrews 13:2 says: 'Do not forget to show hospitality to strangers, for by so doing some people have shown hospitality to angels without knowing it.' 1 Peter 4:9 says: 'Offer hospitality to one another without grumbling.' The Bible's call to hospitality is not conditional on whether it is convenient. It is a command that assumes the Christian will find a way.

## Why South African Christians Have Stopped Practising Hospitality

The reasons are practical: the cost, the security risk, the time, the effort, the fear. These reasons are all legitimate. The Christian who chooses not to have people over is not sinning. But the Christian who has abandoned hospitality entirely is missing something that the New Testament considers essential to Christian identity.

## Hospitality Does Not Have to Be Dinner

Hospitality in Johannesburg can take many forms: the coffee that takes twenty minutes in the garden rather than the four-course dinner. The Sunday braai where the family brings their own meat. The playdate where the children are invited but the parents stay. The small group that meets in the home weekly. The key is not the quality of the food. It is the quality of the welcome.

## Practical Hospitality for the Busy Christian

First: start small. Coffee with one person, not dinner for twelve. Second: make it regular — a standing invitation is more impactful than a one-time event. Third: involve the children. Having other children in the home is one of the most natural forms of hospitality. Fourth: do not wait until the house is perfect. Hospitality is about the person, not the décor.

## A Prayer Before the Next Invitation

Father, we bring the closed door of the Johannesburg home. We bring the fear that makes us lock it tighter. We ask for the courage to invite someone in — not to a performance, but to our home as it is. We ask for the grace to be a host rather than a entertainer. We ask for the community that is built over the dining table, the braai, and the garden. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
