# The Christian From Zimbabwe, Malawi, and Mozambique in Johannesburg: A Bible Study on the Stranger in the City of Gold

## The Scene

The man from Harare has been working in Johannesburg for six years. He sends money home every month. He has not seen his children in eight months. He attends the Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore. He is the most reliable member — always on time, always with his Bible, always with a word of encouragement. He has no valid visa. He is afraid every time the police stop him.

**Mark 10:45** — *"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About the Stranger

Exodus 22:21 says: 'Do not mistreat or oppress a foreigner, for you were foreigners in Egypt.' This command is repeated multiple times in the Old Testament. Leviticus 19:34 says: 'The foreigner residing among you must be treated as your native-born. Love them as yourself.' The biblical command to the South African Christian is unambiguous. The stranger is to be loved as yourself.

## The Tension Between Law and Mercy

Romans 13:1 says Christians should be subject to governing authorities. This includes immigration law. But it is worth noting that the same Bible that commands submission to government also commands the church to care for the foreigner. These two commands exist in tension. The church that does not acknowledge this tension is being dishonest.

## Practical Church Responses to Foreign Christians

First: welcome without conditions. The foreign Christian should feel at home in the church. Second: provide practical support — language help, job networks, advice on legal status. Third: do not make assumptions about the foreign Christian's situation. Fourth: advocate for just treatment — the church should speak against xenophobic attitudes in the community.

## A Word to the Foreign Christian Who Is Struggling

To the Christian from Harare, Lilongwe, or Maputo who is alone and afraid in Johannesburg: the Bible says you are not a stranger. You are a fellow citizen with the saints and a member of God's household (Ephesians 2:19). The papers may say you do not belong here. The Bible says you do. Come to the Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore. You belong.

## A Prayer for the Foreign Christian in Johannesburg

Father, we bring the stranger in our city. We bring the Christian who is far from home and afraid. We bring the family that is divided by borders. We ask for your protection over the vulnerable. We ask for the church to be a place of genuine welcome. We ask for justice in the systems that govern migration. We ask for the faith to believe that you are present in every country, every language, and every struggle. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
