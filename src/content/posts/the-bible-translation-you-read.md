# Which Bible Translation Should You Read? A South African Christian's Practical Guide to Choosing a Bible You Will Actually Read

## The Scene

The woman in Randburg bought a Bible at a Christian conference. It is beautiful. It is hard to read. The language is archaic. She reads two pages a day because it is a struggle. She has been reading it for six months. She is still in Genesis.

**Hebrews 11:1** — *"Now faith is confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Main Categories of Translation

Bible translations fall along a spectrum from 'formal equivalence' (word-for-word, often more difficult to read) to 'dynamic equivalence' (thought-for-thought, often easier to read). Examples of formal-equivalence translations: King James Version, English Standard Version. Examples of dynamic-equivalence translations: New International Version, New Revised Standard Version. The best translation is the one you will actually read.

## South Africa's Multilingual Bible Reality

Many Christians in Johannesburg are multilingual. They may read the Bible best in English, Zulu, Xhosa, Sotho, Afrikaans, or another language. The Bible in the mother tongue is the best Bible. If your mother tongue is Zulu, the Zulu Bible is your primary Bible. The English Bible can supplement it.

## A Word About the Apocrypha and Different Editions

Some Bibles include the Apocrypha — books that are in some Catholic and Orthodox Bibles but not in most Protestant Bibles. This does not affect the core message of Scripture. Do not be alarmed if you notice these books in a study Bible.

## A Prayer Before the Next Reading

Father, open our eyes to your word. We confess that we have not read it enough. We confess that we have not understood what we have read. Give us the translation that speaks to us. Give us the hunger to keep reading. Give us the community to discuss what we have read. Speak to us through your word, whatever translation we hold in our hands. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
