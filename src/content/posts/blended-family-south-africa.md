# The Blended Family in South Africa: A Christian's Guide to Step-Parenting, Bonus Children, and Making a New Family in God's Story

## The Scene

The couple in Sandton got married eighteen months ago. The wedding was beautiful. The blended family has been harder. The step-children have two sets of parents. The ex-wives do not agree on anything. The husband sometimes feels he has to choose between his new wife and his children. The wife sometimes feels she is last on everyone's list.

**2 Corinthians 12:9** — *"My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## God's Redemptive Love for Complicated Families

The Bible is full of complicated families. Abraham had children with two women. Jacob had twelve sons from four women. David had a family that descended into murder and incest. The pattern is consistent: God works redemptively through imperfect families. The blended family in Johannesburg is not a second-rate family. It is a family that has been given a second chance.

## The Spouse Comes First — But Not Always

Ephesians 5:33 says the husband must love his wife as he loves himself, and the wife must respect her husband. In a first marriage, this command is clear. In a blended family, it becomes complicated. The husband who has children from a previous marriage has divided loyalties. The Bible's wisdom is: the marriage is the foundation. If the marriage fails, the family fails. But the marriage must be built in a way that does not emotionally abandon the children.

## The Most Important Rule for Step-Parents

The most important rule for the step-parent is: do not try to replace the other biological parent. The child who is pushed to replace the absent parent with the step-parent will usually resist. The step-parent who supplements the biological parent's role — loving, nurturing, providing — without trying to replace, is doing it right.

## A Prayer for the Blended Family

Father, we bring the blended family that is complicated. We bring the step-parent who feels like an outsider. We bring the child who is navigating two homes. We bring the couple whose marriage is under unusual pressure. We ask for the grace to build a new family in your story. We ask for the wisdom to love without replacing. We ask for the patience to let the family grow at its own pace. We ask for the commitment that holds it together. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
