# The Christian in the Coffee Shop: A South African's Guide to Writing in Public, Working in Public, and the Quiet Witness of Showing Up

## The Scene

The man in Parkmore has been working from a coffee shop three days a week for two years. The barista knows his order. The other regulars know his face. No one knows his name. He writes in public, prays in public, and wonders if the work he is doing has any meaning.

**2 Corinthians 12:9** — *"My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Bible on the Public Square

Acts 17:17 says: 'As was his custom, Paul went into the synagogue, and on three Sabbath days he reasoned with them from the Scriptures.' The apostle Paul worked in public places. He reasoned in synagogues, in the market, in the Areopagus. The Christian does not have to retreat from public spaces to be faithful.

## The Witness of the Quietly Working Christian

After ten years of Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore, we have learned that the quietly working Christian is often the most powerful witness. The barista who is treated with kindness. The stranger who is greeted. The long black coffee that is enjoyed slowly rather than in a rush. These are small acts of presence that reflect a different way of being in the world.

## Solitude in the Midst of Crowds

The coffee shop working Christian faces a specific challenge: the illusion of community without the reality. The other regulars are not friends. The conversations are transactional. The Christian who is honest about this is invited to build real community outside the coffee shop, not in it.

## A Prayer for the Coffee Shop Worker

Father, we bring the work that is done in public. We bring the laptop and the headphones and the long black coffee. We ask for the work to matter. We ask for the presence to be a witness. Give us the real community to sustain the work. Give us the solitude that is healthy, not lonely. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
