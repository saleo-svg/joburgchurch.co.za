# Blessing the Animals: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Pets, Creation Care, and What the Bible Says About the Dog That Is Part of the Family

## The Scene

The woman in Randburg buried her dog under the jacaranda tree in the garden. She was not sure whether she should have a funeral service or not. She is a Christian. She goes to church. She wonders if her faith has anything to say about the dog she buried.

**Genesis 1:31** — *"God saw all that he had made, and it was very good."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Animals

Genesis 1:26 says: 'Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness, and let them rule over the fish of the sea and the birds of the air, over the livestock, over all the earth.' Human beings are given stewardship over the animals. This is not ownership without responsibility. The steward is accountable to God for how the animals are treated.

## The Christian Case for Having Pets

The Bible does not forbid pets. The Bible does not mandate them. For the Christian who has a pet, the pet is an opportunity to practice stewardship, to receive unconditional love, and to participate in the joy of creation. Proverbs 12:10 says: 'The righteous care for the needs of their animals.'

## When the Pet Dies

The grief of losing a pet is real grief. The Christian is not being sentimental or unspiritual when they grieve a pet. They are grieving a creature that God made and that brought joy into their life. The church should acknowledge this grief without minimising it.

## A Prayer for the Family Pet

Father, we bring the pet that is a member of the family. We bring the dog that waits by the gate. We bring the cat that sleeps on the bed. We bring the bird that sings in the morning. Thank you for the animals you have given us. Thank you for the responsibility of caring for them. Thank you for the joy they bring. Hold them and us in your care. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
