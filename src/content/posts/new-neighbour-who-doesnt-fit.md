# The New Neighbour Who Doesn't Look Like You: A South African Christian's Guide to Diversity, Welcome, and What Happens When the Family Next Door Is Different

## The Scene

The family in Fourways has lived in the same house for twenty years. The neighbours have always been like them. Then a family moved in from a different ethnic and linguistic background. The old neighbours do not know how to welcome them. They are polite but distant.

**Mark 10:45** — *"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Neighbourhood Diversity

Revelation 7:9-10 describes the vision of heaven: 'Before me stood a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, tribe, people and language.' Heaven is diverse. The neighbourhood that reflects this diversity is a small picture of what heaven looks like.

## The South African History That Makes This Hard

South Africa's apartheid history has left neighbourhoods that are still largely segregated by race and language. The Christian who chooses to live in a neighbourhood that is diverse, or who finds themselves in one, is swimming against the current of seventy years of deliberate separation. This is hard. It is also faithful.

## Practical Steps for the Diverse Neighbourhood

First: introduce yourself first — do not wait for the new neighbour to come to you. Second: learn a greeting in their language. Third: invite them to something — a braai, a cup of tea, the neighbourhood watch meeting. Fourth: do not assume — ask questions rather than making assumptions about their culture or religion.

## A Prayer for the New Neighbour

Father, we bring the family that has moved in and does not feel welcome. We bring the neighbours who do not know how to welcome them. We ask for the courage to cross the difference. We ask for the curiosity that asks questions rather than assumes answers. We ask for the neighbourhood that reflects your kingdom — every nation, tribe, people, and language. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
