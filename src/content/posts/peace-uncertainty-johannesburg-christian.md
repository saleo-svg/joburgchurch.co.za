## The Small and Large Uncertainties of Johannesburg

There is a particular kind of alert that lives in the body of every Christian in Johannesburg. It is not panic. It is not fear. It is a low-grade attentiveness to the city, a quiet scanning of the environment, a posture that has been trained by years of living in a place where the news can change in an afternoon.

You check the load-shedding schedule before you make the coffee. You check the traffic before you get in the car. You check the news before you open the WhatsApp group. You check the children before they leave the house. You check the locks before you sleep. You check the family in another province. You check the price of petrol, the price of bread, the price of electricity, the price of staying in a country that you love and that exhausts you at the same time.

Johannesburg is a city of small uncertainties that add up to a large weight. The Christian in Johannesburg carries that weight. The Christian in Johannesburg also carries a Bible. The Bible has a great deal to say about peace. The Bible has a great deal to say about anxiety. The Bible has a great deal to say about the difference between the peace the world offers and the peace Jesus offers.

This post is for the Christian in Johannesburg who is tired. It is for the Christian in Johannesburg who is doing the small checking every morning and the larger checking every evening. It is for the Christian who wants to know what the Bible has to say about the weight, and what the Christian is called to do with it.

## What the Bible Actually Says About Anxiety

The Bible does not treat anxiety as a sign of weak faith. The Bible treats anxiety as something the Christian is called to bring to God.

**Philippians 4:6-7** — *Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.*

**Matthew 6:25-26** — *Therefore I tell you, do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink; or about your body, what you will wear. Is not life more than food, and the body more than clothes? Look at the birds of the air; they do not sow or reap or store away in barns, and yet your heavenly Father feeds them. Are you not much more valuable than they?*

**Matthew 6:31-34** — *So do not worry, saying, 'What shall we eat?' or 'What shall we drink?' or 'What shall we wear?' For the pagans run after all these things, and your heavenly Father knows that you need them. But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as well. Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own.*

**1 Peter 5:7** — *Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you.*

**John 14:27** — *Peace I leave with you; my peace I give you. I do not give to you as the world gives. Do not let your hearts be troubled and do not be afraid.*

**Isaiah 26:3** — *You will keep in perfect peace those whose minds are steadfast, because they trust in you.*

**Psalm 55:22** — *Cast your cares on the Lord and he will sustain you; he will never let the righteous be shaken.*

**Romans 8:28** — *And we know that in all things God works for the good of those who love him, who have been called according to his purpose.*

**Romans 8:31** — *What, then, shall we say in response to these things? If God is for us, who can be against us?*

What you notice is that the Bible does not promise the Christian an easier city. The Bible does not promise the Christian an easier news cycle. The Bible does not promise the Christian an easier week. The Bible promises the Christian that the weight can be carried differently. The Bible promises the Christian that the weight can be cast onto a God who is able to hold it. The Bible promises the Christian that the peace that comes from God is different from the peace that comes from the world — different in source, different in stability, different in what it depends on.

## The Difference Between the Peace of the World and the Peace of Jesus

The second thing the Bible has a great deal to say about is the difference between two kinds of peace.

The world offers peace as the absence of trouble. The world offers peace as the right job, the right relationship, the right suburb, the right news cycle, the right time. The peace that the world offers is contingent. It depends on the news. It depends on the load-shedding schedule. It depends on the colleague, the boss, the relative, the economy, the exchange rate. The peace that the world offers is fragile in the way that a sandcastle is fragile. It can be there in the morning and gone by the afternoon.

Jesus offers a different kind of peace. Jesus says, in John 14:27, *'Peace I leave with you; my peace I give you. I do not give to you as the world gives.'* The peace that Jesus offers is not contingent on the news. It is not contingent on the load-shedding schedule. It is not contingent on the colleague, the boss, the relative, the economy, the exchange rate. The peace that Jesus offers is contingent only on the character of God. It is contingent on the cross. It is contingent on the resurrection. It is contingent on the promise that the God who holds the world is holding the Christian too.

The Christian in Johannesburg who has discovered the difference between these two kinds of peace has begun the long work of the gospel. The Christian who has discovered that the peace of Jesus is not contingent on the news has begun to live the way the Bible asks the Christian to live.

## Why the Christian in Johannesburg Struggles with This

The Christian in Johannesburg struggles with this for three reasons.

### 1. The News Is Loud

The news in South Africa is loud. It is loud in the morning. It is loud in the afternoon. It is loud in the evening. It is loud on the radio, on the television, on the phone, on the WhatsApp group. The Christian who tries to live in the peace of Jesus has to learn to live with a news cycle that is actively arguing against the peace.

The Bible is honest about this. The Psalmist wrote psalms about the loud voices of the world.

**Psalm 2:1-2** — *Why do the nations conspire and the peoples plot in vain? The kings of the earth rise up and the rulers band together against the Lord and against his anointed.*

**Psalm 46:1-3** — *God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble. Therefore we will not fear, though the earth give way and the mountains fall into the heart of the sea, though its waters roar and foam and the mountains quake with their surging.*

**Psalm 46:10** — *He says, 'Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth.'*

The Christian in Johannesburg is called to a kind of stillness that the news cycle is actively arguing against. The Christian who can practise the stillness of Psalm 46 in the middle of the Johannesburg news cycle has begun the long work of the gospel.

### 2. The Losses Are Real

The losses in Johannesburg are real. The Christian in Johannesburg does not just read about the crime. The Christian in Johannesburg hears about it from the cousin. The Christian in Johannesburg knows someone who has been hijacked. The Christian in Johannesburg knows someone who has been robbed. The Christian in Johannesburg knows the family that has lost someone to violence. The peace of Jesus is not pretend peace. The peace of Jesus is peace in the middle of the real losses.

The Bible is honest about this too. The Psalms are full of real loss. Job is full of real loss. Lamentations is full of real loss. The Christian who can hold the peace of Jesus in one hand and the real loss in the other has begun to live the way the Bible asks the Christian to live.

**John 16:33** — *I have told you these things, so that in me you may have peace. In this world you will have trouble. But take heart! I have overcome the world.*

The Christian in Johannesburg does not have to pretend that the trouble is not there. The Christian in Johannesburg is asked to take heart because the trouble is not the last word.

### 3. The Christian Often Carries the Burden Alone

The Christian in Johannesburg often carries the weight alone. The Christian does not always have a community that knows the weight. The Christian does not always have a family that understands the weight. The Christian does not always have a colleague who knows what it is to be a Christian in Johannesburg in this particular season.

The Bible is honest about this too. The Bible does not promise the Christian that the carrying will be alone. The Bible promises the Christian that the carrying can be shared.

**Galatians 6:2** — *Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfil the law of Christ.*

**Galatians 6:5** — *for each one should carry their own load.*

Both are true. The Christian carries their own load. The Christian also helps to carry the load of the brother and the sister. The Christian who has a community that carries the weight is in a different position than the Christian who carries alone.

## What the Sandton Bible Study Has Tried to Do

In the last few years, the Wednesday evening Bible study has tried to take peace seriously. Here is what we have done, for what it is worth.

### We Have Done a Psalm 46 Wednesday

We have set aside one Wednesday a year to read Psalm 46 in full. We have read it slowly. We have stopped in the middle to let the room breathe. We have asked the room to name the loud voices that are pressing in. We have asked the room to listen for the still small voice underneath.

### We Have Taught Each Other to Pray the News

We have taught each other to pray the news. Not to read the news first and then pray. To pray the news. To bring the load-shedding schedule to God before we bring it to Eskom. To bring the road to God before we bring it to the M1. To bring the loss to God before we bring it to the WhatsApp group. The Christian who can pray the news has begun to put the news in its place.

### We Have Done a Philippians 4 Series

We have done four Wednesdays in a row on Philippians 4. The verses about anxiety. The verses about prayer. The verses about the peace that transcends all understanding. We have asked the room to name the things they are anxious about. We have asked the room to pray about those things specifically. We have asked the room to come back the next Wednesday and tell the room what happened.

### We Have Practised Stillness

We have started the Wednesday evening with three minutes of silence. Not lecturing silence. Not praying silence. Just silence. The Christian in Johannesburg does not have many places to be still. The Wednesday evening has become one of them. The Christian who has three minutes of stillness in the middle of the week is in a different position than the Christian who has none.

## Five Practices for the Christian Who Wants Peace in Johannesburg

If you are a Christian in Johannesburg and you want to begin living in the peace of Jesus, here are five practices you can try.

### 1. Pray the News

Pray the news before you read the news. Take the news story to God before you take it to your heart. The Christian who prays the news is putting the news in its place.

### 2. Read Philippians 4 Slowly

Read Philippians 4:6-7 once a day for a week. Memorise it if you can. The Christian who has the verse in the heart has the verse in the difficult moment.

### 3. Find One Person to Be Still With

Find one person to be still with. Not to talk with. To be still with. The Christian who has one person to be still with has begun the long work of shared peace.

### 4. Cast One Specific Anxiety

Cast one specific anxiety onto God this week. Name it. Write it down. Pray over it. Let the Christian who can name the anxiety be the Christian who can cast it.

### 5. Show Up on a Wednesday

Show up on a Wednesday evening. Even if you are tired. Even if you are anxious. Even if you are not sure what you believe right now. The Christian who shows up on a Wednesday in Sandton has begun the long work of peace. The Wednesday evening is a small island of stillness in the middle of the city. The Christian who finds the island has begun to find the peace.
