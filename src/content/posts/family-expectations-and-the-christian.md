# When Your Family Expects Too Much: A South African Christian's Guide to Family Obligation, Cultural Duty, and the Freedom of the Gospel

## The Scene

The woman in Randburg sends money home every month. She has done this since she started working. Her mother expects it. Her siblings expect it. Her uncle's children expect it. She earns R22,000 a month. After the sent money, the rent, and the school fees, she has almost nothing left. She cannot say no.

**James 4:6** — *"God opposes the proud but shows favour to the humble."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Family and Discipleship

Luke 14:26 says: 'If anyone comes to me and does not hate father and mother, wife and children, brothers and sisters — yes, even their own life — such a person cannot be my disciple.' This is a hard verse. Jesus is not saying we should hate our families. Jesus is saying that the demands of discipleship must take priority over family obligations when they conflict. The disciple of Jesus is ultimately responsible to God, not to the extended family's expectations.

## The Difference Between Duty and Discipleship

Duty says: I must do this because the family expects it. Discipleship says: I must consider what God is asking me to do, which may or may not align with what the family expects. These two things often align. When they conflict, the Christian is invited to follow Christ, not the family system.

## Saying No Without Losing the Relationship

Saying no to family obligations does not mean losing the relationship. It means setting a boundary that protects the health of the person saying no and, often, the long-term health of the relationship. The family that respects a boundary is healthier than the family that enables exploitation.

## A Prayer for the Person Trapped by Obligation

Father, we bring the obligation that has become a trap. We bring the guilt of saying no. We bring the family that does not understand boundaries. Give us the wisdom to know when to say yes and when to say no. Give us the courage to set boundaries without losing love. Give us the freedom that the gospel offers — the freedom to serve you rather than every human expectation. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
