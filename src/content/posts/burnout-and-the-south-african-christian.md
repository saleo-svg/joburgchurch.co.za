# When the Ministry Worker Burns Out: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Exhaustion, Collapse, and Starting Again

## The Scene

The woman in Randburg has been leading the women's ministry for seven years. She sent the email last week. She is stepping down. The church leadership did not understand. They asked her to reconsider. She does not have anything left to reconsider. She is empty.

**Isaiah 40:31** — *"But those who hope in the Lord will renew their strength. They will soar on wings like eagles; they will run and not grow weary, they will walk and not be faint."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Biblical Story of Elijah's Burnout

1 Kings 19 tells the story of Elijah after his greatest victory — the showdown with the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel. He ran. He collapsed under a broom tree. He asked God to take his life. This is one of the most honest stories in the Bible about burnout. Elijah was not a failure. He was a prophet of God who had given everything. And God did not condemn him for collapsing. God gave him rest, food, and a new assignment.

## What the Bible Says About Sabbath Rest

Exodus 20:8-11 establishes the Sabbath as a creation ordinance — it is not just for Israel, it is built into the fabric of human life. The Christian who does not rest will break. This is not spiritual advice. It is biological reality. The body needs sleep. The soul needs silence. The relationships need time. The minister who does not rest is not more spiritual. They are more broken.

## The Church's Responsibility to the Burned-Out Minister

Galatians 6:2 says: 'Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfil the law of Christ.' The church community has a responsibility to the burned-out volunteer. This means: not guilt-tripping them for stepping down, not immediately finding their replacement, and checking in on them in the months after.

## Practical Steps for Recovery

First: stop. Actually stop. Do not just change the type of ministry. Second: see a doctor. Burnout has physical symptoms that a GP can help with. Third: get honest about the boundaries you have not been maintaining. Fourth: find one person who will hold you accountable to rest. Fifth: when you are ready, start again slowly — with clearer boundaries.

## A Prayer for the Burned-Out Minister

Father, we bring the exhaustion that has no more to give. We bring the minister who has emptied themselves for others. We bring the volunteer who has run dry. We ask for the rest that Elijah received under the broom tree. We ask for the food and the sleep. We ask for the community to carry what you have been carrying alone. We ask for the grace to stop without guilt. We ask for the future that is not just the past again. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
