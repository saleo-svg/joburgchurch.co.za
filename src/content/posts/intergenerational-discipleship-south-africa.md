## The Question Every South African Christian Grandparent Asks

There is a question that almost every Christian grandparent in Johannesburg carries. It is the question the grandparent asks at the kitchen table when the grandchild is on the iPad in the next room. It is the question the grandparent asks at the school concert while watching the grandchild perform a song the grandparent has never heard. It is the question the grandparent asks on the drive home from the family lunch while the grandchild plays the music the grandparent does not understand.

The question is: *How do I pass my faith to a generation that does not seem to need it from me?*

The question is real. The question is honest. The question is shared by the parents in the middle, who are trying to parent in a world they did not grow up in, and by the young adults who are trying to build a faith that does not feel like a hand-me-down.

South African culture has always been more intergenerational than most other cultures. The Ubuntu principle — *I am because we are* — assumes that the older and the younger are part of the same thing. The household often includes the grandparent in a way that is rarer in the Global North. The Sunday lunch is still the multigenerational gathering in many homes. The Bible study that meets on a Wednesday evening in Sandton includes grandmothers in their seventies and young adults in their twenties and teenagers in their middle years — all sitting around the same table, listening to the same passage.

This post is for the Christian grandparent, parent, and young adult who wants to do the intergenerational work the Bible way.

## What the Bible Actually Says About the Older and the Younger

The Bible has a great deal to say about the relationship between the older and the younger Christian. Most of it is in passages Christians have stopped reading.

**Titus 2:1-8** — *You, however, must teach what is appropriate to sound doctrine. Teach the older men to be temperate, worthy of respect, self-controlled, and sound in faith, in love and in endurance. Likewise, teach the older women to be reverent in the way they live, not to be slanderers or addicted to much wine, but to teach what is good. Then they can urge the younger women to love their husbands and children, to be self-controlled and pure, to be busy at home, to be kind, and to be subject to their husbands, so that no one will malign the word of God. Similarly, teach the younger men to be self-controlled. In everything set them an example by doing what is good. In your teaching show integrity, seriousness and soundness of speech that cannot be condemned, so that those who oppose you may be ashamed because they have nothing bad to say about us.*

**Titus 2:3-5 (paraphrased for clarity)** — *The older women are to teach the younger women. The older men are to be examples to the younger men.*

**2 Timothy 2:2** — *And the things you have heard me say in the presence of many witnesses entrust to reliable people who will also be qualified to teach others.*

**Deuteronomy 6:1-9** — *These are the commands, decrees and laws the Lord your God directed me to teach you to observe in the land that you are crossing the Jordan to possess, so that you, your children and their children after them may fear the Lord your God as long as you live... Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up.*

**Psalm 71:18** — *Even when I am old and grey, do not forsake me, my God, till I declare your power to the next generation, your mighty acts to all who are to come.*

**1 Timothy 5:1-2** — *Do not rebuke an older man harshly, but exhort him as if he were your father. Treat younger men as brothers, older women as mothers, and younger women as sisters, with absolute purity.*

**1 Peter 5:5** — *In the same way, you younger men, submit yourselves to your elders. All of you, clothe yourselves with humility toward one another.*

**Proverbs 20:29** — *The glory of young men is their strength, grey hair the splendour of the righteous.*

What you notice in these passages is that the Bible does not present the older and the younger as separate ministries. The Bible presents them as one body that needs the other. The older need the younger because the older need to pass on what has been given to them. The younger need the older because the younger need what the older have learned. The Bible presents the older and the younger in the same room, at the same table, listening to the same passage, asking each other the same questions.

The Bible does not say the older are to be ignored. The Bible does not say the younger are to be irrelevant. The Bible says the older and the younger are to do the slow work of the faith together.

## The Five Roles the Bible Describes

The Bible describes five intergenerational roles. Each role is a calling. Each role is something the Christian is to do.

### Role 1 — The Older Woman Is to Teach the Younger Woman

Titus 2:3-5 is the most specific passage about this role. The older woman is to teach the younger woman. The teaching is not the classroom teaching. The teaching is the kitchen-table teaching. The teaching is the *how to love your husband and children* teaching. The teaching is the *how to be self-controlled and pure* teaching. The teaching is the *how to be busy at home and kind* teaching.

The teaching is practical. The teaching is relational. The teaching is what happens in the home, over the tea, while the children are at school. The teaching is not a programme. The teaching is a friendship. The friendship is the kind that has been going for years. The friendship is the kind where the younger woman can ask the older woman the question she would not ask her own mother.

### Role 2 — The Older Man Is to Be the Example to the Younger Man

Titus 2:6-8. The older man is not to lecture the younger man. The older man is to be the example. The younger man is to watch the older man. The younger man is to watch how the older man treats his wife. The younger man is to watch how the older man handles the money. The younger man is to watch how the older man handles the criticism. The younger man is to watch how the older man prays.

The example is more important than the lecture. The example is the thing the younger man remembers ten years later. The lecture is forgotten. The example is the thing that shapes.

### Role 3 — The Older Christian Is to Declare the Power of God to the Next Generation

Psalm 71:18. The older Christian is the one who has seen the most. The older Christian is the one who has lived through the most. The older Christian is the one who can testify to the faithfulness of God across the decades. The younger Christian needs this testimony. The younger Christian needs to hear about the God who was faithful in 1985. The younger Christian needs to hear about the God who was faithful in 1994. The younger Christian needs to hear about the God who was faithful in 2008. The testimony is the inheritance.

### Role 4 — The Younger Christian Is to Submit to the Older Christian

1 Peter 5:5. The submission is not the submission of inferiority. The submission is the submission of the disciple to the one who has gone ahead. The submission is the respect for the journey. The submission is the recognition that the older Christian has walked the road the younger Christian is just beginning. The submission is the posture that allows the older Christian's wisdom to land.

### Role 5 — The Older and Younger Are to Honour Each Other as Family

1 Timothy 5:1-2. The older man is to be honoured as a father. The older woman is to be honoured as a mother. The younger man is to be honoured as a brother. The younger woman is to be honoured as a sister. The honouring is the family. The honouring is what makes the intergenerational thing work. The honouring is what the Bible calls *family*.

## What the South African Intergenerational Bible Study Looks Like in Practice

These are the practices that have been developed in our Wednesday Bible study in Sandton over the years. The practices are not inventions. The practices are applications of Titus 2 and the related passages.

### Practice 1 — The Shared Table

The Wednesday Bible study is held at a table that can seat twelve. The table is rarely empty. The table regularly includes the grandmother in her seventies and the university student in his early twenties. The table regularly includes the retired teacher and the young professional. The table regularly includes the married couple and the single adult. The shared table is the intergenerational thing. The shared table is what Titus 2 assumes. The shared table is what the Christian home is supposed to look like.

### Practice 2 — The Reading of the Same Passage

The same passage is read out loud by whoever volunteers. The grandmother reads. The university student reads. The young professional reads. The retired teacher reads. The passage is read in the same room. The passage is read at the same pace. The passage is the same passage for everyone. The reading is what Deuteronomy 6 assumes. The reading is the *talking about it when you sit at home*.

### Practice 3 — The Two Questions the Older Ask and the Younger Ask

After the reading, the older Christian asks the first question. The question is usually about the long view. *Where have you seen this passage in your life? What did this passage mean to you when you were young? What do you think this passage will mean to you when you are my age?*

After the older Christian has asked, the younger Christian asks the second question. The question is usually about the immediate. *What does this passage mean for me this week? What does this passage say about the job I am starting? What does this passage say about the relationship I am in? What does this passage say about the decision I am making?*

The two questions together are the intergenerational thing. The long view from the older. The immediate from the younger. The two questions together cover the arc of the Christian life.

### Practice 4 — The Story of the Older Christian

Several times a year, the older Christian in the Bible study is invited to share the testimony. The testimony is not the altar-call testimony. The testimony is the long testimony. The testimony is the story of the older Christian's walk with God across the decades. The testimony includes the failures. The testimony includes the comebacks. The testimony includes the long marriage and the difficult marriage and the marriage that ended. The testimony includes the job that was lost and the job that was found and the job that was built. The testimony is the inheritance.

The younger Christian who hears the testimony receives something the Bible study can never manufacture. The younger Christian receives the long view of the Christian life. The younger Christian receives the testimony that the faith survives the decades. The younger Christian receives the testimony that the faith survives the failures.

### Practice 5 — The Practical Service Between the Generations

The intergenerational Bible study is not only the talk. The intergenerational Bible study is also the practical service. The younger Christian with the car drives the older Christian to the doctor's appointment. The older Christian with the experience helps the younger Christian write the CV. The younger Christian with the energy helps the older Christian move the furniture. The older Christian with the prayer life prays for the younger Christian's job interview. The intergenerational service is the Titus 2 thing in action.

### Practice 6 — The Holiday Together

Once a year, several of the Bible study families go away together for a weekend. The weekend is not the conference. The weekend is the family. The weekend is the grandmother and the university student and the young professional and the retired teacher in the same house for two days. The weekend is the place where the grandmother tells the story of 1985 and the university student tells the story of the TikTok they saw last week. The weekend is the place where the older Christian and the younger Christian see each other as family.

## The Hard Truths About the Intergenerational Christian Community

The intergenerational Christian community is not the easy community. The intergenerational Christian community is the slow community. The intergenerational Christian community is the community that requires the older Christian to be patient with the younger. The intergenerational Christian community is the community that requires the younger Christian to be patient with the older. The intergenerational Christian community is the community that requires both to give up some of their preferences.

Several of the hard truths:

**The hard truth 1 — The older and younger do not always understand each other.** The grandmother does not always understand the young professional's decision to move back home at 28. The young professional does not always understand the grandmother's decision to stay in the marriage for 50 years. The two do not have to agree. The two have to listen. The listening is the Titus 2 thing.

**The hard truth 2 — The intergenerational community takes time.** The intergenerational community is not built in a programme. The intergenerational community is built over years. The intergenerational community is built by the shared table on the Wednesday evening, the holiday once a year, the phone call between, the prayer at the bedside. The intergenerational community is the slowest of the slow obediences.

**The hard truth 3 — The intergenerational community requires the older Christian to admit weakness.** The older Christian has been through things the younger Christian has not yet been through. The older Christian has been through things the younger Christian will go through. The older Christian who is willing to admit the weakness is the older Christian the younger Christian will trust. The older Christian who pretends the weakness is not there is the older Christian the younger Christian will not trust.

**The hard truth 4 — The intergenerational community requires the younger Christian to ask.** The younger Christian who is too proud to ask the older Christian is the younger Christian who will go through the thing alone. The asking is the Titus 2 thing. The asking is the submission to the elder. The asking is what 1 Peter 5:5 looks like in practice.

## A Prayer for the South African Christian Family Across the Generations

If you are the grandparent, the parent, the young adult, the teenager — here is a prayer for the intergenerational thing.

> *God of the generations,*
>
> *We thank you for the grandmother who taught us to pray. We thank you for the grandfather who read us the Bible stories. We thank you for the parent who drove us to the church. We thank you for the older Christian at the Wednesday Bible study who tells us what the faith looked like in 1985.*
>
> *We thank you for the university student who asks the question we have been afraid to ask. We thank you for the young professional who keeps showing up on a Wednesday evening even though the work is hard. We thank you for the teenager who sits at the table even though the conversation is sometimes over their head.*
>
> *We confess that we have separated the generations. We confess that we have built churches for the young and the old separately. We confess that we have not done the slow work of the shared table. We confess that the older Christian has sometimes been ignored. We confess that the younger Christian has sometimes been patronised.*
>
> *Help us to do the slow work. Help us to put the table in the room where both can sit. Help us to read the passage at the pace that both can hear. Help us to ask the long-view question and the immediate-view question. Help us to share the testimony. Help us to drive each other to the appointment. Help us to pray for each other through the night.*
>
> *In the name of the one who was a child in the temple, a young man in the carpenter's shop, a teacher in the synagogue, an older man on the cross.*
> *Amen.*
