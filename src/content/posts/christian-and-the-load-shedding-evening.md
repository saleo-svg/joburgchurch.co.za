# When the Lights Go Out and the Faith Comes On: A South African Christian's Guide to Load-Shedding as an Unexpected Spiritual Discipline

## The Scene

The lights went out at six-thirty. The inverter was on. The family had candles. The mother in Sandton looked at the children around the candlelight table and realised: this is the most present they had been all week.

**2 Corinthians 12:9** — *"My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Darkness and Light

Genesis 1:1-5 says: 'In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Now the earth was formless and empty, darkness was over the surface of the deep... And God said, Let there be light.' Light in the Bible is not just physical illumination. Light is truth, goodness, presence, and revelation. The load-shedding darkening of the home is an interruption of the light. It is also an invitation to notice what else provides light.

## What Load-Shedding Teaches About Dependency

The Christian who has been through a major load-shedding event has been taught something about dependency. The inverter kicks in. The JoJo tank has water. The gas stove works. These preparations are good and wise. But they also teach that the Christian is ultimately dependent on God, not on Eskom, not on the inverter, not on any system. This is a lesson worth learning.

## Load-Shedding as a Family Practice

The family that uses load-shedding as an opportunity for a specific family practice — a card game, a story, a prayer, a sing-along — has turned a curse into a blessing. The family that spends load-shedding on their phones has missed the opportunity.

## A Prayer in the Load-Shedding Darkness

Father, we thank you for load-shedding. We did not expect to say that. But thank you for the darkness that has forced us to stop. Thank you for the candles that remind us that you are the light. Thank you for the family gathered around the table without the screens. Use this darkness to teach us what we have been missing in the light. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
