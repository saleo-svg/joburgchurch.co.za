## The Bible Study That Survived Stage 6

In the last seven years, we have had sixty-three Wednesday evenings cancelled or interrupted by load-shedding. We have had Bible study sessions continue by candlelight, by car battery, by inverter humming in the corner, by phone call after the WiFi died, and once — memorably — by a borrowed generator that belonged to a member's neighbour.

We have learned that load-shedding does not need to kill your Bible study. It just needs a different plan.

This post is the practical guide we wish someone had given us in 2017. It is for anyone in South Africa — Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban, Pretoria, Soweto, Khayelitsha, Mamelodi, anywhere — who runs a small Christian community and is tired of load-shedding deciding whether people can show up.

## First, Some Honest Truth About Load-Shedding and Bible Study

Before the practical tips, here are some honest observations from the years we have spent doing this.

### Load-shedding hits Bible study at the worst possible time

Wednesday evenings. Sunday afternoons. Saturday mornings. These are the times Bible studies and small groups meet in South Africa. They are also the times Eskom seems to enjoy hitting us hardest. The dinner prep slot, when everyone is supposed to be online together, is also the slot when the inverter is screaming and the kids are doing homework by torch.

This is not a coincidence, but it is also not an excuse. If your Bible study cannot survive one stage of load-shedding, it was never going to survive a real crisis either. Treat load-shedding as a free fire drill.

### Some of our best nights happened by candlelight

This is the part nobody tells you when they warn you about Eskom. Some of the most honest Bible study sessions we have ever had happened because the WiFi was out and half the room could not log in. The half that could — usually five or six people from the same suburb — ended up on a WhatsApp voice note, then a phone call, then in someone's living room with candles. The conversations that followed were quieter, more honest, and closer than our biggest online Wednesday had ever been.

Load-shedding, in other words, sometimes forces the kind of intimacy our modern Bible studies have been quietly trying to avoid.

### The people most affected by load-shedding are usually not the people with the biggest inverters

This is worth saying out loud. The Sandton professional with the solar setup and the R80,000 inverter does not experience load-shedding the same way as the domestic worker in Soweto who has lost three hours of wages because her employer could not run the house. The Bible study community that pretends everyone is equally affected by load-shedding is not seeing clearly.

Plan for the people with the least backup, not the most.

## Practical Adaptations for Your Bible Study

Here are the eleven adaptations we have made over the years. They are listed in order of cost — the first three are free.

### 1. Move the Meeting Online

If you have not already done this, do it now. Online Bible study is not a compromise. For a South Africa that lives with load-shedding, it is the only way to make a midweek Bible study sustainable.

The platform matters less than you think. We use Google Meet because it is free, it works on most phones, and it does not require anyone to install special software. Zoom works too. WhatsApp video calls work if you have five people. The point is to be on a platform your community can actually use when the WiFi is out.

### 2. Set Up a WhatsApp Voice-Note Backup Plan

When the video call dies, move to voice notes. This is what we do. Whoever has signal first sends a voice note summarising the discussion so far. Then people reply with their own voice notes. Then someone with a stronger signal transcribes the key points and sends them as text.

It is messier than a Google Meet. It also has the unexpected benefit of being more inclusive. People who are shy on video will send a voice note. People who do not have data for a video call will read text.

Set up a dedicated WhatsApp group for the Bible study. Do not mix it with your social WhatsApp group. People need to know that when they join the Bible study group, the focus is on the Bible study.

### 3. Choose a Consistent Time You Can Defend

If you move the meeting around every time load-shedding hits, you will lose people. Pick a time, hold it, and tell people the time. Wednesday 7:30pm is what works for us. Most of our regulars have it in their calendar as a recurring event.

Whatever you choose, defend it. The day will come when the meeting is hard and small and inconvenient. Hold it anyway.

### 4. Have a Phone-Torch-and-Paper Backup

For in-person meetings, every Bible study room should have:
- A few candles (real ones, not battery-operated — battery-operated ones die too)
- Matches or a lighter
- A printed copy of the passage for the week
- A bottle of water and a few rusks

If your meeting is in someone's home, ask the host to put a candle and a box of matches in the Bible study room. If your meeting is in a church hall, keep a load-shedding kit in the storeroom. The kit does not need to be fancy. It needs to be ready.

### 5. Use a Low-Energy Inverter, Not a Generator

If your community can afford it, a small 1kVA inverter is enough to keep a WiFi router, a laptop, and a few lights running for two to three hours. Cost in 2026: around R3,500 to R5,500 for a basic unit. Used: half that.

A generator is louder, more expensive to run, and more polluting. The inverter is the right tool for an indoor Bible study.

If you cannot afford an inverter for the whole community, ask one or two of your regulars if they have a battery station (the kind you take camping) that you can borrow for the meeting. Most people with a battery station are happy to lend it for a Bible study.

### 6. Plan the Internet Backup in Advance

We use three layers of internet backup:
- Primary: home WiFi (most reliable for video calls)
- Secondary: mobile data on the lead's phone (hotspot for the rest of the room)
- Tertiary: WhatsApp voice notes (works on the weakest signal)

The lead of the Bible study should have a mobile data bundle of at least 10GB a month dedicated to the meeting. This is a small cost. It is the single biggest reason our Wednesday evening has survived seven years of load-shedding.

### 7. Build a Car-Battery Routine for Longer Meetings

For our Saturday in-person evenings in Parkmore, we sometimes run for three hours. That is too long for most inverters. We have a small routine: a member brings a portable car battery with an inverter clip. The lead's laptop runs off it. The WiFi router runs off it. The candles and torches do the rest.

This is not elegant. It works.

### 8. Tell People the Load-Shedding Schedule Before Each Meeting

EskomSePush is the app every South African has on their phone. If you do not have it, install it. The Bible study lead should check the load-shedding schedule on Monday or Tuesday and send a quick message to the group:

*"Wednesday 7:30pm — Stage 4 from 6pm to 8:30pm in our area. We will start on Google Meet. If the video drops, we move to WhatsApp voice notes. The passage for tomorrow is Psalm 23."*

That single message does three things. It tells people what to expect. It tells them you have thought about the load-shedding. It tells them the Bible study is more important than the load-shedding.

### 9. Pre-Record the Passage Discussion for Late Joiners

This is a small thing that has made a big difference. After the Wednesday evening, one of the regulars records a 15-minute audio summary of the discussion and posts it to the WhatsApp group. People who missed the meeting — because of load-shedding, because of work, because of children — can catch up on the way to work the next morning.

This is not a substitute for showing up. It is a safety net for the week when everything went wrong.

### 10. Build the Bible Study Around the People with the Least Backup

If half your Bible study is on solar and half is on Eskom directly, you will accidentally build a Bible study for the half on solar. The meetings will happen at times that work for the solar half. The backups will assume WiFi. The readings will assume laptops.

Build the Bible study around the half on Eskom directly. That is the half that needs the Bible study the most. That is the half that, when the Bible study survives, will tell their friends about it.

In practice, this means:
- A WhatsApp voice-note option for every meeting
- A printed passage for every meeting
- A torch or candle in every meeting room
- A discussion that does not depend on slides

### 11. Do Not Cancel the Meeting Just Because the Power Is Out

This is the most important adaptation. When Eskom announces stage 6, the temptation is to cancel the Bible study. Do not cancel it. Move it. Hold it. Adapt it.

A Bible study that gets cancelled every time Eskom announces load-shedding is not really a Bible study. It is a Bible study that depends on the grid. The Bible was not written on the grid. The early church met in homes without electricity. The Reformation happened in candlelit rooms. The first Bible study in Soweto in 1976 happened without any infrastructure at all.

Load-shedding is, in its own way, a gift. It strips the Bible study back to what it actually is: a few people, a passage, and a willingness to keep going.

## What We Have Learned

After seven years of running a Bible study that does not stop when Eskom stops, here is what we have learned.

The Bible study does not get weaker when it is interrupted by load-shedding. It gets honest. People stop performing. People stop pretending. People show up with the truth about their week because the alternative — pretending everything is fine — feels exhausting in the candlelight.

The community gets tighter. When you have had to WhatsApp voice-note your way through a Psalm at 9pm because the video call dropped, you know the people in that group differently than you know your Sunday morning congregation.

The teaching gets deeper. The verses that matter in load-shedding are not the comfortable verses. They are the verses about waiting. About darkness. About the kind of hope that does not require the lights to be on.

If you live in South Africa and you have been thinking about starting a Bible study in your home, your community centre, your church hall, your office — do not wait for Eskom to be fixed. It will not be fixed in our lifetimes. Start the Bible study this week. Buy the candles. Set up the WhatsApp group. Send the first voice note. The Bible study will not be perfect. It will be honest. It will be South African. It will be enough.
