# When the Dog Dies: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Losing a Pet and Grieving Something the World Says You Shouldn't

## The Scene

The woman in Roodepoort buried the dog in the garden on a Saturday morning. The children were crying. The husband did not understand why she was crying too. She had had the dog for fourteen years. Her daughter was six when they got him. Her daughter is twenty now.

**Psalm 136:1** — *"Give thanks to the Lord, because he is good. His love endures forever."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Bible and Animals

Genesis 1:31 says: 'God saw all that he had made, and it was very good.' Animals were part of God's 'very good' creation. Proverbs 12:10 says: 'The righteous care for the needs of their animals.' The Bible takes animals seriously. They are part of God's good creation. They are not human. But they are not nothing.

The Christian who grieves a pet is not grieving something imaginary. The grief is real.

## Why the Church Often Gets This Wrong

The church in South Africa often tells the grieving pet owner: 'It's just a dog.' This is unhelpful. The church in the Bible had a different approach. Jesus, in Matthew 10:29, uses the sparrow as an example of God's care. 'Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? Yet not one of them will fall to the ground outside your Father's care.' The God who notices the sparrow notices the dog.

## The Particular Grief of the Long-Term Pet

The pet that has been part of the household for a decade or more is not a pet in the normal sense. It is a family member who has been present through the moves, the children growing up, the difficult years, the happy years. The loss of this pet is a loss of a witness to the family's history.

## Practical Pastoral Care for the Grieving Pet Owner

First: acknowledge the grief. Say the pet's name. Ask about the pet. Second: do not minimise. 'Just a dog' is not what the grieving owner needs to hear. Third: allow the grieving owner to talk about the pet — the story, the character, the habits. Fourth: remember the anniversary. The grieving owner will remember the day for years.

## A Prayer for the Person Who Has Lost a Pet

Father, we bring the grief that the world calls small but that feels very large. We bring the dog who was more than a dog. We bring the cat who slept on the pillow. We bring the animal companion who was present through everything. We ask for the community to acknowledge this grief. We ask for the grace to mourn without embarrassment. We ask for the memories to be a comfort, not a wound. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
