# Faith and the Machine: A South African Christian's Guide to AI, Algorithms, and the Question That Is Not New but Is Now Urgent

## The Scene

The man in Sandton is a software engineer. He builds AI systems. He is also a Christian. He wonders whether the technology he is building is good for the world. He does not know who to ask.

**Genesis 1:31** — *"God saw all that he had made, and it was very good."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Bible and New Technology

The Bible is not a book about technology. But the Bible is a book about the human heart. The same human heart that built the Tower of Babel is the human heart that builds the AI system. Genesis 11:1-9 is not a prediction of AI. But the warning is the same: technology that is built to make a name for ourselves will eventually confuse us.

## The Specific Questions of AI in South Africa

AI in South Africa in 2026 raises specific questions: jobs (which jobs will be displaced), bias (the bias in the data), privacy (the data collection), concentration (the small number of companies building the technology), and the question of human dignity. These are ethical questions that the Christian has something to say about.

## What the Christian Can Contribute

First: humility. The technology is not God. The technology is not even new. Every generation has faced new technology. Second: ethics. The Christian has a long tradition of ethical thinking about human dignity, work, and stewardship. Third: presence. The Christian who is present in the AI conversation is a witness that the technology is not the final word.

## A Prayer in the Age of AI

Father, we bring the technology that is changing everything. We ask for the wisdom to use it well. We ask for the courage to be present in the conversation. We ask for the ethics that honours human dignity. We ask for the presence that is not replaced by the machine. You are still God. You are still the maker of all things. The technology is yours too. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
