# Grandparenting Through the Load-Shedding: A South African Christian's Guide to the Candlelight and the Grandchildren

## The Scene

The lights went out at six in the evening. The inverter was on. The JoJo tank was full. The grandchildren were staying over. The grandmother in Sandton lit the candles, opened the Bible story book, and began.

**Psalm 136:1** — *"Give thanks to the Lord, because he is good. His love endures forever."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## The Unique Role of the Grandparent

The grandparent can be the keeper of the stories — the family history, the faith history, the stories of God's goodness in the past. The load-shedding evening is an opportunity, not just an inconvenience.

## What the Bible Says About the Older Generation

Psalm 71:18: 'Even when I am old and gray, do not forsake me, my God, till I declare your power to the next generation.' The grandparent has a job to do.

## Turning Load-Shedding into Ministry

The grandparent who uses the load-shedding evening for a Bible story, a prayer, a song, and a game has turned the Eskom failure into a family blessing.

## Practical Load-Shedding Grandparent Tips

Have a 'load-shedding box.' Have the stories ready. Have a prayer ready. Use the candlelight for what screens cannot replicate: presence.

## A Prayer for the Load-Shedding Evening

Father, thank you for the load-shedding. Use the evening to speak to the grandchildren. Make the load-shedding a small church in the home. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
