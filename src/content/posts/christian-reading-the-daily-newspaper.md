# The News and the Christian: A South African's Guide to Staying Informed Without Losing Your Mind, Your Faith, or Your Hope

## The Scene

The man in Randburg reads the news every morning. By the time he gets to work, he has read about the crime statistics, the load-shedding schedule, the municipal corruption, the latest political scandal. He starts his day in a state of low-grade anxiety. He is not sure how to stop.

**Romans 12:2** — *"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About News and World Events

Romans 13:1 says: 'Everyone must submit themselves to the governing authorities, for there is no authority except that which God has established.' This verse invites the Christian to engage with the political world — not to withdraw from it. But engagement requires discernment, not anxiety. The Christian who reads every news story with the same level of distress is not engaging wisely.

## The Discipline of News Consumption

The wise news consumer sets boundaries. First: limit the time. Thirty minutes a day of news is enough to stay informed. Second: choose credible sources. Third: avoid the news before bed — it affects sleep. Fourth: follow up action with knowledge. If you read about the municipal failure, contact the ward councillor. If you read about the crime, support the neighbourhood watch. Knowledge without action is just anxiety.

## What the Christian Should Do About South Africa's Problems

After ten years of Bible study in Parkmore, we have learned that the Christian who is overwhelmed by South Africa's problems and does nothing is not being faithful. The Christian who is paralysed by anxiety and does nothing is not being faithful. The faithful response is to do one thing, consistently, in your area of influence — your street, your suburb, your workplace, your family.

## A Prayer for the Informed Christian

Father, we bring the news that is too much. We bring the anxiety that comes from knowing too much and doing too little. Give us the wisdom to know what to read and what to ignore. Give us the courage to act on what we know. Give us the hope that does not depend on the government getting it right. Give us the faith to believe that you are still on the throne even when Eskom is not. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
