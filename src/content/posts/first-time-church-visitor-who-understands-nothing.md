# First Time in a South African Church and Understanding Nothing: A Bible Study for the Person Who Walked Through the Wrong Door

## The Scene

She walked into the Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore by mistake. She was looking for the community hall where the yoga class was supposed to be. She sat down. Everyone had a Bible open. She did not. They seemed to know each other. She felt immediately that she had made a mistake. She almost left.

**Acts 2:42-47** — *"They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About the Stranger at Church

Matthew 25:35 says: 'I was a stranger and you invited me in.' Jesus identifies himself with the stranger who comes to the community. The church that does not welcome the stranger is turning away Christ himself. This is not a metaphor. It is a direct statement from Jesus.

Acts 2:42-47 describes the early church's practice of welcoming: 'They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer. Everyone was filled with awe... and the Lord added to their number those who were being saved.' The church grew because it was welcoming.

## Why the Church Often Fails the First-Time Visitor

The church fails the first-time visitor in predictable ways. The greeter who hands out a leaflet that assumes you know what 'evangelical' means. The hymn book that is impossible to follow without a music degree. The sermon that assumes a Bible background. The coffee time that is really a closed circle of friends. These failures are not malicious. They are simply the result of a community that has forgotten what it was like to be new.

## What the First-Time Visitor Actually Needs

The first-time visitor needs three things: someone to introduce themselves, a seat near but not at the centre, and a reason to come back. Not a theological lecture. Not a hard sell. Just basic human welcome.

## A Word to the Person Who Walked in and Felt Out of Place

If you are reading this and you have walked into a church and felt like a stranger — you were not wrong to feel that way. But you were not as out of place as you thought. The Bible says you belong. Come back. Find the person who said hello and ask them what they got from the sermon. You are not expected to understand everything on the first visit. No one is.

## A Prayer for the First-Time Visitor

Father, we bring the person who walked into church and felt like they did not belong. We bring the person who is not sure what they believe. We bring the person who is genuinely curious. We ask for the community that will not make them feel foolish for asking questions. We ask for the courage to come back. We ask for the encounter with you that they are looking for — even if they do not know how to ask for it. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
