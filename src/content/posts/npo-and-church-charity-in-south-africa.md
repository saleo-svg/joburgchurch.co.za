# Why Your Church Needs an NPO Number: A South African Christian's Practical Guide to Church Charity, Section 18A, and Doing Good Well

## The Scene

The pastor in the East Rand wants to feed the children in the informal settlement nearby. She has the food. She has the volunteers. She does not have the NPO registration that would allow donors to claim their donations against tax. She is losing donors who want the Section 18A certificate. She did not know she needed it.

**2 Corinthians 9:6-7** — *"Remember this: Whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and whoever sows generously will also reap generously. Each of you should give what you have decided in your heart to give, not reluctantly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Christian Charity

2 Corinthians 9:6-7 says: 'Whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly, and whoever sows generously will also reap generously.' The Bible is clear that Christian generosity is a spiritual discipline. But the Bible also assumes that generosity is organised, purposeful, and transparent. The offering plate in the early church was not random. It was administered by the apostles with a specific system.

In South Africa, that system includes the NPO and Section 18A framework.

## The NPO Act and What It Means for Churches

The Non-Profit Organisations Act requires churches with more than a certain income to register as NPOs. This registration is not just legal compliance. It is an act of transparency and accountability. It says to the community: we are not a money-making enterprise. We are a public benefit organisation.

## Section 18A and Tax-Deductible Donations

Section 18A of the Income Tax Act allows donors to claim their church donations as a tax deduction — but only if the church has a Section 18A approval letter from SARS. For a church that is actively fundraising for community projects, this is significant. A R1,000 donation that can be claimed against tax is effectively cheaper for the donor to give than a donation that cannot.

## Practical Steps for the Church Doing Charity

First: register as an NPO if your income exceeds the threshold. Second: apply for Section 18A status. Third: keep transparent financial records — donations received and how they are spent. Fourth: report annually to NPO Directorate. Fifth: communicate clearly to donors what their giving supports.
