# Reading the Bible All the Way Through: A South African Christian's One-Year Plan for the Book That Changes Everything

## The Scene

The man in Randburg bought a Bible reading plan in January. He read consistently for three weeks. Then the load-shedding cut his evening routine. Then the overtime at work. By March he had fallen behind and given up. The Bible reading plan is still on his desk, half-used.

**Hebrews 11:1** — *"Now faith is confidence in what we hope for and assurance about what we do not see."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## Why Reading the Whole Bible Matters

Psalm 119:105 says: 'Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path.' The Bible is not a collection of inspirational quotes. It is a narrative — the story of God's relationship with humanity from creation to consummation. The Christian who reads only the familiar passages is like someone who watches only the highlights of a film. The full story is what changes everything.

## The One-Year Plan for the South African Christian

The plan is simple: read three chapters a day, five days a week. On weekdays: 3 chapters. On weekends: optional catch-up or devotional reading. Old Testament in the morning, New Testament in the evening. This gives approximately 750 chapters in 250 weekdays — enough to get through the whole Bible in a year with some buffer.

## What to Do When You Fall Behind

You will fall behind. Everyone does. The secret is not to skip the reading and catch up. The secret is to reset. If you miss a week, do not try to read 21 chapters to catch up. Just start from where you are. The goal is not to finish the Bible in a year. The goal is to read the Bible — consistently, faithfully, and with attention.

## Tools That Help in South Africa

First: a readable translation. The ESV, NIV, or New International Version are all accessible. Second: a reading app. The Bible App (YouVersion) has built-in reading plans with reminders. Third: a journal. Write one sentence about what you read each day. Fourth: an accountability partner. Find one person who is doing the plan with you.

## A Prayer Before the Reading

Father, open our eyes as we read your word. We confess that we have neglected it. We confess that we are often too tired, too busy, or too distracted to read. Give us the discipline. Give us the hunger. Give us the understanding. Speak through the ancient text to our present situation. Make us people of your word. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
