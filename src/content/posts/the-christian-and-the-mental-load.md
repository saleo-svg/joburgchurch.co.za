# The Mental Load: A South African Christian's Guide to the Invisible Labour That Is Killing Your Marriage, Your Motherhood, and Your Sundays

## The Scene

The woman in Randburg has been keeping the mental load of the household for fifteen years. The husband works. The children have grown. She is exhausted by things that no one sees.

**1 Peter 4:10** — *"Each of you should use whatever gift you have received to serve others, as faithful stewards of God's grace in its various forms."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Mental Load Is

The mental load is the invisible labour of remembering, anticipating, and managing the household. It is the appointment that is in your calendar. The grocery that is on the list. The school shoe that needs replacing. It is not 'helping' the family. It is the work that makes the family function.

## Why the Bible Addresses This

Proverbs 31 describes the excellent wife and mother. The work described there is not just the manual labour. It is the management of the household. The Bible honours this work. But the Bible also does not say that this work should be carried by one person alone.

## What the Church Can Do

The church can name the mental load. The church can preach about it. The church can challenge the men in the congregation to share the invisible labour. The church can also provide practical relief — the meal train, the childcare, the practical help that is more valuable than advice.

## A Prayer for the Woman Carrying the Mental Load

Father, we bring the invisible labour that no one sees. We bring the wife, the mother, the daughter who has been carrying this alone. We ask for the marriage to share the load. We ask for the church to see the work. We ask for the grace to say 'I am tired' without shame. We ask for the rest that is owed. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
