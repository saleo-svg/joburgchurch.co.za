# The Facebook Christian: A South African's Guide to Faith, Social Media, and the Person You Become When You Are Behind a Screen

## The Scene

The man in Sandton has strong opinions about politics, religion, and South African current affairs. He posts them on Facebook. The posts are eloquent, well-argued, and absolutely aggressive. His church friends see them. His work colleagues see them. He has lost friendships over posts he would not have said in person.

**Proverbs 12:22** — *"The Lord detests lying lips, but he delights in people who are trustworthy."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Speech

Matthew 12:36-37 says: 'I tell you that people will have to give account on the day of judgement for every careless word they have spoken. By your words you will be acquitted, and by your words you will be condemned.' This verse was written before social media. It was written for a world of spoken words. It applies with even greater force to written words, because written words are permanent. The careless word on Facebook is there forever.

## The Anonymity Problem

The person behind the screen is often a different person from the person in the room. The screen removes the immediate consequences of the words — the facial expression, the body language, the walk-out. This anonymity produces speech that the same person would never use in a room. Proverbs 25:28 says: 'Like a city whose walls are broken down is a person who lacks self-control.' The social media account is a wall broken down.

## What Faithful Social Media Looks Like

Faithful social media is different from pious social media. Pious social media quotes the Bible without living it. Faithful social media is characterised by: truthfulness (not sharing unverified claims), kindness (not the aggressive tone that would never be used in person), and restraint (not every opinion needs to be posted).

## A Practical Rule for the Christian on Social Media

Before posting, ask: Would I say this if this person was standing in front of me? Would I say this if my pastor was reading it? Would I say this if it was printed in the newspaper? If the answer to any of these questions is no, do not post it.

## A Prayer Before the Next Post

Father, we bring the social media post that is about to be written in anger. We bring the tweet that is about to be sent in frustration. We bring the Facebook argument that will damage a relationship. Give us the pause before the post. Give us the self-control that the screen removes. Make our words online reflect the grace we want to show. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
