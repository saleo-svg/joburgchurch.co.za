# The Step-Parent in the South African Home: A Bible Study on the Family That God Did Not Plan but God Still Honours

## The Scene

The woman in Midrand has been a step-mother for four years. She loves the children. She is not their mother. She will never be their mother. The children call her by her first name. The ex-wife is still difficult. The husband sometimes forgets she is not their biological mother. She is exhausted.

**Colossians 3:13** — *"Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Says About Step-Families

The Bible does not use the word 'step-family.' But the Bible is full of blended and complicated families. Abraham had children with two different women. Jacob's family was a blended mess. David had a household of wives and children from multiple women. Joseph was not the biological father of Jesus.

The biblical pattern is consistent: God works through imperfect families. The step-parent is not a lesser parent. The step-parent is a different kind of parent — one who has chosen to love children who did not come from them.

## The Specific Challenges of the South African Step-Family

The South African step-family faces particular pressures. The maintenance payments, the custody arrangements, the ex-spouse who lives in another suburb, the grandparents who favour the biological grandchildren, the school that needs both sets of parents to sign documents. These pressures are real. They are not solved by reading the Bible. But the Bible gives the framework for navigating them.

## The Rule About Not Alienating the Children

The most important rule for the step-parent — one that is almost never said in South African churches — is: do not try to replace the absent parent. Colossians 3:21 says: 'Fathers, do not embitter your children, or they will become discouraged.' This is addressed to fathers, but the principle applies to step-parents of both genders. The child who is made to feel that their absent parent is unloved or unreliable by the step-parent is being wounded in a specific way. The step-parent's job is to supplement, not to replace.

## Building Trust with Step-Children

Trust with step-children is built slowly and through consistent small acts — not through dramatic declarations of love. The step-parent who shows up to the school concert, who drives to the mall without complaint, who keeps the bedroom door open — these are the acts of love that build trust over years. The step-parent who tries to force an instant bond is almost always disappointed.

## A Prayer for the Step-Parent

Father, we bring the step-parent who is tired of not being enough — not thin enough, not patient enough, not loving enough. We bring the step-parent who is caught between the ex-spouse and the new family. We bring the step-parent who wonders if they are doing more harm than good. Give them wisdom for each day. Give them patience for the long road. Give them the assurance that the small acts of love matter more than the big declarations. Give them the grace they extend to the children. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
