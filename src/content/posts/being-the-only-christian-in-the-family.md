# When You Are the Only Christian in the Family: A South African's Honest Guide to Faith, Family Dinners, and the God Who Witnesses Through You

## The Scene

The woman in Springs is the only Christian in her family. Her mother is a traditional healer. Her brother is Muslim. Her sister is not religious. Every family gathering is a minefield of spiritual assumptions that she navigates silently. At her father's funeral next month, she does not know what she will do.

**Romans 15:13** — *"May the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace as you trust in him, so that you may overflow with hope by the power of the Holy Spirit."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What 1 Peter Says About Suffering for Being a Christian

1 Peter 3:13-17 says: 'Who is going to harm you if you are eager to do good? But even if you should suffer for what is right, you are blessed.' The Bible does not promise that being the only Christian in the family will be easy. It promises that being faithful is more important than being comfortable.

## The Three Mistakes the Solo Christian Makes

First: evangelising constantly and relating never. The family member who is preached at rather than listened to will not hear. Second: separating completely and losing influence entirely. The Christian who disappears from the family to avoid conflict loses the opportunity to be present. Third: compromising the faith to keep the peace. This is not a solution. It is a slow death of the witness.

## The Right Approach: Presence and Consistency

After ten years of Bible study in Parkmore, we have learned that the most powerful witness to an unchurched family is consistency. The family member who is known for their integrity, their generosity, their patience, and their joy — even in difficulty — is a witness. This is what 1 Peter 3:16 means: 'Live such good lives among the pagans that, though they accuse you of doing wrong, they may see your good deeds and glorify God.'

## Navigating Family Rituals You Cannot Participate In

The Christian who cannot participate in a family ritual — a funeral rite, a traditional ceremony, a prayer — needs wisdom, not a rule. The principle from Romans 14 is helpful: 'Accept people who are uncertain about whether to eat food that has been offered to an idol; their conscience, though weak, tells them they should not. But other people's faith does not allow them to eat.' The Christian who is uncertain about participation should follow their conscience and be honest about it.

## A Prayer for the Solo Christian in the Family

Father, we bring the Christian who stands alone in the family. We bring the loneliness of the faith that is not shared. We bring the fear of the family event. We ask for the courage to be present. We ask for the wisdom to know when to speak and when to be silent. We ask for the consistency that is a witness even without words. We ask for the grace to love the family even when they do not share the faith. We ask for the hope that you are working even when we cannot see it. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
