## The Hour the Sky Changes

If you have lived in Johannesburg for more than a summer, you know the hour. Around three o'clock in the afternoon, usually between mid-November and the end of March, the sky over the Magaliesberg darkens in a way no other African sky does. The light goes yellow. The jacarandas go still. The first thunder rolls in from the west, and within twenty minutes the kind of rain that turns Joburg streets into rivers is falling on your roof.

For most of the year, our Wednesday Bible study community in Parkmore has a stable rhythm. People wake up, get to work, come home, and at 7:30pm they log into Google Meet. The rhythm works because it does not depend on the weather. From November to March, it depends on the weather a little more than we would like.

This post is for anyone in Johannesburg — or anywhere in the Highveld — who has noticed that summer changes how they pray, how they read, and how they show up. It is also for the Bible study leader who has had half the room disappear in January and is trying to figure out why.

## What the Highveld Summer Actually Does to Your Devotional Life

Before the practical adjustments, it is worth naming what the season does. The summer rains in Johannesburg are not just weather. They are a disruption to the rhythm most of us carry into the new year.

### The Days Get Longer, but Energy Gets Lower

In November, the sun sets in Johannesburg around 6:45pm. By the end of January, it is after 8pm. The longer days feel like they should give us more time. In practice, they do the opposite. The heat of the Highveld afternoon saps the energy most people use for the part of the day that has traditionally been reserved for prayer and reading. By 7:30pm on a Wednesday, the room is half-asleep. By 9pm, half of them have logged off.

### Load-Shedding Has a Different Shape in Summer

We have written elsewhere about load-shedding and Bible study. In winter, load-shedding hits at the worst possible time. In summer, it hits at a slightly different worst possible time. Eskom's summer schedule tends to push outages into the late afternoon and early evening — exactly the slot most people have historically used for their personal quiet time. The candle that used to be a romantic accessory in November becomes a practical necessity in February.

### People Travel

December in Johannesburg is a city of empty offices. People go to Durban. People go to Cape Town. People go to the Kruger. People go home to Zimbabwe, to Zambia, to Malawi, to the UK for the first time in three years. The Bible study community in Parkmore in mid-December is not the Bible study community in Parkmore in mid-March. The size of the room changes. The faces in the room change. The rhythm that held in October gets disrupted, and the new rhythm takes about three months to settle.

### Schools Close, and the Calendar Splits

For families with school-aged children, the four weeks between early December and mid-January split the calendar in two. The first half is festive and full. The second half is the slow back-end of holidays, where the children are restless, the parents are tired, and the daily quiet time gets squeezed into whatever five minutes is left after the swimming, the lunchboxes, and the screen-time negotiations.

### The New Year Comes in January, but the New Rhythm Comes in February

This is the part that nobody tells you. The Bible study community in Johannesburg does not really start the new year in January. It starts in the second week of February, when the schools go back, the offices fill up, and the Wednesday evening Google Meet stops being an invitation to a half-empty room and becomes an invitation to a full one again. The seven weeks between mid-December and mid-February are a season in themselves, and they deserve to be treated like one.

## Five Practical Adjustments for the Rainy Season

Here are the five adjustments we have made over the years. None of them are dramatic. All of them are practical.

### 1. Move Your Quiet Time to the Morning, Even Temporarily

This is the single biggest adjustment. If your quiet time has historically been at 7pm, and the 7pm slot is now collapsing under load-shedding and the longer evening, move it to the morning for the season. The hour between 5:30am and 7am, when the Highveld sky is still cool and the children are still asleep, is the most reliable quiet hour in a Johannesburg summer.

You do not have to do this forever. You do it for November, December, January, February. You do it for the four months when the rest of your life is in a different shape. In March, when the days start to shorten again and the rain softens into the Highveld autumn, you can move it back.

If you are part of our Wednesday Bible study community, the morning quiet time also feeds the Wednesday evening. The passage you read on Monday morning is the passage you discuss on Wednesday evening. The morning reading becomes the evening discussion. The evening discussion becomes the morning reflection. The cycle holds.

### 2. Get a Rain-Friendly Bible

This sounds trivial, but it is one of the most common problems we have heard from people in our Bible study community. They want to read on the stoep during a thunderstorm. They want to read in the garden when the first drops start falling. They cannot, because their Bible is the nice leather one that does not like water.

A Bible for a Johannesburg summer does not need to be fancy. It needs to survive a sudden storm. Two practical options:

- A paperback Bible in a zip-lock bag. Cheap. Disposable. Easy to grab when you see the sky change.
- A Bible app on your phone with the offline download enabled. The YouVersion app works without signal, which means it works when the load-shedding has killed the WiFi. Use your data only when you want to sync.

Either option lets you read on the veranda, at the pool, at the park, in the garden, without worrying about the first drop of rain on the page.

### 3. Make the Wednesday Evening a Little Shorter in Summer

The Wednesday evening in our Bible study community runs ninety minutes for most of the year. From mid-November to mid-February, we run it for seventy. The drop in length is small. The drop in attendance is significant. People in summer will show up for seventy minutes. They will not show up for ninety.

The shortened format looks like this:

- 7:30pm — Reading and check-in (twenty minutes)
- 7:50pm — Discussion of one main question (forty minutes)
- 8:30pm — One prayer, one song, one closing (ten minutes)
- 8:40pm — Done. People go home in the light.

The discussion goes from three questions to one. The reading goes from a passage and a Psalm to just the passage. The format gets tighter, and the room gets fuller.

### 4. Plan a January Bible Reading Plan That Travels

December and January are the months when the Bible study community is scattered. The people who are in Johannesburg in December are not the same people who are in Johannesburg in January. The people in Cape Town for the holidays are not the same people who are in Durban. The Bible reading plan for these two months needs to work for everyone, no matter where they are.

We use a December-January reading plan that has three rules:

- **It is short.** Twelve verses a day, not a chapter. Twelve verses is the kind of reading you can do in a queue at the airport, in the back of a car on the N1, on the stoep of a house in Umhlanga.
- **It is portable.** The reading is on the WhatsApp group, on the website, and in the YouVersion app. Wherever you are, the reading is there.
- **It is the same for everyone.** When the Bible study community gathers again in February, the first Wednesday back is a chance to share what stood out from the December-January reading. The shared reading creates continuity across the gap.

### 5. Do Not Stop Just Because the Rhythm Has Stopped

This is the most important adjustment. The summer rainy season in Johannesburg will disrupt your quiet time. It will disrupt your Bible study attendance. It will disrupt your small group. It will disrupt your family devotions. It will disrupt your prayer routine. It will do all of this without asking your permission.

The temptation, in the disruption, is to stop. To decide that the quiet time will come back in March. To decide that the Bible study will pick up again in February. To decide that the family devotions will resume when the children are back at school.

The honest truth is that some of those decisions are wise. You do need to give yourself permission to rest in December. You do need to give yourself permission to travel. You do need to give yourself permission to be a parent in the middle of summer holidays.

You do not, however, need to give yourself permission to stop altogether. A daily quiet time of five minutes is still a daily quiet time. A Bible study attended twice in January is still a Bible study. A prayer said in the back of a taxi between Sandton and the airport is still a prayer.

The Johannesburg rainy season is a season. It will end. The sky will clear. The Highveld will go back to its winter blue. The Bible study community will fill up again in February. The quiet time will return to its proper slot. In the meantime, keep showing up. Even for five minutes. Even on the stoep in the rain. Even in the back of the car. Even in the airport queue.

## A Summer Prayer for Johannesburg

If you would like a prayer for the season, this is one we have used at the start of December in our Wednesday Bible study for the last few years.

> *God of the Highveld,*
>
> *We thank you for the rain. For the way it cools the streets of Sandton in the afternoon. For the way it fills the dams in the south of the city. For the way it reminds us that we live in a country that needs your mercy to fall.*
>
> *Forgive us for the rhythms we have built that depend on the weather being kind to us. Forgive us for the quiet times we have let the heat interrupt. Forgive us for the prayer routines we have postponed until the season changes.*
>
> *This summer, give us the discipline to keep showing up. Even tired. Even distracted. Even when the children are restless and the load-shedding is on and the room is half-empty. Give us the grace to start again on the mornings we have missed. Give us the joy of a Bible that has survived the rain on the stoep. Give us the patience to wait for February, knowing you are already there.*
>
> *We pray for the people of Johannesburg in this season. For the families on holiday. For the parents who are tired. For the students who are waiting for results. For the elderly who find the heat hard. For the Bible study communities across the city who are quieter than they were in October.*
>
> *May we be a community that does not depend on the weather. May we be a community that does not depend on the calendar. May we be a community that shows up, even in the rain.*
>
> *In the name of the one who calmed the storm on the Sea of Galilee.*
> *Amen.*

## A Short Reading List for the Rainy Season

For the seven weeks between mid-December and mid-February, here are four short books we recommend for the Bible study community. None of them are heavy. All of them are South African or written for the African context. All of them can be read in an hour or two.

- *The Year of the Flood* — a South African devotional that walks through the church year with a local accent. Useful for anyone trying to read the Bible in the rhythm of a South African calendar.
- *Hearing God in the City* — a series of reflections on paying attention to God in the middle of an urban life. Particularly relevant for anyone who has lost the habit of paying attention during the long summer days.
- *The Psalms for the Hurting* — a short selection of the Psalms of lament, chosen for people who are tired. Useful for the Sunday after a hard week.
- *The Bible* — still the best book for the rainy season, and the one that fits in a zip-lock bag.
