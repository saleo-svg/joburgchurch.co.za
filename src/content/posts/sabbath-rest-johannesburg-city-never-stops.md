## The City That Never Stops

There is a sound Johannesburg makes at six in the morning that you stop hearing after a few years in the city. It is the sound of an engine starting in the next driveway, a kettle clicking on, a taxi hooting on the corner of Republic Road, the Gautrain announcement at Sandton station saying the first train to Pretoria is on its way. The sound means the week has begun. The sound also means the week has not ended, because in Johannesburg the week is rarely ending. The week is a long loop that begins again on the other side of a Sunday that feels like a Saturday.

You know the rhythm. You know it in your body. The Sunday afternoon braai turns into the Sunday evening email. The Sunday evening email turns into the Monday morning meeting. The Monday meeting turns into the Monday evening school run. The school run turns into the Tuesday deadline. The deadline turns into the Wednesday Bible study that you almost did not come to. The Wednesday Bible study turns into the Thursday call from the client. The client call turns into the Friday night out that you almost cancelled. The Friday night out turns into the Saturday morning load-shedding schedule and the Saturday afternoon grocery run and the Saturday evening family WhatsApp group. And then it is six in the morning again. The engine in the next driveway. The kettle. The taxi. The Gautrain. The loop.

The Christian in Johannesburg is not the only one who is tired. The Christian in Johannesburg is, however, the one who has been told by the Bible that the tiredness does not have to win. The Bible has a great deal to say about rest. The Bible has a great deal to say about work. The Bible has even more to say about the relationship between the two. The relationship is older than the city. The relationship is older than the load-shedding schedule. The relationship is older than the WhatsApp group. The relationship was given to the people of God as the third command of the third week of the third month of the third year of their long walk through the wilderness.

This post is for the Christian in Johannesburg who is tired. It is for the Christian in Johannesburg who has not had a real day off in longer than they would like to admit. It is for the Christian in Johannesburg who has been told by a colleague that the busy season will end, and who has begun to suspect that the busy season does not end. It is for the Christian who wants to know what the Bible has to say about the long week, and what the Christian is called to do with it.

## What the Bible Actually Says About Rest

The Bible does not treat rest as an optional extra. The Bible treats rest as a creation ordinance. The Bible treats rest as one of the Ten Words. The Bible treats rest as the practice that holds the rest of the practices together.

**Genesis 2:2-3** — *By the seventh day God had finished the work he had been doing; so on the seventh day he rested from all his work. Then God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it he rested from all the work of creating that he had done.*

**Exodus 20:8-11** — *Remember the Sabbath day by keeping it holy. Six days you shall labour and do all your work, but the seventh day is a sabbath to the Lord your God. On it you shall not do any work, neither you, nor your son or your daughter, nor your male or female servant, nor your animals, nor any foreigner residing in your town. For in six days the Lord made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and all that is in them, but he rested on the seventh day. Therefore the Lord blessed the Sabbath day and made it holy.*

**Exodus 31:17** — *It will be a sign between me and the Israelites forever, for in six days the Lord made the heavens and the earth, and on the seventh day he rested and was refreshed.*

**Leviticus 25:10** — *Consecrate the fiftieth year and proclaim liberty throughout the land to all its inhabitants. It shall be a jubilee for you.*

**Deuteronomy 5:12-15** — *Observe the Sabbath day by keeping it holy, as the Lord your God has commanded you. Six days you shall labour and do all your work, but the seventh day is a sabbath to the Lord your God. On it you shall not do any work... Remember that you were slaves in Egypt and that the Lord your God brought you out of there with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm. Therefore the Lord your God has commanded you to observe the Sabbath day.*

What you notice is that the Bible treats the Sabbath as a creation ordinance and as a liberation ordinance at the same time. The Sabbath is the day on which the people of God remember that the world was made by a God who finished the work. The Sabbath is also the day on which the people of God remember that they were slaves in Egypt, and that the God who brought them out is the God who asks them to rest. The Sabbath is not a reward for hard work. The Sabbath is a gift for people who have been working hard.

The Christian in Johannesburg in 2026 is not the same as the Israelite in the wilderness in 1450 BC. The Christian in Johannesburg is, however, the same kind of creature. The Christian in Johannesburg has a body that needs rest. The Christian in Johannesburg has a mind that needs quiet. The Christian in Johannesburg has a soul that needs the practice of stopping. The Bible that was given to the Israelites in the wilderness is the Bible that is given to the Christian in Sandton on a Sunday afternoon. The command has not been withdrawn. The gift has not been withdrawn.

## What the Bible Says About Work

The Bible also has a great deal to say about work. The Bible does not treat work as a curse. The Bible treats work as a creation ordinance too.

**Genesis 2:15** — *The Lord God took the man and put him in the Garden of Eden to work it and take care of it.*

**Colossians 3:23** — *Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.*

**Proverbs 14:23** — *All hard work brings a profit, but mere talk leads only to poverty.*

**2 Thessalonians 3:10** — *For even when we were with you, we gave you this rule: "If a man will not work, he shall not eat."*

**Ecclesiastes 3:13** — *That each of them may eat and drink, and find satisfaction in all their toil — this is the gift of God.*

The Bible treats work as good. The Bible treats work as a gift. The Bible treats work as the way the Christian serves the neighbour, provides for the family, and joins God in the care of the world. The Christian in Johannesburg is not wrong to work hard. The Christian in Johannesburg is called to work hard. The Christian is also called to stop working.

The thing the Christian in Johannesburg has to learn is that the two belong together. The Bible never separates the work from the rest. The Bible never separates the rest from the work. The Bible holds the two together as a single practice — the practice of living a week that has a beginning, a middle, and an end, and then beginning again. The Christian who has learned to hold the two together has begun the long work of the gospel in the body.

## What the Bible Says About Burnout

The Bible is also honest about what happens when the two are separated. The Bible is honest about what happens when the work goes on without the rest. The Bible calls it something different in different places. The Bible calls it weariness. The Bible calls it despair. The Bible calls it the long nights of Elijah under the broom tree. The Bible calls it the long years of the Israelites in Babylon. The Bible calls it the cry of Job in the ash heap.

**Exodus 18:17-18** — *Moses' father-in-law replied, "What you are doing is not good. You and these people who come to you will only wear yourselves out. The work is too heavy for you; you cannot handle it alone."*

**1 Kings 19:4-8** — *He came to a broom tree, sat down under it and prayed that he might die. "I have had enough, Lord," he said. "Take my life; I am no better than my ancestors." He lay down under the tree and fell asleep. All at once an angel touched him and said, "Get up and eat." He looked around, and there by his head was some bread baked over hot coals, and a jar of water. He ate and drank and then lay down again. The angel of the Lord came back a second time and touched him and said, "Get up and eat, or the journey will be too much for you." So he got up and ate and drank. Strengthened by that food, he travelled forty days and forty nights until he reached Horeb, the mountain of God.*

**Galatians 6:9** — *Let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up.*

**Matthew 11:28-30** — *Come to me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy and my burden is light.*

**Psalm 127:2** — *In vain you rise early and stay up late, toiling for food to eat — for he grants sleep to those he loves.*

What you notice is that the Bible does not treat burnout as a sign of weak faith. The Bible treats burnout as a sign of being a creature. The Bible treats burnout as a sign that the body, the mind, and the soul have limits, and that the limits are real. Elijah, after the great victory at Mount Carmel, sat down under a broom tree and asked God to let him die. God did not rebuke Elijah for the prayer. God sent an angel with bread and water. The angel told Elijah to get up and eat. The angel told Elijah that the journey was too much without the food. The Christian in Johannesburg who has sat down under a broom tree is in good company with Elijah. The Christian in Johannesburg who has been told by an angel — by a friend, by a colleague, by a Bible study leader, by a small voice — to get up and eat has begun the long work of being a creature again.

## What the Bible Says About the City That Never Stops

The Christian in Johannesburg lives in a city that never stops. The Christian in Johannesburg works in an industry that often does not stop. The Christian in Johannesburg has a phone that does not stop. The Christian in Johannesburg has a WhatsApp group that does not stop. The Christian in Johannesburg has a news cycle that does not stop. The Christian in Johannesburg has been told by the world that the not-stopping is a virtue. The Bible has a different word for the not-stopping.

**Jeremiah 17:21-22** — *This is what the Lord says: Be careful not to carry a load on the Sabbath day or bring it through the gates of Jerusalem. Do not bring a load out of your houses or do any work on the Sabbath, but keep the Sabbath day holy, as I commanded your ancestors.*

**Nehemiah 13:15-18** — *In those days I saw people in Judah treading winepresses on the Sabbath and bringing in grain and loading it on donkeys, together with wine, grapes, figs and all other kinds of loads. And they were bringing all this into Jerusalem on the Sabbath. Therefore I warned them against selling food on the Sabbath. Men from Tyre who lived in Jerusalem were bringing in fish and all kinds of merchandise and selling them in Jerusalem on the Sabbath to the people of Judah. I rebuked the nobles of Judah and said to them, "What is this wicked thing you are doing — desecrating the Sabbath day?"*

**Mark 2:27** — *Then he said to them, "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath."*

**Luke 6:5** — *Then Jesus said to them, "The Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath."*

What you notice is that the Bible treats the not-stopping as a real temptation. Nehemiah, when he saw the people of Judah working on the Sabbath, did not shrug. Nehemiah rebuked the nobles. Nehemiah said that the not-stopping was a desecration. Nehemiah said that the not-stopping was a wicked thing. The Christian in Johannesburg who has begun to notice the not-stopping in the city, in the office, in the phone, in the WhatsApp group, has begun the long work of the Sabbath.

Mark 2:27 is important here. Jesus said, *The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath.* Jesus did not say the Sabbath does not matter. Jesus said the Sabbath was made for the human being. The Sabbath is a gift for the creature. The Sabbath is not a burden for the creature. The Christian who has begun to receive the Sabbath as a gift has begun the long work of the gospel in the body.

## Why the Christian in Johannesburg Struggles with Sabbath

The Christian in Johannesburg struggles with Sabbath for three reasons.

### 1. The Job Rewards the Long Hours

The job in Johannesburg often rewards the long hours. The promotion goes to the person who stayed late. The contract goes to the firm that answered the email at eleven on a Sunday night. The client is grateful to the lawyer, the accountant, the doctor, the consultant who picked up the phone on the Saturday morning. The job in Johannesburg does not always reward the Christian who keeps the Sabbath. The job in Johannesburg often rewards the Christian who breaks it.

The Bible is honest about this. The Bible does not pretend the job will reward the Sabbath. The Bible asks the Christian to keep the Sabbath anyway. The Bible asks the Christian to keep the Sabbath as a witness to a different kingdom. The Bible asks the Christian to keep the Sabbath as a small act of defiance against the long hours of the city. The Christian who has begun to keep the Sabbath in a job that does not reward it has begun the long work of the gospel in the office.

**Colossians 3:23** — *Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.* The Christian works hard in the six days. The Christian rests in the seventh day. The Christian does not make the rest conditional on the job. The Christian makes the rest conditional on the God who rested on the seventh day.

### 2. The Family Is Already Running

The family in Johannesburg is already running. The children have soccer on Saturday morning. The children have extra lessons on Saturday afternoon. The grocery shopping has to happen somewhere. The laundry has to be done somewhere. The house has to be cleaned somewhere. The Sunday is the only day the family has been together all week, and the Sunday is also the day the family is the most tired. The Christian in Johannesburg who tries to keep the Sabbath is often told by the family that the Sabbath is a nice idea, but the family has a schedule.

The Bible is honest about this too. The Bible does not promise the Christian an easy family. The Bible asks the Christian to teach the family the practice of stopping. The Bible asks the Christian to show the family that the schedule is not the last word. The Christian who has begun to teach the family the practice of stopping has begun the long work of the gospel in the home. The teaching is slow. The teaching is often a long road. The teaching is also worth it.

**Deuteronomy 6:7** — *Impress them on your children. Talk about them when you sit at home and when you walk along the road, when you lie down and when you get up.* The Christian teaches the practice of Sabbath by living the practice. The Christian teaches by stopping on a Sunday afternoon. The Christian teaches by not answering the email. The Christian teaches by sitting at the table with the family and looking at the family instead of the phone.

### 3. The Christian Often Carries the Work Inside the Body

The third reason the Christian in Johannesburg struggles with Sabbath is that the work has moved inside the body. The work is no longer at the office. The work is in the phone. The work is in the laptop on the kitchen table. The work is in the WhatsApp group that lights up at nine on a Sunday evening. The work is in the thought that runs through the mind at three in the morning. The Christian in Johannesburg is not only tired in the body. The Christian in Johannesburg is tired in the soul.

The Bible is honest about this too. The Bible knows that the heart is a noisy place. The Bible knows that the work moves inside the body. The Bible asks the Christian to bring the noisy heart to God. The Bible asks the Christian to lay the noisy heart down in front of the Sabbath God.

**Psalm 4:8** — *In peace I will lie down and sleep, for you alone, Lord, make me dwell in safety.*

**Psalm 23:2-3** — *He makes me lie down in green pastures, he leads me beside quiet waters, he refreshes my soul.*

**Philippians 4:6-7** — *Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.*

The Christian in Johannesburg who has begun to lie down in the green pastures has begun to stop carrying the work inside the body. The Christian in Johannesburg who has begun to give the noisy heart to God has begun the long work of the Sabbath in the soul. The giving is not a one-time event. The giving is a practice. The giving is a Sunday afternoon practice, a Wednesday evening practice, a Saturday morning practice, a three-in-the-morning practice.

## What the Sandton Bible Study Has Tried to Do

In the last few years, the Wednesday evening Bible study in Sandton has tried to take Sabbath seriously. Here is what we have done, for what it is worth.

### We Have Set Aside One Wednesday a Month for Rest

We have set aside one Wednesday a month as a no-Bible-study Wednesday. Not because the Bible study is a bad thing. Because the Bible study is a good thing, and the good thing can become another item on the long list if it is not watched. We have set aside the Wednesday and told the room to spend the Wednesday evening in the practice of stopping. To walk. To cook. To sit with the family. To read a novel. To be a creature.

The first time we tried it, three people came back the next Wednesday and said the no-Bible-study Wednesday had been the best thing that had happened to them in a month. The Christian who has begun to put a hole in the long week has begun to put a hole in the long loop.

### We Have Done a Six-Sermon Series on the Sabbath

We have done a six-week series on the Sabbath. The first week was Genesis 2. The second week was Exodus 20. The third week was Exodus 31. The fourth week was Nehemiah 13. The fifth week was Mark 2. The sixth week was Hebrews 4 — *There remains, then, a Sabbath-rest for the people of God.* The series was not a lecture series. The series was a conversation. The room was invited to name the things that kept the Sabbath from happening. The room named the phone. The room named the email. The room named the WhatsApp group. The room named the client. The room named the colleague. The room named the children. The room named the self.

The series was also a confession. The room confessed that the Christian in Johannesburg is part of a city that does not know how to rest. The room confessed that the Christian has often forgotten the practice of rest. The room confessed that the practice has to be relearned, slowly, Sunday by Sunday, week by week, month by month.

### We Have Taught Each Other to Cook on a Sunday

We have started a Sunday cooking practice. Not a fancy practice. A simple practice. We have taught each other to make one dish on a Sunday afternoon that can be eaten on a Sunday evening and a Monday lunch. The dish is not the point. The dish is a way of slowing the Sunday down. The dish is a way of being in the kitchen with the family. The dish is a way of noticing that the food was given by a God who rested on the seventh day.

The Christian who has begun to cook on a Sunday has begun to slow the Sunday down. The Christian who has begun to slow the Sunday down has begun the long work of the Sabbath.

## Five Practices for the Christian Who Wants to Keep the Sabbath in Johannesburg

If you are a Christian in Johannesburg and you want to begin the practice of Sabbath in a city that does not rest, here are five practices you can try.

### 1. Set Aside One Hour

Set aside one hour in the week. Not a full day. Not a full Sunday. One hour. Put it in the calendar. Tell the family. Tell the colleagues. Turn the phone off. Read a psalm. Walk the dog. Sit in the garden. Drink one cup of coffee slowly. The Christian who has one hour in the week has begun the long work of the Sabbath. The hour is not the Sabbath. The hour is a doorway into the Sabbath. The hour is a small hole in the long loop.

### 2. Stop Answering the Email After Eight

Stop answering the email after eight on a weeknight. Stop answering the email after noon on a Saturday. Stop answering the email on a Sunday. The Christian who has stopped answering the email has not become a worse worker. The Christian who has stopped answering the email has become a better creature. The Christian who has stopped answering the email has begun the long work of the Sabbath in the phone.

**1 Thessalonians 4:11** — *Make it your ambition to lead a quiet life.* The ambition is not for the loud life. The ambition is for the quiet life. The Christian who has made the quiet life the ambition has begun the long work of the Sabbath.

### 3. Cook One Meal Slowly

Cook one meal slowly this week. Not the whole week. One meal. Walk to the shop. Buy the ingredients. Wash the vegetables. Stir the pot. Sit at the table. Eat the meal. Wash the dishes. The Christian who has cooked one meal slowly has begun the long work of the Sabbath in the body. The meal is not the Sabbath. The meal is a doorway. The meal is a way of being a creature who is fed by a God who rested.

### 4. Read Psalm 127 Before Bed

Read Psalm 127 before bed on a Saturday night. The psalm that begins with *Unless the Lord builds the house, the builders labour in vain.* The psalm that says *In vain you rise early and stay up late, toiling for food to eat — for he grants sleep to those he loves.* The psalm that ends with *so his people will be like plants sprouting, like a cedar of Lebanon with many branches.* Read the psalm once. Read it twice. Read it as a prayer. The Christian who has read Psalm 127 before bed has begun to give the noisy heart to God before the night comes.

### 5. Show Up on a Wednesday

Show up on a Wednesday evening. The Wednesday evening in Sandton is a small island of rest in the middle of the week. The Wednesday evening is a place where the room is still. The Wednesday evening is a place where the psalm is read. The Wednesday evening is a place where the long loop is interrupted for an hour. The Christian who has shown up on a Wednesday has begun the long work of the Sabbath in the week. The Wednesday evening is not the Sabbath. The Wednesday evening is a doorway. The Wednesday evening is a way of being a creature who is held by a God who rested.
