# When the Drink Has Taken Over: A South African Christian's Honest Guide to Addiction and Recovery

## The Scene

The man in Springs has not had a drink for six days. He counts the days. The church congregation does not know. The wife knows. The children know. He is afraid that if the church finds out, he will be the subject of prayer requests that are really gossip. He is afraid that if he does not get help, the drink will take the family anyway.

**1 Corinthians 10:13** — *"No temptation has overtaken you except what is common to mankind. And God is faithful; he will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What the Bible Does Not Say About Addiction

The Bible does not use the word 'addiction.' But the Bible has a great deal to say about the person who is enslaved to something they cannot stop. Romans 7:15 says: 'I do not understand what I do. For what I want to do I do not do, but what I hate I do.' The apostle Paul — one of the most disciplined people in history — described the inner struggle with compulsive behaviour in remarkably personal terms.

The church has often responded to addiction with shame. This is the wrong response. The church is called to offer the same grace it offers to every other struggle.

## What 1 Corinthians 10:13 Actually Says

1 Corinthians 10:13 says: 'No temptation has overtaken you except what is common to mankind. And God is faithful; he will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear.' This verse is usually quoted to people facing minor temptations. But the verse was written to people facing severe temptation — the same Israelites who built a golden calf.

The verse promises two things: the temptation is common (you are not uniquely broken), and the way out is real (God will provide it). For the person addicted to alcohol or drugs, the way out often includes professional treatment, support groups, and medical intervention. God provides through these means.

## The Church and the Addicted Person

Galatians 6:1-2 says: 'Brothers and sisters, if someone is caught in a sin, you who live by the Spirit should restore that person gently. But watch yourselves, or you also may be tempted.' The church is called to restore the addicted person, not to exclude them. The church is called to walk with the family of the addicted person, not to gossip about them.

After ten years of Bible study in Parkmore, we have learned that the addicted person needs three things from the church: unconditional welcome, honest accountability, and practical support in recovery.

## Practical Steps for Recovery

First: acknowledge the problem honestly. Denial is the first symptom of addiction. Second: seek professional help — the AA programme, a therapist, a rehabilitation centre if needed. Third: find an accountability partner — someone who will ask the hard questions. Fourth: find a community that does not trigger — this may mean a different church for a season. Fifth: celebrate the small wins — six days sober is six days more than yesterday.

## A Prayer for the Person Struggling with Addiction

Father, we bring the struggle with addiction that no one talks about in church. We bring the shame that keeps people silent. We bring the family who does not know how to help. We ask for the courage to name the problem. We ask for the treatment that is needed. We ask for the community to be safe. We ask for the grace that welcomes without enabling. We ask for the recovery that is possible. We ask for the hope that does not give up. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
