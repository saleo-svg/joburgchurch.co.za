## The News That Comes Through the Phone

There is a moment in many Sandton kitchens in 2026 when the news comes through the phone. The child is doing homework at the kitchen table. The parent is making supper. The phone lights up. The parent reads the headline. The headline is a shooting, a hijacking, a story about a school somewhere in Gauteng, a story about a country somewhere else. The child looks up. The child has been trained to look up. The child has been trained to ask. The parent has to decide in that moment what to say.

The parent has a number of choices in that moment. The parent can tell the child the news is not real. The parent can tell the child the news is too big for the child. The parent can tell the child to put the headphones back in and finish the homework. The parent can tell the child the truth and walk with the child through the truth. The parent can tell the child the truth and walk with the child through the truth and the Bible at the same time.

The choice the parent makes in that moment is one of the most important choices the parent will make all week. The child is forming a theology of the news. The child is forming a theology of the world. The child is forming a theology of God. The theology the child forms in that moment will be the theology the child carries into the next hard news. The theology will be the theology the child carries into adulthood. The Christian parent in Johannesburg in 2026 has not been given a simple job. The Christian parent has been given a holy job.

This post is for the Christian parent in Johannesburg in 2026 who is doing the holy job. It is for the Christian parent who has stopped pretending the news is not there. It is for the Christian parent who has stopped pretending the Bible is not enough. It is for the Christian parent who has been told by a teacher that the child is anxious, and who has been told by a colleague that the child is too young, and who has begun to wonder what the Bible actually says about how to walk with a child through the hard news.

**Proverbs 22:6** — *Start children off on the way they should go, and even when they are old they will not turn from it.*

## What the Bible Says About Walking with a Child Through the Hard News

The Bible has specific guidance for the parent who is walking with a child through the hard news. The guidance is not complicated. The guidance is also not easy.

### 1. Do Not Pretend the News Is Not There

The first piece of guidance is that the parent should not pretend the news is not there. The child is forming a theology of the world. The child will form the theology with or without the parent. The theology formed without the parent will often be a theology of fear. The theology formed with the parent can be a theology of the God who holds the hard news.

**Psalm 46:1-3** — *God is our refuge and strength, an ever-present help in trouble. Therefore we will not fear, though the earth give way and the mountains fall into the heart of the sea, though its waters roar and foam and the mountains quake with their surging.*

The Christian parent in Johannesburg in 2026 who has begun to walk with the child through the hard news has begun to teach the child that the earth may give way. The Christian parent has also begun to teach the child that God is the refuge. The two teachings happen together. The Christian parent who only teaches the refuge without the earth that gives way has taught the child a smaller God than the Bible teaches.

### 2. Tell the Truth in Age-Appropriate Language

The second piece of guidance is that the parent should tell the truth in age-appropriate language. The child does not need every detail. The child does need the truth. The child needs to know that the parent is willing to walk with the child through the truth. The child needs to know that the parent is not afraid of the truth. The child needs to know that the parent has a place to bring the truth.

**Proverbs 22:6** — *Start children off on the way they should go, and even when they are old they will not turn from it.* The way the child goes begins with the way the parent tells the truth. The way the parent tells the truth on a Tuesday evening in a Sandton kitchen is the way the parent is teaching the child to tell the truth for the next forty years.

### 3. Bring the News to God Together

The third piece of guidance is that the parent should bring the news to God together. Not after the parent has processed it. Together. The parent and the child sit at the table. The parent and the child read a psalm. The parent and the child pray. The prayer is not fancy. The prayer is honest. The prayer is the truth the parent has just told the child, brought to God. The prayer is the parent's theology in miniature.

**Psalm 13:1-2** — *How long, Lord? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me? How long must I wrestle with my thoughts and day after day have sorrow in my heart?*

**Psalm 56:3-4** — *When I am afraid, I put my trust in you. In God, whose word I praise, in God I trust; I shall not be afraid. What can flesh do to me?*

**Philippians 4:6-7** — *Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God.*

The Christian parent who has begun to bring the news to God with the child has begun to teach the child the practice that will carry the child through the next hard news, and the next, and the next. The practice is not the practice of pretending. The practice is the practice of bringing the hard news to the God who can hold it.

### 4. Walk Slowly

The fourth piece of guidance is that the parent should walk slowly. The child does not need the parent to fix the news in one conversation. The child needs the parent to walk with the child through the news over many conversations. The Christian parent who has walked slowly with the child through the hard news has begun to teach the child that the hard news is not the last word.

**2 Corinthians 1:3-4** — *Praise be to the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of compassion and the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our troubles, so that we can comfort those in any trouble with the comfort we ourselves receive from God.*

The Christian parent who has been comforted by God in the hard news is the Christian parent who can comfort the child in the hard news. The comfort is not a one-time event. The comfort is a long walk. The Christian parent who has begun the long walk with the child has begun the long work of being the parent the Bible calls the parent to be.

### 5. Tell the Child That the Child Is Held

The fifth piece of guidance is that the parent should tell the child that the child is held. The hard news is real. The God who holds the child is also real. The two realities sit together. The child does not have to choose between the hard news and the God. The child can hold both. The Christian parent who has begun to teach the child to hold both has begun the long work of the gospel in the heart of the child.

**Psalm 139:7-12** — *Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence? If I go up to the heavens, you are there; if I make my bed in the depths, you are there.
