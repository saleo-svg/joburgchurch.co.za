## The Conversation South African Christians Rarely Have in Church

There is a conversation that almost never happens in South African churches. It is the conversation about what forgiveness actually requires in a country that carries what South Africa carries. The conversation is uncomfortable. The conversation is avoided. The conversation is the conversation the Bible has the most to say about.

What South Africa carries is not the same as what other countries carry. South Africa carries the weight of a system that was deliberately built on the dispossession of the majority of its people. South Africa carries the weight of a Truth and Reconciliation Commission that heard nearly seven thousand testimonies and was not able to hear all of them. South Africa carries the weight of families who know what was done to them and have never received an apology. South Africa carries the weight of families who know what was done by them and have not yet found the words to say it. South Africa carries the weight of the everyday — the security company on the corner, the estate wall, the job that was not given, the school that was not attended, the language that was lost.

The Christian in South Africa is not outside this weight. The Christian in South Africa is inside it. The Christian in South Africa is also called, by the same Bible that calls the Christian in any other country, to forgive.

This post is an attempt to have the conversation the Bible way. Not the prosperity gospel way. Not the legalism way. Not the quick-prayer-at-the-altar way. The Bible way. The way that takes the long view. The way that takes the weight seriously. The way that does not pretend the cost is small.

## What the Bible Actually Says About Forgiveness

The Bible has more to say about forgiveness than most Christians realise. Forgiveness is not a single teaching. Forgiveness is a set of teachings that hold together.

**Matthew 18:21-22** — *Then Peter came to Jesus and asked, 'Lord, how many times shall I forgive my brother or sister who sins against me? As many as seven times?' Jesus answered, 'I tell you, not seven times, but seventy-seven times.'*

**Ephesians 4:32** — *Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you.*

**Colossians 3:13** — *Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you.*

**Matthew 6:14-15** — *For if you forgive other people when they sin against you, your heavenly Father will also forgive you. But if you do not forgive others their sins, your Father will not forgive your sins.*

**Luke 23:34** — *Jesus said, 'Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.'*

**Mark 11:25** — *And when you stand praying, if you hold anything against anyone, forgive them, so that your Father in heaven may forgive you your sins.*

**Acts 7:60** — *Then he fell on his knees and cried out, 'Lord, do not hold this sin against them.'*

What you notice is that forgiveness in the Bible is not a one-time event. Forgiveness is a posture. Forgiveness is a discipline. Forgiveness is a way of being in the world. The Bible does not say the forgiveness is easy. The Bible does say the forgiveness is necessary. The Bible does say the forgiveness has been modelled by Jesus himself on the cross — *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing.*

The Bible does not promise that the forgiveness will be quick. The Bible does not promise that the forgiveness will be felt. The Bible does not promise that the wrong will be made right in this lifetime. The Bible promises that the Christian is called to the forgiveness. The Bible promises that the Christian is not alone in the forgiveness. The Bible promises that the God who calls the Christian to forgive is also the God who forgives the Christian.

## What Forgiveness Is Not

This part matters most. Several of the things that forgiveness is commonly taken to mean in some South African churches are not what the Bible teaches.

### Forgiveness Is Not Forgetting

The Bible does not say that forgiveness requires forgetting. The Bible says in Psalm 137 that the exiles in Babylon *remembered* Jerusalem, that they *wept* when they remembered Zion, that they hung their harps on the willows and could not sing. The Bible is not against remembering. The Bible is against the memory becoming the prison. Forgiveness is the act of holding the memory without letting the memory hold you.

For the South African Christian, this matters. The Christian is not asked to forget the dispossession. The Christian is not asked to forget the humiliation. The Christian is not asked to forget the relative who never came back from the farm. The Christian is asked to hold the memory in the hand that does not clench into a fist. The memory can stay. The memory is the witness. The clenching is what forgiveness loosens.

### Forgiveness Is Not Reconciliation

The Bible distinguishes between forgiveness and reconciliation. Forgiveness is a one-sided act. Forgiveness is the act of the person who has been wronged releasing the wrong from the centre of their own heart. Reconciliation is a two-sided act. Reconciliation requires the person who did the wrong to come, to acknowledge, to repent. Where there is no repentance, there is no reconciliation. Where there is no reconciliation, there can still be forgiveness. The Christian can forgive without being reconciled. The Christian can pray for the wrongdoer without sitting at the table with the wrongdoer.

This distinction matters enormously in South Africa. The Christian who has been wronged is not required to pretend the wrong did not happen in order to forgive. The Christian is not required to befriend the person who did the wrong in order to forgive. The Christian is required to release the wrong from the centre of the heart. The Christian is required to pray for the wrongdoer. The Christian is not required to do more than the Bible requires.

### Forgiveness Is Not the End of the Justice Conversation

The Bible is not against justice. The Bible is against the kind of forgiveness that pretends justice is not necessary. Micah 6:8 says — *He has shown you, O mortal, what is good. And what does the Lord require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God.* The verse pairs justice and mercy. The verse does not choose one over the other. The verse assumes both.

For the South African Christian, this matters. The Christian can forgive the person who did the wrong and still call for the justice. The Christian can release the wrong from the centre of the heart and still support the prosecution. The Christian can pray for the wrongdoer and still support the system that holds the wrongdoer accountable. Forgiveness is the personal act. Justice is the public act. The Bible requires both.

### Forgiveness Is Not a Single Moment

The Bible says *seventy-seven times*. The Bible does not say *once and then never again*. Forgiveness in the Bible is a practice. Forgiveness is the daily decision to release the wrong that came up again in the news, in the conversation, in the memory, in the dream. The forgiveness is not a one-time altar call. The forgiveness is the slow work of years. The forgiveness is the work the Holy Spirit does in the Christian who is willing.

## The Four Stories the Bible Study Has Heard

Over the years, our Wednesday Bible study in Sandton has heard four kinds of forgiveness stories. The four kinds are not the same. The four kinds each call for a different kind of response.

### Story 1 — The Personal Wrong

A friend betrayed you. A spouse was unfaithful. A relative stole from you. A business partner defrauded you. A colleague lied about you. The personal wrong is the most common story. The personal wrong is the one most often addressed by the Sunday sermon on forgiveness. The personal wrong is the one most amenable to the seven-times-seventy teaching. The personal wrong is the one that the Bible study has prayed through with several members over the years.

The practice for the personal wrong is the practice of the long obedience. The long obedience is the small decision to release the wrong each morning. The long obedience is the small decision to pray for the person who wronged you. The long obedience is the small decision to refuse the gossip that would rehearse the wrong. The long obedience is years. The long obedience is the Bible's normal Christian life.

### Story 2 — The Family Wrong

A parent favoured one child over the others. A parent abandoned the family. A parent was violent. A sibling took what was not theirs. An uncle abused. The family wrong is the next kind of story. The family wrong is harder because the family is still the family. The wrong is still being done at the dinner table on Sunday. The forgiveness has to be done with the family still in the room.

The practice for the family wrong is the practice of the boundaries. The Bible is not against boundaries. The Bible is against the abuse. The Christian who has been wronged in the family can forgive and still set the boundary. The Christian who has been wronged in the family can release the wrong from the centre of the heart and still say *this is not acceptable in my home*. Forgiveness is the heart. The boundary is the wall. The two are not the same. The two can both be present.

### Story 3 — The Communal Wrong

A community was dispossessed. A community was displaced. A community was excluded. A community was told their language was lesser. A community was told their worship was lesser. A community was told their way of being in the world was wrong. The communal wrong is the hardest kind of story. The communal wrong is the one that has been passed across generations. The communal wrong is the one that the individual Christian did not do but has inherited.

The Bible is not silent about the communal wrong. The Bible is full of the communal wrong. The Israelites in Egypt. The exiles in Babylon. The early Christians under Rome. The Bible assumes that the Christian lives inside a history. The Bible does not ask the Christian to pretend the history is not there. The Bible asks the Christian to do the slow work of reconciliation across the history. The slow work is generational. The slow work is the kind of work the Truth and Reconciliation Commission tried to do. The slow work is not finished.

### Story 4 — The Wrong the Christian Did

The Christian was the wrongdoer. The Christian participated in the system. The Christian benefitted from the dispossession. The Christian was silent when the Christian should have spoken. The Christian is the one who carries the guilt.

The Bible is clear about this story too. Luke 19:8 — *Zacchaeus stood up and said to the Lord, 'Look, Lord! Here and now I give half of my possessions to the poor, and if I have cheated anybody out of anything, I will pay back four times the amount.'* The Bible assumes the wrong can be confessed. The Bible assumes the wrong can be made right. The Bible assumes the Christian who has done the wrong can come to the person who was wronged and say the words.

The practice for the Christian who has done the wrong is the practice of the confession. The confession is named in James 5:16 — *Therefore confess your sins to each other and pray for each other so that you may be healed.* The confession is not the Sunday altar call. The confession is the specific, named, in-person confession to the specific person who was wronged. The confession is hard. The confession is the only path the Bible outlines for the wrongdoer.

## Six Practices That Have Helped the Bible Study Members With Forgiveness

### Practice 1 — The Daily Release

Each morning, before the day starts, the Christian who is carrying the wrong says a brief prayer releasing the wrong from the centre of the heart. The prayer does not pretend the wrong did not happen. The prayer asks God to hold the wrong. The prayer asks God to release the Christian from the grip of the wrong. The prayer is repeated for as many mornings as the wrong keeps coming back.

### Practice 2 — The Letter That Does Not Get Sent

Several members of our Bible study have written the letter to the person who wronged them. The letter has named every detail of the wrong. The letter has said every thing the Christian needed to say. The letter has not been sent. The letter has been folded and put in the drawer. The letter has been the way the Christian said the thing without reopening the door. The letter is not a substitute for forgiveness. The letter is a step on the way to forgiveness.

### Practice 3 — The Prayer for the Wrongdoer

The Christian who has been wronged begins to pray for the wrongdoer. Not for the wrongdoer's harm. For the wrongdoer's good. For the wrongdoer's healing. For the wrongdoer's repentance. The prayer is not a quick prayer. The prayer is the slow work of years. The prayer is the practice the Bible teaches in Matthew 5:44 — *Love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you.*

### Practice 4 — The Confession to a Safe Person

The Christian who is carrying the wrong tells the safe person. The safe person is the older Christian, the trusted friend, the pastor, the Bible study leader. The safe person does not minimise. The safe person does not gossip. The safe person prays. The safe person holds the Christian accountable to the slow work of the forgiveness. James 5:16 assumes this kind of friendship.

### Practice 5 — The Boundary Where Necessary

The Christian who has been wronged sets the boundary where the boundary is necessary. The boundary is not the unforgiveness. The boundary is the wall around the Christian while the forgiveness is being worked out. The boundary is the protection. The boundary is what allows the forgiveness to happen in safety.

### Practice 6 — The Generational Work

The Christian who is carrying a communal wrong begins the generational work. The generational work is the work of telling the children the truth about the family history. The generational work is the work of teaching the children the language and the songs and the prayers of the community. The generational work is the work of supporting the organisations that are doing the slow work of justice in the country. The generational work is not the unforgiveness. The generational work is the opposite of the unforgiveness. The generational work is the love that refuses to let the wrong be the end of the story.

## A Prayer for the South African Christian and Forgiveness

If you are carrying a personal wrong, a family wrong, a communal wrong, or the wrong you have done — here is a prayer.

> *God of the long forgiveness,*
>
> *We bring you the wrong that has been done to us. We bring you the family that did the wrong. We bring you the country that did the wrong. We bring you the system that did the wrong. We bring you the silence that did the wrong.*
>
> *We do not pretend the wrong is small. We name the wrong as wrong. We hold the wrong up to you. We ask you to hold the wrong with us.*
>
> *We confess that we have clenched the wrong into a fist. We confess that we have rehearsed the wrong in our minds. We confess that we have let the wrong become the centre of our hearts. We confess that we have used the wrong as a reason not to love.*
>
> *We ask you to loosen the fist. We ask you to release the rehearsal. We ask you to make room in the centre of the heart for something other than the wrong. We ask you for the grace to pray for the wrongdoer. We ask you for the grace to set the boundary where the boundary is necessary. We ask you for the grace to do the generational work.*
>
> *We pray for South Africa. We pray for the families who have not been able to forgive. We pray for the families who have not been able to ask for forgiveness. We pray for the churches that have not yet had this conversation. We pray for the Bible studies that are just beginning to have it.*
>
> *In the name of the one who said from the cross, Father, forgive them, and who himself forgave the one who denied him three times.*
> *Amen.*
