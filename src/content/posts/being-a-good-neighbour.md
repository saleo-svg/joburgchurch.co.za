# The Neighbour You Don't Know: A South African Christian's Guide to Neighbourliness in a World of Electric Fences

## The Scene

The woman in Linksfield noticed her neighbour's garden bore was overflowing. The neighbour was an elderly man who she had seen only once, when they waved at each other across the wall. She thought about asking if he was alright. She did not. Two weeks later she heard he had been in hospital alone.

**Mark 10:45** — *"For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."*

The South African Christian in the situations described above is not alone. The Bible has been speaking to these situations for two thousand years. The Wednesday Bible study in Parkmore has been hearing these passages read, discussed, and applied for ten years. What follows is a distillation of what the Bible says and what we have learned from the journey.


## What Luke 10 Actually Says

Luke 10:29 asks: 'Who is my neighbour?' This question — asked to avoid the harder command to love — has been answered by Jesus with the parable of the Good Samaritan. The neighbour is not the person who lives next door to you. The neighbour is the person who is in need, regardless of who they are. The question is not 'who do I live near?' The question is 'who is in need near me?'

## Why Johannesburg Neighbours Don't Talk

The reasons Johannesburg neighbours do not talk are practical: the walls, the gates, the fear of crime, the assumption that the neighbour is too busy or too different. These reasons are all understandable. They are also, cumulatively, the reason that Johannesburg communities are atomised and crime thrives in the gaps.

## The Simple First Step

The simplest first step is also the hardest: introduce yourself. A wave is not an introduction. 'Hello, I am your neighbour. We have been living next to each other for a while. I wanted to say hello.' This takes thirty seconds and removes the awkwardness for the next encounter.

## Practical Neighbourliness in Johannesburg

First: introduce yourself. Second: share your contact number for emergencies. Third: know their schedule — when they travel, when they are usually home. Fourth: offer to collect their post when they are away. Fifth: attend the neighbourhood WhatsApp group — it is not community, but it is the beginning of one.

## A Prayer for the Neighbour You Don't Know

Father, we bring the neighbour we have never spoken to. We bring the fence that separates us. We bring the fear that keeps us apart. We ask for the courage to introduce ourselves. We ask for the small acts of neighbourliness that build community. We ask for the safety that comes from knowing each other, not from knowing less of each other. Make us neighbours, Lord, not just people who live near each other. Amen.


## A Closing Prayer

Father, we bring this struggle to you. We ask for your wisdom, your strength, and your presence. We ask for the community to hold us. We ask for the hope that does not disappoint. Amen.
